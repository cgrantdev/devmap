## What the categories actually are

While FDA builds the **503A Bulks List** through evaluation and rulemaking, it has used interim categories for nominated bulk drug substances:

- **Category 1** is the interim bucket associated with evaluation and possible enforcement discretion. It is not FDA drug approval, and it is not, by itself, a license to compound.
- **Category 2** flags significant safety-risk concerns and a do-not-compound posture under that interim framing.
- **Withdrawn nominations** are a separate posture. A nominator withdrawing a nomination, or a substance coming off Category 2, does not place it in Category 1 and does not add it to the Bulks List.

Under section 503A, a bulk drug substance generally may be used in compounding only if it has an applicable USP/NF monograph, is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or appears on the 503A Bulks List established by FDA rulemaking. Interim labels are not a shortcut around that statute.

## What early-2026 headlines got wrong

Early-2026 coverage described federal actions involving peptide bulk substances and FDA’s interim compounding categories. Many write-ups treated a Category 2 change as if it were Category 1 status and as if pharmacies could compound the named peptides again. Those are different steps.

- Taking a substance **off Category 2** is not the same as moving it **into Category 1**.
- It is not the same as adding it to the **503A Bulks List**.
- It does not mean a compounding pharmacy has new permission to produce that peptide.

**BPC-157** is the clearest illustration. FDA’s compounding safety-risk materials discuss BPC-157 in a **withdrawn-nomination / off Category 2** context (immunogenicity, impurity and API characterization, and limited human safety information for proposed routes). That is not Category 1 status. **TB-500** (thymosin beta-4 fragment LKKTETQ) is discussed in the same safety-risk materials. Neither is an FDA-approved drug.

Other peptide names that circulated in those headlines — including thymosin alpha-1, AOD-9604, and GHK-Cu — need the same three-way check against current FDA pages. A secondary list of names is not a final bulks-list rule, and this article does not assign each of them a category.

## July 2026 PCAC votes are advisory

On **23–24 July 2026**, FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee reviewed nominated peptide bulk substances for possible **503A Bulks List** inclusion. Contemporaneous reporting describes committee votes that **recommended** inclusion for several peptides, including BPC-157 and TB-500.

A PCAC recommendation is **advisory and non-binding**. It is not a final FDA rule, not drug approval, and not by itself permission to compound. Addition to the Bulks List generally requires FDA action, typically notice-and-comment rulemaking. Until a substance actually qualifies under the statute, treat claims that compounding is now allowed with skepticism and check the primary FDA pages.

## Research-use labeling is not a third approval lane

“Research use only” or “not for human consumption” is a seller’s claimed intended-use framing. It does not create FDA approval, does not place a substance on a bulks list, and does not erase how FDA evaluates intended use from labeling and advertising (see [21 CFR 201.128](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-D/section-201.128)).

## Bottom line

The 2026 category headlines are easy to misread as permission. They are not. Category 2 removal is not Category 1, not placement on the 503A Bulks List, and not compounding permission. A PCAC vote is not a final rule. None of this is FDA approval for human therapeutic use.
