AOD-9604 and tesamorelin often sit side by side in "fat loss" catalog sections. They are different kinds of molecule with very different evidence and regulatory records. AOD-9604 is a short synthetic fragment modeled on the tail end of human growth hormone (hGH). Tesamorelin is a modified version of growth hormone–releasing hormone (GHRH), and specific Egrifta products are FDA-approved for one narrow indication.

A note on "176–191": in mature hGH, residues 176–191 read FLRIVQCRSVEGSCGF. AOD-9604 has a tyrosine in place of that first phenylalanine, and its developers describe it as Tyr-hGH(177–191). Catalogs use the names loosely, so a label should state the full sequence. For the full-length hormone, see [HGH 191AA](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/hgh-191aa).

## How they are thought to work

**Tesamorelin (label-described mechanism).** According to the Egrifta labels, tesamorelin "binds and stimulates human GRF receptors with similar potency as the endogenous GRF." GHRH acts on the pituitary to release the body's own growth hormone. The label states that tesamorelin "stimulates growth hormone secretion, and subsequently increases IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 levels." Working upstream of GH means raised GH and IGF-1, which is why the label tells prescribers to monitor IGF-1 and evaluate glucose.

**AOD-9604 (proposed mechanism).** Monash University researchers proposed that the lipolytic (fat-releasing) activity of hGH sits partly in its C-terminal region. In obese mice, AOD-9604 reduced body-weight gain and raised markers of fat breakdown and fat oxidation. Unlike hGH, it did not cause high blood sugar, and in cell assays it did not compete for the hGH receptor or drive cell proliferation (Heffernan et al., 2001, *Int J Obes Relat Metab Disord*). A sponsor-funded summary of human trials (Stier et al., 2013) reported no effect on serum IGF-1. "Lipolysis without IGF-1 effects" is therefore a hypothesis built on animal and cell work plus one industry-authored safety summary. It is not an established mechanism in people, and the sources reviewed here identify no confirmed receptor target.

## Evidence side by side

| Evidence layer | AOD-9604 | Tesamorelin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell and animal | Obese mouse and rat studies (lipolysis, fat oxidation); a rabbit osteoarthritis model | GHRH pharmacology; supports the clinical program |
| Human RCTs | Six randomized, placebo-controlled trials (about 900 adults) by the developer, mostly obese adults, mostly oral | Two multicenter phase 3 RCTs (412 and 404 patients), each with an extension phase |
| Population studied | Mostly obese, otherwise healthy adults | Adults with HIV on antiretroviral therapy with excess abdominal fat |
| Primary endpoint | Body weight (in the phase 2b efficacy trials) | Change in visceral adipose tissue (VAT) on CT |
| Published outcome | Larger 24-week phase 2b trial did not reach statistical significance on weight; obesity program terminated (2007) | VAT fell versus placebo; IGF-1 rose; VAT returned after stopping |
| Status | No approved drug; no ClinicalTrials.gov record found | FDA-approved since 2010 for one indication |

**Tesamorelin human data.** In the first phase 3 trial (Falutz et al., *NEJM* 2007; NCT00123253), 412 adults with HIV and abdominal fat buildup were randomized to tesamorelin or placebo for 26 weeks. Visceral fat decreased in the tesamorelin group and increased with placebo. Lipid measures improved and IGF-1 rose. Adverse events did not differ significantly overall, but more tesamorelin patients withdrew because of one. A second phase 3 trial of 404 patients (Falutz et al., *J Acquir Immune Defic Syndr* 2010) also found reduced visceral fat versus placebo. A pooled analysis of 806 patients (Falutz et al., *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2010) found no significant change in abdominal subcutaneous fat, and in extension phases VAT reaccumulated after stopping (Falutz et al., *AIDS* 2008).

**AOD-9604 human data.** The main published human source is Stier, Vos, and Kenley (2013). It summarizes six randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials run by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals in about 900 adults. Two of its three authors list Metabolic Pharmaceuticals as their affiliation, its declared interests include a shareholding in Metabolic's parent company (Calzada) and consultancies to Metabolic, and Metabolic funded all six trials. The paper reports safety outcomes (IGF-1, glucose tolerance, antibodies, adverse events) but not efficacy results. Metabolic's 21 February 2007 ASX announcement said weight loss compared with placebo in its phase 2b OPTIONS trial (536 subjects enrolled, 502 randomized) "was too low to reach statistical significance." It added that "[d]evelopment of the drug for this condition is terminated." A 2026 review in *Frontiers in Endocrinology* (Dominikowski et al.) cites the same outcome and calls AOD-9604's human evidence "limited and largely short-term." Joint and cartilage claims rest mainly on animal work, such as a rabbit osteoarthritis study (Kwon and Park, 2015). The developer's two injected trials were single-infusion intravenous safety studies, and Peptidemap found no published human trial of repeated subcutaneous AOD-9604.

## Why "fat loss" is a misleading shared label

Tesamorelin's label is specific. Egrifta WR "is indicated for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy." The same label says it "is not indicated for weight loss management as it has a weight neutral effect," and that "long-term cardiovascular safety … has not been established." Its trials measured visceral fat in one population, not general weight loss.

AOD-9604 was developed as an oral obesity drug, and its larger efficacy trial did not meet its endpoint. Grouping them under "fat loss" mixes an approved drug with a narrow label and an unapproved fragment whose obesity program ended, two very different evidence tiers.

## Regulatory and legal status (checked 12 October 2026)

**Tesamorelin approval.** FDA approved Egrifta (NDA 022505) on 10 November 2010 "for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy." Egrifta SV, a single-vial formulation, followed. The application was deemed a biologics license application (BLA 022505) on 23 March 2020 under the BPCI Act transition, and FDA approved the Egrifta WR formulation on 25 March 2025 as supplement BLA 022505/S-020. Approval covers those finished products and that indication only. A research vial labeled "tesamorelin" is not Egrifta and is not an FDA-approved drug. Because tesamorelin is regulated as a licensed biological product, the 503A and 503B compounding exemptions do not apply to it.

**AOD-9604 approval and GRAS.** AOD-9604 is not an approved drug in the U.S. Its developer, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals, announced in June 2012 what its CEO called a "self-affirmed conditional GRAS status" for AOD-9604 as a food ingredient, based on an expert panel the company contracted. Stier et al. (2013) repeat that "an appropriately qualified GRAS panel of experts" found it "Generally Recognized As Safe" for intended uses in foods. That is a self-affirmed determination reported by the developer, not an FDA finding. Peptidemap searched FDA's GRAS Notice Inventory on 12 October 2026 and found no notice for AOD-9604, and a food-ingredient determination would not cover an injectable product anyway.

**Compounding (AOD-9604).** FDA's page on bulk substances that may present significant safety risks (content current as of 22 April 2026) lists AOD-9604 among nominations "previously in category 2" that "were withdrawn by the nominators." FDA kept its concern text. It warns of possible "significant risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration" and notes that FDA "has identified no, or only limited, safety-related information." It also says FDA "has also identified serious adverse events that may be associated with AOD-9604, though causality is not clear." FDA's 503A categories list (updated 14 May 2026) places AOD-9604 in none of Categories 1, 2, or 3. Being off Category 2 is not permission to compound. AOD-9604 is not on the agenda of the July 2026 Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meeting or the one announced for before the end of February 2027. For how approval, 503A compounding, and RUO labels differ, see [Are research peptides legal? FDA, RUO & compounding explained](/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding) and [FDA peptide reclassification 2026](/blog/fda-peptide-reclassification-2026-what-researchers-need-to-know).

**WADA (2026 and 2027 lists).** Both are prohibited at all times. AOD-9604 is named under S2.2.3 ("growth hormone fragments, e.g. AOD-9604 and hGH 176-191"). Tesamorelin is named under S2.2.4 as a GHRH analog. Both entries are unchanged on the 2027 List, effective 1 January 2027.

## What a CoA can and can't tell you

A certificate of analysis can show whether the submitted sample's mass matches the label (about 1815.1 g/mol for AOD-9604; about 5135.9 Da free base for tesamorelin) and what HPLC purity it had under that lab's method. For AOD-9604, a useful CoA would also confirm the sequence and disulfide-bonded form, since "fragment 176–191" listings vary, and state free base or salt.

A CoA cannot show that other lots match, that a vial is sterile or endotoxin-free unless tested, or that the compound is safe, effective, or legal for any use. A tesamorelin CoA does not make a research vial equivalent to Egrifta. ## FAQ

### Are AOD-9604 and tesamorelin the same kind of peptide?

No. AOD-9604 is a 16-amino-acid fragment modeled on the C-terminal end of human growth hormone. Tesamorelin is a modified 44-amino-acid GHRH analog that signals the pituitary to release growth hormone.

### Is either FDA-approved?

Tesamorelin is approved only as specific Egrifta products for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adults with lipodystrophy. AOD-9604 is not an approved drug. Research vials of either are not FDA-approved products.

### Is AOD-9604 a growth hormone peptide?

It is derived from part of the growth hormone sequence, but it is not growth hormone and is not thought to act through the GH receptor. In cell assays it did not compete for the hGH receptor, and WADA classes it as a growth hormone fragment.

### How does the human evidence differ?

Tesamorelin has two published phase 3 trials in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat, which led to FDA approval. AOD-9604 has developer-run trials in obese adults, and its larger weight-loss trial did not reach statistical significance.

### Are they banned in sport?

Yes. Both are named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List and remain on the 2027 List: AOD-9604 as a growth hormone fragment and tesamorelin as a GHRH analog. Both are prohibited at all times.
