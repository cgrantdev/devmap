Orforglipron and oral semaglutide are both once-daily GLP-1 receptor agonist pills, but they are built differently. Orforglipron, from Eli Lilly, is a non-peptide small molecule. Oral semaglutide, from Novo Nordisk, is a peptide that needs an absorption enhancer to work as a tablet. ACHIEVE-3, published in *The Lancet* in 2026, is the first phase 3 trial to compare the two head to head, in adults with type 2 diabetes.

This article covers what each drug is, how ACHIEVE-3 was designed, what it reported for HbA1c, weight and tolerability, and where each drug stands with the FDA. Every efficacy figure is labelled with its estimand. Lilly's press releases and the Lancet paper lead with different estimands, so the figures are shown side by side with their source.

Peptidemap is an RUO / grey-area peptide comparison publisher, not a clinic, pharmacy or supplier. This article is educational. It is not medical advice, gives no dosing, and does not say which drug is better.

## What each is

| | Orforglipron | Oral semaglutide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developer | Eli Lilly (discovered by Chugai) | Novo Nordisk |
| Molecule type | Non-peptide small molecule | Peptide (modified GLP-1 analogue) |
| Target | GLP-1 receptor | GLP-1 receptor |
| Tablet design | No absorption enhancer needed | Co-formulated with the absorption enhancer SNAC (salcaprozate sodium) |
| Label instructions on food | With or without food | Empty stomach with a small amount of plain water, then a wait before food, drink or other oral medicines |
| US brand (FDA) | Foundayo (weight management, approved 1 April 2026) | Rybelsus (type 2 diabetes, approved 20 September 2019) |

**Orforglipron.** Lilly's September 2025 release describes orforglipron as a once-daily "small molecule (non-peptide) oral glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist that can be taken any time of the day without restrictions on food and water intake." The Lancet paper calls it a "novel non-peptide" GLP-1 receptor agonist. The Foundayo label says it "binds to and activates the human GLP-1 receptor," and reports no clinically relevant food effect on exposure. Because it is not a peptide, it is made by chemical synthesis rather than as a modified hormone.

**Oral semaglutide.** Semaglutide is a modified GLP-1 peptide. Per the Rybelsus label, it is modified to resist breakdown by the DPP-4 enzyme and to bind albumin, which gives it a long half-life of about one week. Peptides are normally broken down in the gut, so the tablets are "co-formulated with SNAC which facilitates the absorption of semaglutide after oral administration." The label says absorption occurs mainly in the stomach, which is why it carries strict fasting and water instructions.

Both drugs act on the same receptor. The differences lie in molecule type, how each gets absorbed, and the administration rules that follow from that.

Peptidemap's compound pages are [Orforglipron](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/orforglipron) and [Semaglutide](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/semaglutide). There is no separate oral-semaglutide entry.

## How ACHIEVE-3 compared them

ACHIEVE-3 (ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT06045221](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06045221); Rosenstock et al., *Lancet* 2026) design:

- Design: phase 3, randomized 1:1:1:1, open-label, active-controlled, multicenter and multinational. Investigators and participants knew which drug was given.
- Population: 1,698 adults with type 2 diabetes not adequately controlled on metformin, with an HbA1c of 7.0% to 10.5% and a BMI of at least 25. Participants came from 131 sites in Argentina, China, Japan, Mexico and the USA. Mean baseline HbA1c was 8.3% and mean baseline weight 97.0 kg.
- Arms: a lower-dose and a higher-dose orforglipron arm (424 and 423 participants) and two oral semaglutide arms at the two Rybelsus maintenance doses for type 2 diabetes (426 and 425). Each arm escalated stepwise to its assigned maintenance dose. Below, the arms are called "lower" and "higher" within each drug.
- Comparator: oral semaglutide was tested only at the two Rybelsus maintenance doses for type 2 diabetes, the higher of which is Rybelsus's highest dose. ACHIEVE-3 did not test the higher-strength oral semaglutide approved as Wegovy tablets for weight management. Lilly's release says both drugs were given "according to approved label instructions."
- Duration: 52 weeks of treatment after a lead-in of up to 4 weeks.
- Primary objective: non-inferiority on HbA1c change at week 52, lower orforglipron vs lower semaglutide and higher orforglipron vs higher semaglutide, with a 0.3% margin. Superiority was tested in a prespecified hierarchy once non-inferiority was met.

Lilly announced topline results on 17 September 2025. The paper and Lilly's detailed results followed on 26 February 2026.

### Two estimands, two sources

- The **treatment-regimen estimand** counts all randomized participants regardless of whether they stopped the drug or added other diabetes medicines. The Lancet paper names this its primary estimand.
- The **efficacy estimand** estimates the effect had everyone stayed on the assigned drug without adding other diabetes medicines. Lilly's releases lead with this one, and the Lancet paper calls it supportive. The results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov use on-treatment data that match this definition.

So the same arm can show "−1.9%" in one source and "−2.2%" in another. Neither figure is wrong: they answer different questions.

According to the Lancet authors, most gastrointestinal events were mild to moderate. Gastrointestinal events, discontinuations due to adverse events and mean pulse-rate increases were all higher with orforglipron than with oral semaglutide. Discontinuation due to adverse events was roughly twice as high in each orforglipron arm as in the matching semaglutide arm. Lilly's September 2025 release adds that "the study was not powered to compare the safety and tolerability" of the two drugs and that "no hepatic safety signal was observed for orforglipron." The ClinicalTrials.gov percentages here were calculated from the posted counts.

## Regulatory status

**Oral semaglutide (Rybelsus, NDA 213051): FDA-approved.**

- 20 September 2019: approved as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes. This was the first approval on Drugs@FDA.
- 9 December 2024: a second tablet formulation, R2, was approved with its own dosage and switching instructions. On 30 January 2026, FDA approved a supplement under the same NDA that changed the name of formulation R2 to Ozempic tablets. The current label says Rybelsus and Ozempic tablets are not substitutable on a mg-to-mg basis.
- 17 October 2025: a new indication was approved to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with type 2 diabetes at high risk of those events.
- Separately, 22 December 2025: Wegovy tablets (oral semaglutide, NDA 218316) were approved for use in combination with a reduced calorie diet and increased physical activity to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, or non-fatal stroke) in adults with established cardiovascular disease and either obesity or overweight, and to reduce excess body weight and maintain weight reduction long term in adults with obesity or overweight in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbid condition. That is a different product and was not the ACHIEVE-3 comparator.

**Orforglipron (Foundayo, NDA 220934): FDA-approved for weight management only.**

- 20 January 2026: FDA received the NDA.
- 1 April 2026: FDA approved Foundayo "to reduce excess body weight and maintain weight reduction long term" in adults with obesity, or with overweight and at least one weight-related comorbid condition, alongside diet and physical activity. FDA said it was the first new molecular entity approved under its Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot program.
- 5 August 2026: Lilly's second-quarter report listed "the submission for orforglipron for type 2 diabetes in the U.S." The FDA has not published a decision date in the sources reviewed.
- As of 19 October 2026, Drugs@FDA shows no type 2 diabetes approval for orforglipron. Its listed supplement since approval (4 August 2026) is a Medication Guide labelling change. Type 2 diabetes is therefore not an FDA-approved use of orforglipron yet.

Outside the US: on 15 October 2026 the EMA's Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) adopted a positive opinion, recommending the granting of a marketing authorisation for the medicinal product Foundayo, intended for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and for weight management. An opinion is not a marketing authorisation; the European Commission decision is pending.

## What the trial doesn't tell us

- **Not a test of higher-strength oral semaglutide.** Only the two Rybelsus diabetes maintenance doses were comparators. The results say nothing about the higher-strength oral semaglutide approved for weight management.
- **Open-label, with different administration rules.** Everyone knew which drug they took. Semaglutide was taken under its fasting instructions, and orforglipron without food restrictions. Either factor could affect adherence and reporting.
- **Type 2 diabetes only.** ACHIEVE-3 enrolled adults on metformin with type 2 diabetes. It does not describe people without diabetes, and its weight results are secondary endpoints in a glycemic trial.
- **52 weeks.** No longer-term or cardiovascular outcomes come from this trial.
- **Safety comparison not powered.** Lilly states that the study was not powered to compare safety and tolerability, so the adverse-event differences are descriptive.
- **Dose labels changed.** Lilly's later releases relabel the orforglipron arms using the strengths of the approved Foundayo tablets. The same arms therefore appear under different numbers in different sources. The trial arm, not the number printed, is what matters.
- **Cross-trial comparisons are not valid.** Figures from other orforglipron or semaglutide trials, such as obesity trials with different populations and durations, should not be lined up against ACHIEVE-3. Only within-trial comparisons are reported here.
- **Not about individual response.** Trial averages do not predict one person's response. Treatment choices belong with a licensed clinician.

## FAQ

### What did ACHIEVE-3 compare?

ACHIEVE-3 was a 52-week, open-label, randomized phase 3 trial in 1,698 adults with type 2 diabetes not controlled on metformin. It compared two once-daily orforglipron dose arms with two once-daily oral semaglutide dose arms. Oral semaglutide was tested only at the two Rybelsus maintenance doses for type 2 diabetes.

### Was orforglipron non-inferior or superior on HbA1c?

Both. The Lancet paper reports that the primary non-inferiority objective was met and that both orforglipron arms were superior to both oral semaglutide arms on HbA1c change at week 52. Under the treatment-regimen estimand, HbA1c fell by 1.71% and 1.91% with orforglipron vs 1.23% and 1.47% with oral semaglutide.

### Why do Lilly's numbers differ from the Lancet abstract?

They use different estimands. The Lancet paper's primary estimand is the treatment-regimen estimand, which counts all randomized participants regardless of stopping treatment. Lilly's headline figures use the efficacy estimand, which estimates the effect had everyone stayed on treatment. For example, the higher orforglipron arm's HbA1c change is 1.91% under the treatment-regimen estimand and 2.2% under the efficacy estimand.

### How much weight change was reported?

Under the efficacy estimand, mean weight change was 6.7% and 9.2% with orforglipron vs 3.7% and 5.3% with oral semaglutide. Under the treatment-regimen estimand, company-reported by Lilly, the figures were 6.1% and 8.2% vs 3.9% and 5.3%. Baseline weight averaged 97.0 kg.

### Did orforglipron have more side effects?

Yes, in this trial. Gastrointestinal adverse events were reported in 59% and 58% of the orforglipron arms vs 37% and 45% of the oral semaglutide arms. Discontinuation due to adverse events was 8.7% and 9.7% vs 4.5% and 4.9%, roughly twice as high. Mean pulse rate rose more with orforglipron.

### How do the two drugs differ chemically?

Orforglipron is a non-peptide small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist taken once daily with or without food. Oral semaglutide is a peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist formulated with the absorption enhancer SNAC, and its label requires it to be taken on an empty stomach with a little water and a wait before eating.

### Are both FDA-approved?

Rybelsus (oral semaglutide) was approved on 20 September 2019 for type 2 diabetes. Orforglipron was approved as Foundayo on 1 April 2026 for weight management only. Lilly reported submitting it to the FDA for type 2 diabetes in the second quarter of 2026, and as of 19 October 2026 no FDA diabetes approval was found. Research-labelled orforglipron sold online is not Foundayo and is not FDA-approved.
