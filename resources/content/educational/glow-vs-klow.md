GLOW and KLOW are marketplace names for multi-peptide research blends. They are not single molecules, and they are not approved medicines. Readers often treat KLOW as “more GLOW,” or treat a vial of GHK-Cu alone, or of KPV alone, as the same product. Those are different listings. On Peptidemap’s catalog, GLOW is the three-part label GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500. KLOW is that same named set plus a fourth component, KPV (Lys-Pro-Val). The added piece is KPV, not a higher GHK-Cu dose. This page is a contents comparison, and a limit on blend evidence. It is not a shopping guide, not a beginner primer, and not medical advice.

Vendor and price listings stay on their own pages: [GLOW](https://peptidemap.com/compare/glow) and [KLOW — GHK-Cu/BPC-157/TB-500/KPV](https://peptidemap.com/compare/klow-blend-ghk-cu-bpc-157-tb-500-kpv). A head-to-head price URL, `/compare/glow-vs-klow`, returned 404 when checked on 5 October 2026. This article does not copy vendor tables, coupon codes, or prices. Listings on those compare pages are framed as research use only (RUO).

## What each catalog label names

The component lists below are the aliases on Peptidemap’s live compare records, reviewed 5 October 2026.

The GLOW page uses the alias **GLOW — GHK-Cu/BPC-157/TB-500**. Three named components:

1. **GHK-Cu.** The copper complex of the tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine. PubChem lists the free tripeptide as glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine (CID 73587) and the copper complex as prezatide copper, also called GHK copper and copper tripeptide-1 (CID 71587328).
2. **BPC-157.** A synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide. PubChem lists it as Bpc-157 (CID 9941957).
3. **TB-500.** A marketplace name, not a second copy of GHK-Cu or of BPC-157. The identity caveat is below.

The KLOW page uses the alias **KLOW — GHK-Cu/BPC-157/TB-500/KPV**. The same three names, plus:

4. **KPV.** The tripeptide Lys-Pro-Val, described as the C-terminal fragment of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH residues 11–13). Wikipedia and PubChem list CAS **67727-97-3** and PubChem CID 125672 (formula C16H30N4O4 on that record).

KLOW is GLOW plus KPV. It is not GLOW with extra GHK-Cu, and it is not a higher copper-peptide strength under a new name.

## What KPV adds, and what it does not

KPV adds a fourth named molecule. On the chemistry, it is lysine–proline–valine. It is not a copper peptide, and it is not a fragment of GHK. Adding that name to a blend label does not mean the vial contains more GHK-Cu.

Any paper about KPV is a paper about that tripeptide, or about alpha-MSH-related fragments, unless the methods say otherwise. It is not a trial of a product named KLOW. The same split applies to the other names. Work on GHK-Cu is work on GHK-Cu. Work on BPC-157 is work on BPC-157. Printing several of those names on one research label does not merge their literatures into one blend result.

A PubMed check on 5 October 2026 did not find the commercial blend names as indexed phrases. NCBI reported that the exact phrases “GLOW blend,” “KLOW blend,” and “GHK-Cu/BPC-157/TB-500” were not found. A separate title search for “KLOW” and peptide returned no records. That is a statement about indexed product-name literature. It is not a safety conclusion, and it is not a claim that component peptides have never been studied on their own.

## A single peptide is not the blend

GHK-Cu alone is not GLOW, and it is not KLOW. KPV alone is not KLOW. BPC-157 alone is not either blend. TB-500 alone is not either blend.

A blend listing names more than one component. A single-peptide listing names one. Sharing one ingredient does not make the materials the same. A certificate of analysis that reports only one sequence is not the four-name KLOW entry, even if a shop title borrows the word.

Vial size strings on the compare pages differ across listings. Peptidemap does not publish one official ratio for GLOW or for KLOW, and this guide does not treat any milligram split as the composition. If a seller states a fill weight, it belongs on that lot’s certificate of analysis.

## The TB-500 name is not full-length thymosin beta-4

Research catalogs often use TB-500 for the acetylated fragment Ac-LKKTETQ (Ac-Leu-Lys-Lys-Thr-Glu-Thr-Gln). PubChem’s TB-500 record is that sequence: CID 62707662, CAS 885340-08-9. A 2012 *Drug Testing and Analysis* paper reported the N-terminal acetylated 17–23 fragment of thymosin beta-4 in material identified as TB-500 (Esposito et al., doi:10.1002/dta.1402).

Full-length thymosin beta-4 is a different substance. PubChem lists it under a separate record (CID 45382195). Studies of the full-length protein are not automatically studies of the short acetylated fragment. Neither kind of study is a study of a vial labeled GLOW or KLOW. Sellers sometimes use these names loosely. The certificate of analysis is what shows which sequence a lot was tested as. This page does not give doses or reconstitution steps.

## Where blend evidence stops

There is no published human trial of the named commercial blends “GLOW” or “KLOW” as products. Do not read a component paper as if the blend had been the test article. A cell study or an animal study of one peptide does not say what a three-part or four-part research mixture does in people.

This page does not rank GLOW against KLOW for healing, skin, gut, or recovery, and it does not pick a winner. It does not convert cosmetic research on copper tripeptide, or separate preclinical work on BPC-157 or on KPV, into a blend effect. Those are different materials.

A Peptidemap listing is a research-catalog record, not an approved drug label and not a clinical protocol.

## Bottom line

On Peptidemap’s labels, KLOW is the GLOW set — GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500 — plus KPV. KPV is Lys-Pro-Val, CAS 67727-97-3, not extra GHK-Cu. GHK-Cu alone is not the blend. KPV alone is not the blend. No human trial of those commercial product names was identified, and a paper on one component stays a paper on that component.
