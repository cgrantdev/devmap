Retatrutide is an investigational once-weekly injection that acts on GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. It is not approved anywhere. This article collects the adverse events reported in the primary trial publications, the ClinicalTrials.gov results that have been posted, and, where no full paper was available, figures Lilly has released. Each number is labelled with where it came from. Nothing here is a dosing guide, a safety verdict or medical advice.

## How to read the numbers on this page

The trials tested several dose arms. This article names them by position only: the **lowest dose arm**, the **middle dose arm** and the **highest dose arm**, plus placebo. The two phase 2 trials had more arms than that, including pairs of arms that reached the same dose from a lower or a higher starting dose. For those trials the tables show the lowest dose arm, the highest dose arm, and the range across every retatrutide arm. These positions are relative to each trial's own arms: the lowest, middle or highest dose arm in one trial is not necessarily the same dose as the arm in the same position in another trial.

Three kinds of source appear below:

- **Paper**: the peer-reviewed publication. For the two phase 2 trials, the full text was read. For TRIUMPH-2, the rows labelled Paper are those in the published paper. TRIUMPH-1 figures that appear in the NEJM paper are labelled as reported in the paper and company-reported by Lilly.
- **Registry**: results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov. These use the trial's own adverse-event terms and time frames, which can differ from the paper's.
- **Company-reported**: figures from Lilly press releases or Lilly Medical Information pages. These have not been peer reviewed, and they are labelled as company-reported every time they appear.

Percentages are the share of participants with at least one event, unless stated otherwise. Phase 2 and TRIUMPH-2 paper figures use the safety population, meaning participants who received at least one dose. Lilly's releases do not define their analysis set. These are safety counts, not efficacy results, so the efficacy and treatment-regimen estimands used for weight outcomes do not apply to them. Rates from different trials are not directly comparable. The populations, durations, escalation schemes and event definitions differ. The trial details sit on the [retatrutide encyclopedia entry](/encyclopedia/retatrutide). Mechanism and half-life are shown side by side with tirzepatide on the [retatrutide vs tirzepatide comparison page](/compare/retatrutide-vs-tirzepatide), which does not report trial results.

## Gastrointestinal events

Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse events in every trial covered here.

### Phase 2 obesity trial (paper)

The paper says gastrointestinal events "occurred primarily during dose escalation, were predominantly mild to moderate in severity, were more frequent in higher-dose groups, were partially mitigated by the use of a lower starting dose", and were the most common adverse events leading to discontinuation.

| Event | Placebo (n=70) | Lowest dose arm (n=69) | Highest dose arm (n=62) | Range across the six retatrutide arms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nausea | 11% | 14% | 45% | 14% to 60% |
| Diarrhoea | 11% | 9% | 15% | 9% to 20% |
| Vomiting | 1% | 3% | 19% | 3% to 26% |
| Constipation | 3% | 7% | 16% | 6% to 16% |
| Decreased appetite | 9% | 13% | 29% | 11% to 31% |
| Severe gastrointestinal event | 0 | 0 | 6% | 0 to 6% |

The highest nausea and vomiting rates (60% and 26%) were in an intermediate-dose arm that started at the higher starting dose. The arm reaching the same dose from the lower starting dose had 17% and 6%.

### Phase 2 type 2 diabetes trial (paper)

Gastrointestinal adverse events were reported in 67 of 190 retatrutide participants (35%). The rate ranged from 13% in the lowest dose arm to 50% in an intermediate-dose arm with fast escalation. It was 13% with placebo and 35% with the trial's active comparator, dulaglutide. The paper reports these events were mostly mild to moderate and more frequent with the higher starting dose.

## Discontinuation due to adverse events

Definitions differ. The phase 2 papers report stopping study drug because of an adverse event. The TRIUMPH-1 paper reports discontinuation of retatrutide or placebo because of an adverse event, excluding deaths, out of all randomized participants. The TRIUMPH-2 paper reports permanent treatment discontinuation due to an adverse event or death. The Lilly releases report "discontinuation due to adverse events".

| Trial | Source | Placebo | Lowest dose arm | Middle dose arm | Highest dose arm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 2 obesity | Paper | 0 | 7% | not applicable | 16% |
| Phase 2 type 2 diabetes | Paper | 4% | 2% | not applicable | 15% |
| TRIUMPH-1 | Paper (randomized population, excluding deaths) | 4.6% | 4.1% | 6.5% | 11.2% |
| TRIUMPH-2 | Paper (includes death) | 5% | 4% | 12% | 8% |
| TRIUMPH-3 | Company-reported | 4.8% | not tested | 9.8% | 13.5% |
| TRIUMPH-4 | Company-reported | 4.0% | not tested | 12.2% | 18.2% |

Points the sources add:

- **Phase 2 obesity.** Across all six retatrutide arms, 6% to 16% discontinued because of adverse events, and gastrointestinal events were the most common reason.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes.** 16 of 190 retatrutide participants (8%) discontinued, most often for gastrointestinal events (6, or 3%). The highest arm-level rate, 17%, was in an intermediate-dose arm with fast escalation.
- **TRIUMPH-1.** The figures are from Table 3 of the NEJM paper. The paper's results text gives 11.1% for the highest dose arm. Lilly's May 2026 release reported 4.9% with placebo and 4.1%, 6.9% and 11.3% in the dose arms (company-reported) without stating the population.
- **TRIUMPH-2.** The middle dose arm had a higher rate than the highest dose arm, in both the paper's counts and the company's percentages. Neither source accessible here explains why.
- **TRIUMPH-4.** Lilly said discontinuations were "highly correlated with baseline BMI" and "included discontinuations for perceived excessive weight loss". In participants with a baseline BMI of 35 or higher, the company-reported rates were 4.8% with placebo and 8.8% and 12.1% in the two retatrutide arms.

The ClinicalTrials.gov participant-flow tables list a separate figure: withdrawals from the *study* recorded as "Adverse Event". These are not the same as stopping study drug and are smaller numbers. They should not be read as discontinuation rates.

## Heart rate and its time course

Both phase 2 papers describe a rise in heart rate that peaked and then fell during treatment.

- **Phase 2 obesity (paper).** Heart rate "increased in a dose-dependent manner with retatrutide up to 24 weeks and then declined thereafter". The discussion describes the increase as peaking at 24 weeks, followed by a decline at 36 and 48 weeks. The numeric changes are in the supplementary appendix, which was not reviewed for this article, so no values are given here.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes (paper).** Pulse rose by "up to approximately 7 bpm" early in treatment and had decreased by 36 weeks. At 36 weeks the mean change ranged from 0.03 to 4.34 beats per minute across the retatrutide arms. It was minus 3.16 with placebo and 1.76 with dulaglutide. The paper says the week-36 retatrutide changes were not significantly different from dulaglutide. They were significantly different from placebo in every retatrutide arm except one escalation arm (p=0.0819).
- **TRIUMPH trials.** The abstracts and Lilly releases reviewed do not report heart-rate changes or their time course. The phase 3 time course is not covered here for that reason.

## Dysesthesia, allodynia and skin-sensation events

Different trials used different terms for these events. They should not be pooled as if they were one measure.

### What the phase 2 trials reported

The phase 2 obesity paper grouped events as "hyperesthesia or related". These occurred in 1% with placebo, 1% in the lowest dose arm and 13% in the highest dose arm, with a range of 1% to 14% across the retatrutide arms. The text says "cutaneous hyperesthesia and skin sensitivity adverse events were reported in 7% of the participants who received retatrutide and 1% of those who received placebo." It adds that none were severe or serious, none were associated with overt skin findings, and none led to discontinuation. The discussion says the events "did not appear to be related to the magnitude or rate of weight loss".

The posted ClinicalTrials.gov results for the same trial list three separate terms:

- **Allodynia:** 4 of 62 participants in the highest dose arm, and none in any other arm or with placebo.
- **Hyperaesthesia:** 1 participant on placebo, and reported in five of the six retatrutide arms.
- **Sensitive skin:** reported in three of the retatrutide arms and not with placebo.

In the phase 2 type 2 diabetes paper, "hyperaesthesia and related adverse events" occurred in 3 of 281 participants (1%): none with placebo or in the lowest dose arm, and 1 participant (2%) in the highest dose arm.

### What the TRIUMPH program reported

The phase 3 trials report **dysesthesia**. Lilly's Medical Information page defines it as "a skin burning sensation and related adverse events" and says events were mostly mild to moderate.

| Trial | Source | Placebo | Lowest dose arm | Middle dose arm | Highest dose arm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TRIUMPH-1 | Reported in the paper and company-reported by Lilly | 0.9% | 5.1% | 12.3% | 12.5% |
| TRIUMPH-2 | Paper | 1% | 4% | 6% | 7% |
| TRIUMPH-3 | Company-reported | 1.3% | not tested | 6.4% | 6.4% |
| TRIUMPH-4 | Company-reported | 0.7% | not tested | 8.8% | 20.9% |

Lilly said the TRIUMPH-4 events were "generally mild and rarely led to treatment discontinuation".

### What has not been characterized in these sources

- **Mechanism.** None of the sources reviewed establish why these events occur.
- **Onset and duration.** The sources give no time to onset or duration, and no breakdown of how many events resolved. Lilly's TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2/3 releases say dysesthesia events (reported together with urinary tract infections) were generally mild to moderate and that the majority resolved during treatment, without counts or timing.
- **Clinical workup.** No neurological examination, nerve-conduction or skin-biopsy findings for the phase 3 trials appear in the sources reviewed.
- **Terminology.** No source reviewed explains whether the phase 2 "hyperesthesia or related" cluster and the phase 3 "dysesthesia" definition cover the same preferred terms. Allodynia appears as a separate term only in the phase 2 obesity registry results.
- **Weight loss.** The phase 2 obesity statement that the events did not track the magnitude or rate of weight loss has not been repeated for phase 3 in the sources reviewed.

## Arrhythmia, hepatobiliary, pancreatitis and hypoglycaemia findings

These are reported only where a source gives them.

### Arrhythmia and cardiovascular events

- **Phase 2 obesity (paper).** Cardiac arrhythmias, meaning supraventricular arrhythmias and conduction disorders, occurred in 3% with placebo, 4% in the lowest dose arm and 11% in the highest dose arm. The range across the retatrutide arms was 0 to 14%. The paper says these events were mild to moderate, except one severe prolonged QT in a participant treated with ondansetron.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes (paper).** The same category occurred in 14 of 281 participants (5%): 2% with placebo, 4% in the lowest dose arm and 7% in the highest dose arm. The registry lists one serious atrial fibrillation event in a retatrutide arm.

The TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 papers (Table 3 in each) report counts of participants with severe or serious arrhythmias and cardiac conduction disorders. Lilly Medical Information reports the same counts (company-reported).

| Trial | Placebo | Lowest dose arm | Middle dose arm | Highest dose arm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TRIUMPH-1 | 5 | 1 | 4 | 5 |
| TRIUMPH-2 | 1 | 2 | 7 | 2 |

Adjudicated major adverse cardiovascular events were reported in all arms of both trials. Lilly states the two trials "were not designed or powered to evaluate cardiovascular safety".

**TRIUMPH-3 (company-reported).** This trial enrolled people with established cardiovascular disease. Lilly reported 44 vs 52 five-component MACE events (hazard ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.22) and 27 vs 23 three-component MACE events (hazard ratio 1.12, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.96), retatrutide vs placebo (pre-specified in-study analysis). Lilly says MACE occurred less often than anticipated in both arms. Both confidence intervals cross 1, and these figures do not establish a cardiovascular benefit or harm. A dedicated outcomes trial, TRIUMPH-Outcomes, is ongoing.

### Liver and gallbladder

- **Phase 2 obesity (paper).** Transient rises in ALT to more than 3 times the upper limit of normal occurred in 1% of retatrutide recipients. Mean ALT and AST were unchanged or lower at week 48. Biliary events occurred in 3 participants, all on retatrutide: 1 cholecystitis and 2 cholelithiasis.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes (paper).** Hepatic or biliary disorders occurred in 4 of 281 participants (1%), including 1 on placebo. Mean ALT and AST generally decreased. The investigator attributed one serious cholecystitis to study drug.
- **TRIUMPH-2 (paper).** The Lancet paper (Table 3) reports severe or serious gallbladder and biliary tract disorders in 3, 1 and 1 participants in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 1 with placebo, and severe or serious hepatic disorders in 1, 0 and 0 vs 1. Lilly Medical Information lists the same counts (company-reported).
- **TRIUMPH-1 (paper).** Table 3 of the NEJM paper reports severe or serious gallbladder or biliary tract disorders in 10, 5 and 4 participants in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 4 with placebo, and severe or serious hepatic disorders in 0, 0 and 3 vs 0. Hepatobiliary counts for TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4 were not in the sources reviewed.

### Pancreatitis

- **Phase 2 obesity (paper).** One adjudicated case of acute pancreatitis, in the highest dose arm, was reported as a serious adverse event. Otherwise, amylase and lipase increases were asymptomatic.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes (paper).** Two adjudicated pancreatitis events occurred, one in the lowest dose arm and one in a middle dose arm (one of the four arms between the lowest and highest). The investigator attributed one of them to study drug: it occurred 7 days after that participant's only dose.
- **TRIUMPH-2 (paper).** The Lancet paper (Table 3) reports adjudication-confirmed pancreatitis in 0, 1 and 2 participants in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 0 with placebo. Lilly Medical Information lists the same counts (company-reported).
- **TRIUMPH-1 (paper).** Table 3 of the NEJM paper reports adjudication-confirmed pancreatitis in 1, 3 and 4 participants in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 2 with placebo. Pancreatitis counts for TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4 were not in the sources reviewed.

### Hypoglycaemia

- **Phase 2 obesity (paper).** No clinically significant hypoglycaemia was reported.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes (paper).** No severe hypoglycaemia was reported. Hypoglycaemia below 54 mg/dL occurred in one participant in each of three retatrutide arms, including the highest dose arm, and in no one on placebo.
- **TRIUMPH-2 (paper).** The Lancet paper (Table 3) reports level 2 or level 3 hypoglycaemia in 2, 4 and 9 participants in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 5 with placebo. Lilly Medical Information lists the same counts (company-reported).

### Serious adverse events and deaths

- **Phase 2 obesity (paper).** Serious adverse events occurred in 4% of both the retatrutide and placebo groups. One death, by drowning, was assessed by the investigator as not related to retatrutide.
- **Phase 2 type 2 diabetes (paper).** There were no deaths. The investigator attributed three serious adverse events to study drug: cholecystitis, acute pancreatitis and starvation ketoacidosis.
- **TRIUMPH-2 (paper).** There were 2, 3 and 1 deaths in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms and 1 with placebo, all deemed unrelated by the investigator. The authors describe the safety profile as "generally consistent with other molecules with GLP-1 receptor agonism".

## What the trials can't tell us

- **Duration.** Treatment lasted 36 to 80 weeks. Events that take longer to appear, or that depend on years of exposure, are outside what these trials could detect.
- **Populations.** Both phase 2 trials ran at US sites only. The trials enrolled specific groups: people without diabetes, people with type 2 diabetes, people with cardiovascular disease and people with knee osteoarthritis. They screened out people with many other conditions. Results in one group do not automatically carry over to another.
- **Rare events.** Single-digit counts of pancreatitis, gallbladder or arrhythmia events cannot produce precise rates. The phase 3 rates for these events have not been fully published in the sources available.
- **Full phase 3 data.** Arm-level phase 3 data on heart rate and the time course of dysesthesia were not available in the sources reviewed. For hepatobiliary events and pancreatitis, the arm-level phase 3 figures used here are TRIUMPH-1 counts from Table 3 of the NEJM paper and TRIUMPH-2 counts from the Lancet paper, which Lilly Medical Information also lists. For hypoglycaemia, the arm-level phase 3 figures used here are TRIUMPH-2 counts from the Lancet paper, which Lilly Medical Information also lists. Much of the phase 3 picture above is company-reported, not peer reviewed.
- **Monitored conditions.** Trial participants received pharmaceutical-grade study drug under a protocol, with set escalation steps, scheduled visits, laboratory monitoring and adjudication of key events. Those conditions are part of what the numbers describe.

### Research-labelled "retatrutide" is not the trial drug

Retatrutide is not approved by the FDA or any other regulator. Lilly states that it "cannot be legally sold or marketed for human use" and is legally available only to participants in its clinical trials. Vials sold online as research-use-only "retatrutide" are not the trial material. Their identity, purity, sterility and contents are not covered by any of the data above, and their use is not monitored. The trial adverse-event rates do not describe what happens with those products. The [peptide legality guide](/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding) explains what research-use-only labelling does and does not mean. A separate article covers [what retatrutide testing and COAs can and cannot show](/blog/retatrutide-testing-coa-limits).

## FAQ

### What were the most common side effects in the retatrutide trials?

Gastrointestinal events: nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation and decreased appetite. In both phase 2 papers they were dose-related and mostly mild to moderate, and the obesity paper says they occurred mainly during dose escalation. In the TRIUMPH-2 paper, diarrhoea was reported in 27% to 34% of the retatrutide arms vs 13% with placebo, and nausea in 14% to 28% vs 8%.

### How many people stopped retatrutide because of adverse events?

In the phase 2 obesity trial, 6% to 16% of the retatrutide arms vs none on placebo. In the phase 2 type 2 diabetes trial, 16 of 190 retatrutide participants (8%) vs 2 of 45 on placebo (4%). In the TRIUMPH-2 paper, permanent discontinuation due to adverse events or death was 4%, 12% and 8% in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 5% with placebo. In the TRIUMPH-1 paper, discontinuation of study drug due to adverse events (randomized population, excluding deaths) was 4.1%, 6.5% and 11.2% in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 4.6% with placebo.

### Does retatrutide raise heart rate?

In the phase 2 obesity paper, heart rate rose in a dose-dependent manner, peaked at 24 weeks and declined at 36 and 48 weeks. In the phase 2 type 2 diabetes paper, pulse rose by up to about 7 beats per minute early in treatment and had decreased by 36 weeks. Heart-rate time courses for the phase 3 TRIUMPH trials were not available in the sources reviewed.

### What is the dysesthesia reported in the TRIUMPH trials?

Lilly defines it as a skin burning sensation and related adverse events. In the TRIUMPH-2 paper it was reported in 4% to 7% of the retatrutide arms vs 1% with placebo. The TRIUMPH-1 rate of up to 12.5% is reported in the paper and company-reported by Lilly. Company-reported rates were up to 20.9% in TRIUMPH-4. Lilly says events were generally mild to moderate and most resolved during treatment. The cause, timing and duration have not been characterized in the sources reviewed.

### Were pancreatitis, gallbladder or hypoglycaemia events reported?

In the phase 2 papers these events were few. The obesity trial reported one acute pancreatitis, three biliary events and no clinically significant hypoglycaemia. The type 2 diabetes trial reported two adjudicated pancreatitis events and three participants with hypoglycaemia below 54 mg/dL, none severe. In the TRIUMPH-1 paper, Table 3 reports severe or serious gallbladder or biliary tract disorders in 10, 5 and 4 participants in the lowest, middle and highest dose arms vs 4 with placebo, severe or serious hepatic disorders in 0, 0 and 3 vs 0, and adjudication-confirmed pancreatitis in 1, 3 and 4 vs 2. That table does not report hypoglycaemia. In the TRIUMPH-2 paper, Table 3 reports severe or serious gallbladder and biliary tract disorders in 3, 1 and 1 vs 1 with placebo, severe or serious hepatic disorders in 1, 0 and 0 vs 1, adjudication-confirmed pancreatitis in 0, 1 and 2 vs 0, and level 2 or level 3 hypoglycaemia in 2, 4 and 9 vs 5.

### Is research-labelled retatrutide the same as the trial drug?

No. Retatrutide is investigational and not approved anywhere. Lilly states it cannot be legally sold or marketed for human use. Products sold online as research-labelled retatrutide are not the material used in these trials, and the trial safety data do not describe them.

### Can these trials be compared with trials of other drugs?

Not reliably. The trials differ in population, duration, dose escalation and how events were defined and counted. This article reports results within each trial only and does not rank retatrutide against other drugs.
