Retatrutide and semaglutide both act on the GLP-1 receptor, but they are at very different stages. Retatrutide, from Eli Lilly, is an investigational once-weekly injection that also acts on GIP and glucagon receptors. Semaglutide, from Novo Nordisk, is a GLP-1 receptor agonist that the FDA has approved as Ozempic, Wegovy and Rybelsus. As of 19 October 2026, no head-to-head trial of the two has reported results. The one phase 3 trial that compares them directly, TRANSCEND-T2D-2, is still running.

This article covers what each molecule is, how their mechanisms differ, what each trial program measured, the side effects reported in the trial publications, and where each stands with regulators. Every efficacy figure is labelled with its estimand and its source. Retatrutide and semaglutide figures are kept in separate tables, because they come from different trials with different populations, durations and analysis methods.

Peptidemap is an RUO / grey-area peptide comparison publisher, not a clinic, pharmacy or supplier. This article is educational. It is not medical advice, gives no dosing, and does not say which drug is better. It is not a guide to choosing, switching or using either drug.

| Receptor targets | GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors | GLP-1 receptor |
| How it is given | Once-weekly subcutaneous injection, in clinical trials only | Once-weekly injection (Ozempic, Wegovy) or once-daily tablet (Rybelsus, Ozempic tablets, Wegovy tablets) |

**Retatrutide.** The TRIUMPH-1 paper in the *New England Journal of Medicine* describes retatrutide as "a triple-receptor agonist of glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide, glucagon-like peptide-1, and glucagon receptors." The 2023 phase 2 obesity paper calls it "a single peptide conjugated to a fatty diacid moiety" and reports a half-life of about 6 days, which allows weekly injection. An FDA letter dated 2 October 2026 describes it as a chain of 39 alpha amino acids with a side chain that includes a C20 fatty diacid.

**Semaglutide.** The current Wegovy prescribing information (NDA 215256/S-031, approved 5 May 2026) describes semaglutide as "a GLP-1 analogue with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1" that "selectively binds to and activates the GLP-1 receptor." Its peptide backbone is made by yeast fermentation. It is modified to resist breakdown by the DPP-4 enzyme and carries a C18 fatty diacid that binds albumin. Per the label, its elimination half-life is about 1 week. The same molecule is sold under several brand names, and each product has its own approved uses.

Peptidemap's compound pages are [Retatrutide](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/retatrutide) and [Semaglutide](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/semaglutide).

## Mechanism: triple agonist vs GLP-1 receptor agonist

Both molecules activate the GLP-1 receptor. The Wegovy label calls GLP-1 "a physiological regulator of appetite and caloric intake" and notes that the receptor is present in brain areas involved in appetite regulation.

Retatrutide adds two more targets:

- **GIP receptor.** GIP is a second gut hormone released after meals. In laboratory assays cited in the phase 2 obesity paper, retatrutide was more potent than natural GIP at the human GIP receptor.
- **Glucagon receptor.** Glucagon is a pancreatic hormone. The phase 2 authors write that adding glucagon receptor agonism "may further reduce energy intake, increase energy expenditure, or both." They describe this as a premise and a speculation, not an established human mechanism.

The same paper reports that, compared with the natural hormones, retatrutide is less potent at the human glucagon and GLP-1 receptors. A triple-receptor design is a difference in pharmacology. On its own, it says nothing about whether a drug is more effective or safer, and no trial has yet compared the two drugs directly.

## Has there been a head-to-head trial?

Not with results. A ClinicalTrials.gov search on 19 October 2026 found two Lilly-sponsored trials that randomize participants to retatrutide or semaglutide:

- **TRANSCEND-T2D-2 ([NCT06260722](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06260722)).** A phase 3, open-label trial in adults with type 2 diabetes who are not controlled on metformin, with or without an SGLT2 inhibitor. It has two retatrutide dose arms and one once-weekly injected semaglutide arm, and an estimated 1,250 participants. The primary endpoint is change in HbA1c from baseline to week 80. Secondary endpoints include body weight. The registry lists the status as active, not recruiting, with primary completion on 27 August 2026 and study completion estimated for February 2027. No results are posted, and a PubMed search found no publication.
- **A phase 1 mechanistic study ([NCT06982859](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06982859)).** About 95 adults with type 2 diabetes, comparing retatrutide with placebo on insulin secretion and insulin sensitivity over 28 weeks. A semaglutide arm, open-label, is included as a secondary comparison. The study is active, not recruiting, with completion estimated for January 2027.

Lilly's Medical Information page (last reviewed 21 April 2026) says that "no phase 3 head-to-head active comparator trials have been completed against retatrutide therefore no efficacy or safety comparisons can be made at this time."

So every efficacy and safety figure below comes from trials in which retatrutide or semaglutide was compared with placebo, never with each other. Network meta-analyses that estimate indirect comparisons have been published, but they are not randomized head-to-head evidence and are not used here.

## What the trials measured

### How to read the numbers

- **Dose arms.** The trials tested several dose arms. This article names them by position only: the lowest, middle and highest dose arm within each trial. Within the TRIUMPH program the positions refer to the same three dose levels; TRIUMPH-3 tested only the middle and highest. Semaglutide arm labels are separate and do not correspond to retatrutide positions.
- **Estimands.** Each figure is labelled with its estimand. Lilly's treatment-regimen estimand and Novo Nordisk's treatment-policy estimand both count everyone randomized, whether or not they stayed on treatment. Lilly's efficacy estimand and Novo's trial-product estimand estimate the effect had participants stayed on treatment as intended. Efficacy-type figures are usually larger, and they are the ones that tend to appear in headlines.
- **Sources.** "Paper" means a peer-reviewed publication. "Company-reported" means a Lilly or Novo Nordisk release or information page that has not been peer reviewed.

Points the sources add:

- **TRIUMPH-1.** The paper's primary analyses compared the middle and highest dose arms with placebo, with differences of −19.8 and −21.0 percentage points. The trial also included basket studies in people with knee osteoarthritis and obstructive sleep apnea, reported in the same paper. Lilly reports a mean baseline weight of 112.7 kg and a mean BMI of 40.0.
- **TRIUMPH-2.** Estimated treatment differences vs placebo under the treatment-regimen estimand were −6.9, −11.8 and −13.8 percentage points (all p<0.0001). Mean baseline BMI was 38.2 and mean baseline HbA1c 7.71%. Under the efficacy estimand, HbA1c changed by −1.41%, −1.63% and −1.54% vs −0.19% with placebo, reported in the paper's appendix and company-reported by Lilly.
- **TRIUMPH-3.** No paper was found. Lilly reports 44 vs 52 five-component major adverse cardiovascular events (hazard ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.22) and 27 vs 23 three-component events (hazard ratio 1.12, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.96) for pooled retatrutide vs placebo, in a pre-specified in-study analysis. Both confidence intervals cross 1. These figures do not establish a cardiovascular benefit or harm. A dedicated cardiovascular and kidney outcomes trial, TRIUMPH-Outcomes ([NCT06383390](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06383390)), is active, not recruiting, with primary completion estimated for February 2029.

It is the basis for Wegovy's cardiovascular risk-reduction indication.

### Why these tables are not a ranking

TRIUMPH-1 and STEP 1 both enrolled adults without diabetes, and TRIUMPH-2 and STEP 2 both enrolled adults with type 2 diabetes. They are still different trials. They ran for 80 vs 68 weeks, enrolled different people years apart, used different escalation schemes and lifestyle programs, and named and handled their estimands differently. Retatrutide also has no completed outcomes trial comparable to SELECT. Placing the numbers side by side does not show which drug works better. Only a randomized head-to-head trial, such as TRANSCEND-T2D-2 once it reports, can address that, and only in the population it studied.

The current Wegovy label carries a boxed warning on the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors, based on rodent findings. Retatrutide has no approved label, and having no labelled warnings is not evidence that it carries fewer risks.

## Regulatory status

**Retatrutide: investigational, not approved by the FDA or the EMA.**

- A search of FDA drug-approval records (openFDA Drugs@FDA data, 19 October 2026) found no retatrutide product. EMA's published list of medicines (downloaded 19 October 2026) includes no retatrutide entry.
- 23 July 2026: Lilly said it is completing the chemistry, manufacturing and controls package and plans to submit retatrutide for US approval in the first quarter of 2027, as a Biologics License Application (company-reported). A planned submission is not an approval.
- The FDA has twice concluded (March 2024 and October 2026) that retatrutide is a drug, not a biological product. A federal court sent the first decision back to the agency in October 2025, and Lilly's appeal is pending at the Seventh Circuit with no ruling as of 19 October 2026. For general background on FDA status and RUO labelling, see [our legality guide](/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding).
- Lilly states that retatrutide "cannot be legally sold or marketed for human use."

**Semaglutide: FDA-approved in several products (Drugs@FDA).**

- Ozempic injection (NDA 209637): approved 5 December 2017 for blood-sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes. A cardiovascular risk-reduction indication for adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease was added on 16 January 2020, and a kidney-outcomes indication for adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease on 28 January 2025.
- Rybelsus tablets (NDA 213051): approved 20 September 2019 for type 2 diabetes. A cardiovascular risk-reduction indication was added on 17 October 2025. On 30 January 2026 the FDA approved renaming one Rybelsus tablet formulation as Ozempic tablets (NDA 213051/S-030).
- Wegovy injection (NDA 215256): approved 4 June 2021 for chronic weight management in adults. Use in adolescents aged 12 and older followed on 23 December 2022. On 8 March 2024 a cardiovascular risk-reduction indication was added, based on SELECT, and on 15 August 2025 an accelerated approval for noncirrhotic MASH with moderate to advanced fibrosis. On 19 March 2026 the FDA approved a higher weekly maintenance dose for weight reduction.
- Wegovy tablets (NDA 218316): approved 22 December 2025.
- In the EU, the European Commission first authorised Ozempic on 8 February 2018, Rybelsus on 3 April 2020 and Wegovy on 6 January 2022 (EMA).

**The RUO and compounding context.** The FDA states that retatrutide "cannot be used in compounding under federal law" and is not a component of any FDA-approved drug. It also says it has warned companies that sold semaglutide, retatrutide and other GLP-1 drugs "falsely labeled 'for research purposes' or 'not for human consumption'" to consumers. Research-labelled "retatrutide" or "semaglutide" sold online is not the trial material or an approved product. Its identity, purity and contents are not covered by any of the data above. For how approval, compounding and RUO labelling differ, see [Are Research Peptides Legal? FDA & RUO Basics](https://peptidemap.com/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding).

## What the trials don't tell us

- **No head-to-head data yet.** TRANSCEND-T2D-2 has not reported. Even when it does, it will describe people with type 2 diabetes on metformin, in an open-label design, not people without diabetes.
- **Different durations and eras.** The TRIUMPH trials ran 80 weeks and reported in 2026. STEP 1 and STEP 2 ran 68 weeks and reported in 2021.
- **Peer review is partial for retatrutide.** TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 have papers. The efficacy rows are reported in those papers and company-reported by Lilly. TRIUMPH-3 figures are company-reported only.
- **Outcomes evidence differs.** Semaglutide has a completed cardiovascular outcomes trial in people without diabetes (SELECT) and FDA-approved cardiovascular and kidney risk-reduction indications. Retatrutide's outcomes trial is not expected to complete before 2029.
- **Long-term safety.** Semaglutide has been FDA-approved since December 2017. Retatrutide data come only from controlled trials lasting up to about two years.
- **Not about individual response.** Trial averages do not predict how one person will respond.

## FAQ

### Has retatrutide been compared head to head with semaglutide?

Not with results. TRANSCEND-T2D-2 (NCT06260722) is a phase 3, open-label trial comparing two retatrutide dose arms with once-weekly semaglutide in about 1,250 adults with type 2 diabetes, with HbA1c change at week 80 as the primary endpoint. As of 19 October 2026 it was active, not recruiting, with no results posted or published.

### How do retatrutide and semaglutide work differently?

Semaglutide is a modified GLP-1 analogue that activates the GLP-1 receptor only. Retatrutide is a single peptide that activates GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Researchers have proposed that the glucagon component may add effects on energy intake or energy expenditure, but this has not been established in humans, and a different mechanism does not by itself make a drug more effective or safer.

### Can the TRIUMPH and STEP weight results be compared directly?

No. They are separate placebo-controlled trials that differ in population, duration, era, dose escalation and estimand definitions, and none compared the two drugs with each other. This article reports results within each trial only and does not rank the two drugs.

### Why do some retatrutide figures look higher in news coverage?

They usually use a different estimand. In TRIUMPH-1, mean weight change in the highest dose arm was 25.0% under the treatment-regimen estimand in the NEJM paper and 28.3% under the efficacy estimand, reported in the paper and company-reported by Lilly. The first counts everyone randomized. The second estimates the effect had everyone stayed on treatment.

### What side effects were reported in the trials?

For both drugs, gastrointestinal events such as nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and constipation were the most common. In the TRIUMPH-2 paper, diarrhoea was reported in 27% to 34% of the retatrutide arms vs 13% with placebo, and dysesthesia and hypotension were more frequent than with placebo. In STEP 1, gastrointestinal disorders were reported in 74.2% of the semaglutide group vs 47.9% with placebo.

### Is retatrutide FDA-approved?

No. As of 19 October 2026, retatrutide is investigational and not approved by the FDA or the EMA. Lilly says it plans to submit it for US approval in the first quarter of 2027. The FDA has determined that retatrutide is a drug rather than a biological product, a classification Lilly is challenging in court.

### What is semaglutide approved for?

In the US, Ozempic (injection and tablets) and Rybelsus are approved for type 2 diabetes, with cardiovascular risk-reduction indications and, for Ozempic injection, a kidney-outcomes indication. Wegovy injection is approved for weight management in adults and adolescents aged 12 and older, cardiovascular risk reduction in adults with established cardiovascular disease and obesity or overweight, and MASH with moderate to advanced fibrosis. Wegovy tablets were approved on 22 December 2025.

### Is research-labelled retatrutide or semaglutide the same as the trial drugs?

No. Products sold online as research-use-only retatrutide or semaglutide are not the material used in these trials and are not FDA-approved products. The FDA says retatrutide cannot be used in compounding and has warned companies selling GLP-1 drugs falsely labelled for research purposes. This article is educational and is not medical advice.
