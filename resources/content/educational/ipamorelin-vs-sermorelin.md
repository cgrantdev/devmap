Sermorelin and ipamorelin are often listed together as "growth hormone peptides". Both make the pituitary release growth hormone (GH), but through different receptors and with very different records. Sermorelin is a fragment of the body's own GH-releasing hormone and was once an FDA-approved drug. Ipamorelin is a synthetic ghrelin-receptor agonist that reached phase 2 trials and was never approved. This article compares identity, mechanism, evidence and FDA status from primary sources. It is not a dosing guide, a protocol, a ranking or medical advice. Marketplace listings are on the separate [ipamorelin vs sermorelin compare page](/compare/ipamorelin-vs-sermorelin). Compound overviews are on the [Ipamorelin](/encyclopedia/Ipamorelin) and [Sermorelin](/encyclopedia/Sermorelin) encyclopedia entries.

## How to read this page

Each fact is labelled by source: **paper** (peer-reviewed, PubMed-indexed; "abstract" where only the abstract was read), **registry** (ClinicalTrials.gov), or **FDA document** (Drugs@FDA, the Federal Register, FDA bulk-substance lists, or Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) materials).

No company-reported figures are used. Doses are not given; multi-arm trials are described by arm position only. All sources were checked on 19 October 2026.

## What each compound is

### Sermorelin

Sermorelin is synthetic GHRH(1-29)-amide: the first 29 of the 44 amino acids of human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH). A 1999 review in BioDrugs describes sermorelin as "the shortest synthetic peptide with full biological activity of GHRH" and says it "specifically stimulate[s] growth hormone secretion from the anterior pituitary" (paper). PubChem lists its molecular formula as C149H246N44O42S. The U.S. brand was GEREF (sermorelin acetate), held by EMD Serono (FDA document).

### Ipamorelin

Ipamorelin is a synthetic pentapeptide, Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2, described by Novo Nordisk researchers in 1998 (paper). Three of its residues are non-standard: Aib (aminoisobutyric acid) and D-forms of 2-naphthylalanine and phenylalanine. FDA's 2024 briefing notes these "unnatural amino acids" complicate characterization (FDA document). PubChem lists its formula as C38H49N9O5. It is a growth hormone secretagogue (GHS) acting at GHS receptor type 1a (GHS-R1a), the receptor for the stomach hormone ghrelin. Drugs@FDA returns no application for it.

## Mechanism: GHRH receptor vs ghrelin receptor

### Two receptors, one pituitary output

GHRH from the hypothalamus is the main hormone stimulating GH release. Its receptor, cloned in 1992, is a G protein-coupled receptor expressed "predominantly, if not exclusively, in the anterior pituitary gland" (paper). Sermorelin, the active fragment of GHRH, works through it.

A second pathway came from synthetic secretagogues. In 1996 a separate G protein-coupled receptor in the pituitary and hypothalamus was cloned as their target (paper). In 1999 its natural ligand was found: ghrelin, an acylated 28-residue stomach peptide, showing GH release "may be regulated not only by hypothalamic GHRH, but also by ghrelin" (paper). Ipamorelin acts at this ghrelin receptor. In its 1998 characterization, antagonist experiments showed that ipamorelin, like GHRP-6, "stimulates GH release via a GHRP-like receptor" rather than the GHRH receptor (paper).

Sermorelin mimics the hypothalamic signal, so it depends on a pituitary that responds to GHRH; the BioDrugs review notes a normal response "cannot exclude growth hormone deficiency due to a hypothalamic deficit" (paper). Ghrelin receptors also occur outside the pituitary, including the gut, which is why ipamorelin was studied for postoperative ileus (temporary loss of gut motility after surgery). FDA's briefing also notes ghrelin receptors in brain reward regions (FDA document).

### Selectivity: what Raun 1998 showed and did not show

Raun and colleagues called ipamorelin "the first selective growth hormone secretagogue", based on these findings (paper, abstract):

- **In vitro (rat pituitary cells).** Ipamorelin released GH with potency and efficacy similar to GHRP-6.
- **In vivo (anaesthetized rats and conscious swine).** GH-releasing potency and efficacy were again comparable to GHRP-6.
- **Hormone specificity (swine).** None of the secretagogues tested changed FSH, LH, prolactin or TSH. GHRP-6 and GHRP-2 raised ACTH and cortisol. Ipamorelin did not raise ACTH or cortisol to levels significantly different from those after GHRH stimulation, "even at doses more than 200-fold higher than the ED50 for GH release".

The authors concluded its selectivity was "similar to that displayed by GHRH". The limits matter: these were animal experiments, the specificity work was in swine, and the comparators were GHRP-6, GHRP-2 and GHRH stimulation in animals, not sermorelin in people. No human head-to-head study of ACTH, cortisol or prolactin selectivity was found. "Selective" here means selective compared with older GHRPs, in animals.

### What human pharmacology studies add

- **Ipamorelin (paper, abstract).** A 1999 intravenous dose-escalation study in healthy men reported a terminal half-life of about 2 hours and a single GH episode peaking at about 0.67 hours, falling to negligible levels at every dose. The abstract describes eight subjects per dose level across five levels; FDA's briefing describes 48 enrolled, six active and two placebo per group (FDA document). Five groups of 6 + 2 would be 40, not 48, so the two descriptions do not fully reconcile; both are reported as stated.
- **Sermorelin (paper).** A 2003 review by Serono researchers gives GRF(1-29) a plasma half-life of "about 10-20 min in humans", mostly from renal filtration and N-terminal enzymatic breakdown. That is why longer-acting GHRH analogues were developed; the [CJC-1295 DAC vs no DAC article](/blog/cjc-1295-dac-vs-no-dac) covers their naming.

## Sermorelin's regulatory history

### Approval: two GEREF applications

Drugs@FDA lists two GEREF new drug applications, both held by EMD Serono (FDA document):

| Application | Product | Original approval | Drugs@FDA classification | Indication (per Federal Register) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NDA 019863 | GEREF (sermorelin acetate) injection, ampule | 28 December 1990 | Type 1, new molecular entity | Evaluating the ability of the pituitary somatotroph to secrete GH (diagnostic) |
| NDA 020443 | GEREF (sermorelin acetate) injection, vials | 26 September 1997 | Type 3, new dosage form; orphan | Treatment of idiopathic GH deficiency in children with growth failure |

Both products are now listed in Drugs@FDA as "Discontinued". Each carries the note "Federal Register determination that product was not discontinued or withdrawn for safety or effectiveness reasons".

The published paediatric record includes a multicentre, open-label GEREF International Study Group study of 110 previously untreated prepubertal children with GH deficiency, treated for up to one year (86 evaluable for efficacy). Mean height velocity rose from 4.1 cm/yr at baseline to 8.0 and 7.2 cm/yr at 6 and 12 months, with no adverse biochemical or hormonal changes reported (paper, abstract). The 1999 review adds that gains were smaller than with somatropin in the regimens compared, that effect on final adult height was not established, and that transient facial flushing and injection-site pain were the most common adverse events (paper, abstract).

### Discontinuation and withdrawal

FDA's 2013 Federal Register notice sets out the sequence (FDA document):

1. In a letter dated **11 July 2008**, EMD Serono told FDA that the diagnostic GEREF product (NDA 19-863) was being discontinued.
2. In a letter dated **2 December 2008**, EMD Serono said the paediatric GEREF product (NDA 20-443) was being discontinued and asked FDA to withdraw that NDA.
3. In a letter dated **12 December 2008**, EMD Serono asked FDA to withdraw NDA 19-863.
4. On **19 May 2009** (74 FR 23407), FDA withdrew approval of both NDAs, **effective 18 June 2009**. They were among 92 NDAs and 49 ANDAs in that notice.

None of these FDA documents gives a commercial reason. The 2009 notice says only that the holders told FDA the products were no longer marketed and asked for withdrawal.

### The 2013 determination: not withdrawn for safety or effectiveness

A citizen petition dated 12 October 2012 (Docket FDA-2012-P-1071) asked whether the paediatric product was withdrawn for safety or efficacy reasons; FDA also reviewed the diagnostic product on its own initiative. In a notice published on **4 March 2013** (78 FR 14095), FDA said it had "reviewed the available evidence and determined that both GEREF products were not withdrawn from sale for reasons of safety or effectiveness" (FDA document).

Both products stay on the Orange Book's Discontinued Drug Product List, and FDA may approve abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs) referencing them "as long as they meet all other legal and regulatory requirements". As of 19 October 2026, an openFDA query of Drugs@FDA for sermorelin returns only the two EMD Serono NDAs; no approved sermorelin ANDA was found. The determination made generic approval possible; it approved nothing.

### Compounding context today

Under section 503A, FDA says compounders may use only bulk drug substances that (FDA document):

- comply with a USP or NF monograph, if one exists;
- are components of FDA-approved drug products, if no monograph exists; or
- appear on the 503A bulks list, if there is no monograph and the substance is not a component of an approved drug.

Sermorelin's position in FDA's lists on 19 October 2026:

- **503A bulks list (21 CFR 216.23).** Sermorelin is not on it. The list names six substances, none of them peptides.
- **503A category list (updated 14 May 2026).** Sermorelin does not appear in any category.
- **503B category list (updated 21 March 2025).** "Sermorelin Acetate" is in Category 1 (under evaluation), double-asterisked as one of the "bulk drug substances that are components of FDA approved drugs". A footnote says FDA does not intend to act against an outsourcing facility filling orders for a drug that is "essentially a copy of an approved drug that has been discontinued and is no longer marketed".
- **Safety-risk page (current as of 22 April 2026).** Sermorelin is not listed.

This article does not decide whether any compounded sermorelin preparation qualifies under 503A or 503B; that depends on the product, the pharmacy and state law. FDA has not published a sermorelin-specific statement on how this applies to a drug whose approval was withdrawn, and absence from FDA's 503A lists is not, by itself, an FDA statement either way. A compounded drug is not FDA-approved, and a research-use-only "sermorelin" vial is neither GEREF nor a compounded drug. The [peptide legality guide](/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding) explains how these categories differ.

## Ipamorelin's clinical and regulatory history

### Human trials

Two registered phase 2 trials, both in postoperative ileus and sponsored by Helsinn Therapeutics (U.S.), Inc., tested ipamorelin as a treatment (registry).

**NCT00672074 (published as Beck et al. 2014).** A multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled proof-of-concept trial (April 2008 to December 2009) in adults having bowel resection, with intravenous ipamorelin or placebo after surgery for up to seven days or until discharge (registry; paper, abstract).

- 117 patients were enrolled. 114 formed the safety and modified intent-to-treat populations.
- Key endpoint, time to tolerating a standardized solid meal: median 25.3 hours with ipamorelin vs 32.6 hours with placebo (p = 0.15, not significant).
- The authors reported "no significant differences between ipamorelin and placebo in the key and secondary efficacy analyses".
- Any treatment-emergent adverse event: 87.5% with ipamorelin vs 94.8% with placebo. The abstract calls the drug well tolerated.

FDA's 2024 briefing document gives more detail from the paper (FDA document):

- Hypokalaemia 12.5% vs 3.4%; insomnia 10.7% vs 5.2%; hyperglycaemia at discharge 14.3% vs 8.6% (ipamorelin vs placebo).
- Serious adverse events: 10 participants (17.9%) with ipamorelin vs 9 (15.5%) with placebo, mostly after therapy ended.
- Two ipamorelin participants had fatal serious adverse events after anastomotic leaks. FDA wrote "it is unclear whether the deaths ... were related to ipamorelin", but that their occurrence, with the higher hypokalaemia and hyperglycaemia rates, "raises safety concerns about the use of ipamorelin in compounding".
- A subgroup analysis reported shorter bowel recovery with ipamorelin after open laparotomy; the authors urged caution given the small sample.

**NCT01280344 (no results posted).** A randomized, quadruple-masked, dose-finding phase 2 trial in bowel resection, with three ipamorelin dose-regimen arms (lowest, middle and highest dose arm) and placebo, all intravenous. The record lists 320 participants (actual), start April 2011, primary completion June 2013, completion May 2014, status Completed. No results are posted, and a PubMed search for the NCT number found no publication (registry; checked 19 October 2026). Its outcome is not public, and this article does not guess at it. FDA's briefing cites a 2020 review saying that after the Beck trial's "disappointing results, its development was discontinued" (FDA document).

**Healthy volunteers.** The only other human study FDA identified was the 1999 intravenous PK/PD study, which "did not state that there were any adverse events". FDA found no human data for the subcutaneous route the nominators proposed (FDA document).

### FDA bulk-substance status, with dates

- **503B Category 2, since 29 September 2023.** FDA's safety-risk page says compounded ipamorelin acetate "may pose risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration", citing the unnatural amino acids, serious adverse events including death in the intravenous ileus study, and missing safety data for other injectable routes. The 503B list (updated 21 March 2025) confirms Category 2 (FDA documents).
- **503A nominations withdrawn, then reviewed anyway.** Both nominators withdrew their nominations on 19 September 2024 (FDA-2015-N-3534-0471 and -0472), but FDA's slides for the 29 October 2024 PCAC say "FDA is electing to proceed" (FDA document). FDA's briefing says it evaluated both ipamorelin and ipamorelin acetate "on its own initiative". Reviewing GH deficiency and postoperative ileus, FDA "has not identified data to support the effectiveness" for either and proposed against listing (FDA document).
- **PCAC vote, 29 October 2024.** The final summary minutes record two separate votes. On ipamorelin free base: 0 yes, 12 no, 1 abstain. On ipamorelin acetate: 0 yes, 12 no, 1 abstain. Members voting no cited "a lack of information supporting safety and efficacy" for GH deficiency and postoperative ileus (FDA document).
- **Where it sits now.** FDA's safety-risk page (current as of 22 April 2026) still lists ipamorelin acetate in its main Category 2 table (503B, added 29 September 2023). It also appears in the "nominated but withdrawn" table, which notes that it "also appears in the table above because it is in category 2 under the 503B interim policy". It is not in the 503A category list (14 May 2026) or on the 503A bulks list in 21 CFR 216.23. A PCAC vote is advisory, and no Federal Register rule on ipamorelin under 21 CFR 216.23 was found as of 19 October 2026.

### The July 2026 PCAC and the October 2026 interim access policy

Neither compound was on the **23-24 July 2026** PCAC agenda, which covered BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, emideltide (DSIP), Semax and Epitalon (FDA document).

On **14 October 2026**, the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs received an FDA item titled "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" (RIN 0910-ZD94, stage: Notice). The listing names no peptides, and the text is not public, so whether it would cover either compound is unknown. It would not make any peptide an FDA-approved drug. The [peptide legality guide](/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding) tracks it.

## Evidence grades

These grades describe the type and size of the evidence. They are not a ranking.

| Evidence tier | Sermorelin | Ipamorelin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory approval | Yes, historical: GEREF diagnostic (1990) and paediatric GHD (1997) | None |
| Human randomized controlled trials | Small paediatric studies. One example randomized 43 children with hypothalamic GH deficiency to two GHRH(1-29) dose arms or GH for 6 months (paper, abstract). No large modern adult RCTs found | One published phase 2 RCT (117 enrolled), negative on its key endpoint. One completed phase 2 RCT (320 enrolled) with no posted or published results |
| Other human studies | Open-label paediatric study (110 treated). Small uncontrolled adult study: 11 healthy men aged 64-76, selected for low baseline IGF-1, given nightly GHRH(1-29) for 6 weeks. Nocturnal GH rose, but IGF-1, weight and body composition did not change; 2 of 6 muscle-strength measures improved (paper, abstract) | One healthy-volunteer intravenous PK/PD study (paper, abstract) |
| Animal and in vitro | Not reviewed here; the human record is the larger part of sermorelin's evidence | Most of the record: rat and swine GH release and selectivity, rodent postoperative ileus models, rat bone-growth and bone-mineral studies (papers) |
| Head-to-head human comparison | None found | None found |

**Grade summary.** Sermorelin: historical approval for narrow paediatric and diagnostic uses, backed by open-label and small randomized paediatric studies; adult data are small and uncontrolled. Ipamorelin: one negative phase 2 RCT, one unreported phase 2 RCT and one PK study; most data are from animals. Neither has controlled human evidence for the anti-ageing, body-composition or recovery uses common in marketing.

A retrospective review of 14 men on testosterone reported higher IGF-1 on a sermorelin, GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 combination (paper, abstract), with no control group and no way to separate each peptide's effect. Populations, routes, endpoints and eras differ across all these studies, so cross-trial comparison is not meaningful.

### Blends and combinations

FDA's briefing reports that outsourcing facilities compounded injectable ipamorelin-sermorelin combinations and that websites market ipamorelin with sermorelin or CJC-1295. FDA's adverse event database (FAERS) held two non-serious ipamorelin reports through 30 September 2023; for the one involving a two-peptide product, FDA said it was "not possible to assess a potential relationship between ipamorelin specifically and the reported adverse event" (FDA document). No randomized trial of the combination was found. A vial containing both is two receptor lanes in one container, not evidence of synergy.

## FAQ

### Are ipamorelin and sermorelin the same kind of peptide?

No. Sermorelin is GHRH(1-29)-amide, the first 29 amino acids of human growth hormone-releasing hormone, and it acts at the GHRH receptor. Ipamorelin is a synthetic five-residue peptide that acts at the ghrelin receptor, GHS-R1a. Both lead to growth hormone release from the pituitary, but through different receptors.

### Is sermorelin FDA-approved?

Not currently. FDA approved GEREF (sermorelin acetate) under NDA 19-863 on 28 December 1990 for diagnostic pituitary testing and under NDA 20-443 on 26 September 1997 for idiopathic growth hormone deficiency in children with growth failure. EMD Serono discontinued both products in 2008. FDA withdrew the approvals effective 18 June 2009. In 2013 FDA determined they were not withdrawn for reasons of safety or effectiveness. No approved sermorelin generic was found in Drugs@FDA.

### Has ipamorelin ever been FDA-approved?

No. Ipamorelin reached phase 2 trials for postoperative ileus. The published trial, NCT00672074, found no significant difference from placebo on its key and secondary efficacy endpoints. A second phase 2 trial, NCT01280344, was completed in 2014 but has no posted or published results.

### What does "selective" mean for ipamorelin?

It comes from a 1998 study in rats and swine. Ipamorelin released growth hormone about as potently as GHRP-6. Unlike GHRP-6 and GHRP-2, it did not raise ACTH or cortisol to levels significantly different from those after GHRH, even at doses more than 200-fold above the ED50 for GH release. This was selectivity compared with older GHRPs, in animals. It was not tested against sermorelin in humans.

### Can ipamorelin be compounded?

FDA lists ipamorelin acetate in 503B Category 2 for significant safety risks, effective 29 September 2023. On 29 October 2024, FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 0 yes, 12 no and 1 abstain on adding each of ipamorelin free base and ipamorelin acetate to the 503A bulks list, after FDA proposed against it. It is not on the 503A bulks list. Ipamorelin was not on the July 2026 PCAC agenda.

### Where does sermorelin stand for compounding?

Sermorelin is not on the 503A bulks list and does not appear in FDA's 503A category list. FDA's 503B list places sermorelin acetate in Category 1 and marks it as a component of an FDA-approved drug. FDA's 503A page lists components of FDA-approved drug products as one permitted source of bulk drug substances. FDA has not published a sermorelin-specific statement on how this applies to a drug whose approval was withdrawn, and absence from FDA's 503A lists is not, by itself, an FDA statement either way. Whether a specific compounded product qualifies is a legal question this article does not answer.

### Which one is better?

This page does not rank them. They act on different receptors, were studied in different populations for different uses, and have no head-to-head human trial. Neither has controlled human evidence for the anti-ageing or recovery uses common in marketing.

### Is a research-use-only vial the same as GEREF or a trial drug?

No. GEREF was a specific FDA-approved finished product, now discontinued, and the ipamorelin trial material was a sponsor's investigational drug used under a protocol. A research-use-only vial is neither, and none of the data on this page describe its identity, purity or sterility.
