## One molecule each, not the branded pen

Retatrutide (LY3437943) is one investigational molecule with agonist activity at three receptors: glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP), glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), and glucagon. The phase 3 papers describe once-weekly subcutaneous injection. It is not an approved medicine. A research-use vial is not a branded product and is not tirzepatide.

Tirzepatide is one peptide and a dual agonist at the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. It does not add glucagon-receptor agonism. In the United States it is approved as Mounjaro and as Zepbound for labeled uses. That approval is not a dosing guide. A research-use vial is not those brands.

The milligram figures below are assigned trial arms, not instructions.

## SURMOUNT-1 is tirzepatide without diabetes

SURMOUNT-1 (NCT04184622) randomized 2,539 adults with a body-mass index of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with at least one weight-related complication, excluding diabetes. Jastreboff and colleagues published it in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (2022;387:205-216; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2206038). Assigned treatment was once-weekly subcutaneous tirzepatide at 5 mg, 10 mg, or 15 mg, or placebo, for 72 weeks, including a 20-week dose-escalation period.

Coprimary end points were the percentage change in weight and a reduction of 5% or more. The treatment-regimen estimand counted everyone randomized, whether or not they stayed on treatment. At week 72 the treatment-regimen change was −15.0% at 5 mg, −19.5% at 10 mg, and −20.9% at 15 mg, versus −3.1% with placebo.

The same paper’s efficacy estimand is the average effect if treatment had been taken as intended. At week 72 that row was −16.0% at 5 mg, −21.4% at 10 mg, and −22.5% at 15 mg, versus −2.4% with placebo. Lilly’s 4 June 2022 release states the same efficacy-estimand percents. The −22.5% figure is the 15 mg efficacy estimand, not the treatment-regimen −20.9%. SURMOUNT-2 enrolled people with type 2 diabetes. Its percents are not used here.

## TRIUMPH-1 is retatrutide without diabetes

TRIUMPH-1 (NCT05929066) randomized 2,339 adults with obesity without diabetes to once-weekly subcutaneous retatrutide at 4 mg, 9 mg, or 12 mg, or placebo, for 80 weeks. Jastreboff and colleagues published it in the *New England Journal of Medicine* on 29 September 2026 (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2604169; PMID 42814954). The prespecified primary weight comparisons were 9 mg and 12 mg versus placebo. The 4 mg comparison was a key secondary end point.

Weight end points used a treatment-regimen estimand, which the paper treats as intention-to-treat. At 80 weeks the treatment-regimen change was −17.6% at 4 mg, −23.7% at 9 mg, and −25.0% at 12 mg, versus −3.9% with placebo.

The same paper’s efficacy estimand for that 80-week weight change was −19.0% at 4 mg, −25.9% at 9 mg, and −28.3% at 12 mg. The −28.3% figure is the 12 mg efficacy estimand, not the treatment-regimen −25.0%.

A prespecified extension later moved participants, including those from placebo, toward a maximum tolerated dose. That period is not the 80-week placebo comparison, so its percents are not used here. Knee-pain and sleep-apnea results are separate end points and are not used here.

## TRIUMPH-2 is retatrutide with type 2 diabetes

TRIUMPH-2 (NCT05929079) is retatrutide again, not tirzepatide and not TRIUMPH-1. It randomized 1,152 adults with a body-mass index of 27 or higher and type 2 diabetes to once-weekly subcutaneous retatrutide at 4 mg, 9 mg, or 12 mg, or placebo, for 80 weeks. Bellido and colleagues published it in *The Lancet* on 29 September 2026 (DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01861-1). Primary weight comparisons were 9 mg and 12 mg versus placebo. The 4 mg comparison was key secondary.

On the treatment-regimen estimand, mean percent change in body weight at week 80 was −11.9% at 4 mg, −16.8% at 9 mg, and −18.8% at 12 mg, versus −5.1% with placebo. On the efficacy estimand in the same paper, the mean change was −12.7% at 4 mg, −19.1% at 9 mg, and −20.8% at 12 mg, versus −4.0% with placebo. The −18.8% figure is the 12 mg treatment-regimen result in type 2 diabetes. It is not the TRIUMPH-1 treatment-regimen −25.0%, measured without diabetes.

## Why these percents cannot be stacked

A larger percent is not a winner across these programs.

- Molecule and time. Retatrutide is one GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon agonist, studied to 80 weeks. Tirzepatide is one GIP and GLP-1 agonist, studied to 72 weeks in SURMOUNT-1. Dual versus triple is a design difference, not a result.
- Population. SURMOUNT-1 and TRIUMPH-1 excluded diabetes. TRIUMPH-2 required type 2 diabetes. The −25.0% and −18.8% figures are both retatrutide 12 mg on the treatment-regimen estimand, in different populations.
- Estimand. Read treatment-regimen rows first. Efficacy-estimand rows ask what would have happened if treatment had continued as intended. Placebo also differs by estimand: treatment-regimen −3.1% and efficacy −2.4% in SURMOUNT-1; treatment-regimen −5.1% and efficacy −4.0% in TRIUMPH-2.
- Product. Mounjaro or Zepbound approval does not make a research-use listing those brands. A retatrutide trial does not make a research-use vial an approved drug.

A direct comparison is registered, not reported. TRIUMPH-5 (NCT06662383) is a phase 3 trial of retatrutide versus tirzepatide in adults with obesity. The ClinicalTrials.gov record retrieved on 5 October 2026 was active, not recruiting, with estimated enrollment of 800 and a primary outcome of percent change in body weight at week 80. That record had no results. No TRIUMPH-5 percent is used here. Subtracting a SURMOUNT percent from a TRIUMPH percent would invent a comparison the published trials did not run.
