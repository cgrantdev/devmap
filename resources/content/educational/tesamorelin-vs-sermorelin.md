## What each compound is

### Tesamorelin

Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of human growth hormone–releasing factor (GRF / GHRF). Per EGRIFTA WR prescribing information, it comprises the **44–amino acid sequence of human GRF** plus a **hexenoyl moiety** on the N-terminal tyrosine, prepared as an acetate salt (free-base molecular weight ≈ **5135.9 Da**).

Labeling describes binding and stimulation of human GRF receptors with similar potency to endogenous GRF, driving pituitary GH release and rises in IGF-1—the pathway behind both labeled use and labeled monitoring themes (IGF-1 elevation, glucose effects, fluid retention).

U.S. finished-drug products use the **EGRIFTA** brand family (including EGRIFTA SV and EGRIFTA WR). Current DailyMed labeling lists **initial U.S. approval: 2010**.

### Sermorelin

Sermorelin is a synthetic **amidated 29–amino acid peptide** corresponding to the amino-terminal segment of natural human GHRH—**GHRH(1–29)-amide** (GRF 1–29 NH₂). PubChem and ChEBI describe it as that biologically active N-terminal fragment of the full 44-residue GHRH.

The historical U.S. brand was **GEREF** (sermorelin acetate, EMD Serono). Unlike tesamorelin’s current products, GEREF is no longer a commercially marketed brand-name drug on the Orange Book’s active list; its NDAs were withdrawn after the sponsor discontinued marketing.

## Tesamorelin’s labeled indication—and its limits

Current EGRIFTA WR prescribing information (DailyMed; revised March 2025) indicates tesamorelin for **reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy**.

Explicit **limitations of use** on the same label:

- Long-term **cardiovascular safety has not been established**.
- **Not indicated for weight-loss management** (labeling describes a weight-neutral effect).
- **No data** support improved antiretroviral-therapy compliance in HIV-positive patients taking the product.

Those limits matter when tesamorelin appears in general “fat-loss” or wellness marketing. The approved finished drug’s labeled use is narrow—not open-ended body-composition use.

FDA approved the original Egrifta NDA **022505** on **November 10, 2010** (Drugs@FDA approval letter).

**Research-labeled vials named “tesamorelin” are not equivalent to approved EGRIFTA.** Approval attaches to specific finished products, manufacturers, and labeling—not to every catalog listing with a similar peptide name.

## Sermorelin’s earlier approvals and discontinuation (cited record)

FDA’s March 4, 2013 Federal Register notice (78 FR 14095) summarizes GEREF’s history:

1. **NDA 19-863** (0.05 mg base/amp) — approved **December 28, 1990**, for **evaluating** pituitary somatotroph GH secretion (diagnostic).
2. **NDA 20-443** (0.5 and 1.0 mg base/vial) — approved **September 26, 1997**, for **idiopathic growth hormone deficiency in children with growth failure**.

EMD Serono notified FDA in **2008** that the products were being discontinued and requested NDA withdrawal. On **May 19, 2009** (74 FR 23407), FDA announced withdrawal of both NDAs, **effective June 18, 2009**.

In 2013, FDA determined GEREF was **not withdrawn from sale for reasons of safety or effectiveness**, allowing ANDAs that reference those listed drugs if other requirements are met. The products remain on the Orange Book **Discontinued Drug Product List**—products discontinued for reasons **other than** safety or effectiveness.

That finding is a specific Orange Book determination. It does **not** mean every modern sermorelin listing is an approved generic or a GEREF equivalent.

## Who was actually studied (plain language)

### Tesamorelin

DailyMed describes **two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled** trials in **HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy and excess abdominal fat**, each with a 26-week main phase and 26-week extension.

Label inclusion themes: adults (age 18–65 in described criteria), sex-specific waist / waist-to-hip thresholds, and a stable antiretroviral regimen for ≥8 weeks before randomization. The primary efficacy assessment was **percent change in visceral adipose tissue (VAT)** by CT at L4–L5—not general weight loss.

Baseline demography in the label’s Study 1 / Study 2 text was predominantly male (mid-80% range), mean age about 48. Labeling notes 740 HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy treated across clinical trials (including extension exposure).

This page does not restate numerical effect sizes; see the FDA label / DailyMed clinical studies section for labeled efficacy numbers.

### Sermorelin (GEREF)

The Federal Register notice defines the **approved indications’ populations**: children with idiopathic GHD and growth failure (treatment NDA), and patients evaluated for pituitary GH secretory capacity (diagnostic NDA). This post does not invent pediatric trial outcomes or modern adult wellness results; those were not the labeled adult VAT story tesamorelin’s NDA pursued.

## FAQs

### Are tesamorelin and sermorelin the same?

**No.** Both are GHRH-pathway analogs, but tesamorelin is a modified full-length GRF analog (44 aa + hexenoyl), while sermorelin is the shorter GHRH(1–29)-amide fragment. Brand histories (EGRIFTA vs GEREF) and labeled uses also differ.

### Is either FDA-approved?

**Tesamorelin:** Yes—as specific EGRIFTA finished products for the labeled HIV-lipodystrophy indication (initial approval 2010; products remain on DailyMed).

**Sermorelin:** GEREF was FDA-approved historically (diagnostic 1990; pediatric treatment 1997). The sponsor discontinued marketing; FDA withdrew those NDA approvals effective June 18, 2009, later determining the withdrawals were **not** for safety or effectiveness. That is not a currently marketed brand-name GEREF product.

A research-use vial is **not** an FDA-approved drug merely because it shares a peptide name.

### Is this medical advice?

**No.** This is educational comparison content from a RUO / peptide-comparison publisher. It is not medical advice, not a prescription decision aid, and not instruction to use a research product as treatment.

### Which one should someone take?

This page **does not answer that**. No dosing, reconstitution, stacking, or product-selection guidance. Therapy decisions belong with licensed clinicians and approved labeling—not with a blog or research catalog.

## Bottom line

Tesamorelin and sermorelin share a GHRH-analog family, but the primary record separates them: tesamorelin’s current labeled story is adult HIV-associated excess abdominal fat (with clear limitations of use); sermorelin’s documented U.S. brand story is earlier pediatric/diagnostic GEREF approvals, sponsor discontinuation, and NDA withdrawal—with FDA later stating the withdrawal was not for safety or effectiveness. Marketplace RUO listings are a third lane entirely.
