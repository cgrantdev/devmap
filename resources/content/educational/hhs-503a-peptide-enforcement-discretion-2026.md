On 28 Oct 2026, the HHS Office of the Secretary announced a draft "Enforcement Discretion Policy Regarding 503A Compounding Using Certain Peptide Bulk Drug Substances" [1][2] [primary]. The Federal Register notice is FR Doc. 2026-22192 (docket HHS-OASH-2026-0496, RIN 0910-ZD94). It was filed on 28 Oct and published in the Federal Register on 30 Oct 2026 at 91 FR 68526 [2] [primary]. The notice says that, in listed circumstances, "HHS does not intend to take enforcement action against a State-licensed pharmacy, Federal facility, or licensed physician" compounding with the named substances [1] [primary].

## The six peptides

Appendix A lists the six peptides. Appendix B limits coverage to specific forms and routes [1] [primary]. The routes below describe the draft's scope only. They are not usage information.

1. [BPC-157](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/BPC-157): the draft lists the acetate form, for subcutaneous, intramuscular, oral and topical dosage forms.
2. [Epitalon](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/epitalon): the draft lists the acetate form, for subcutaneous or intramuscular injectable dosage forms "dispensed in intermittent courses."
3. [KPV](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/kpv): the draft lists the acetate form, for topical and oral dosage forms.
4. [MOTS-c](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/mots-c): the draft lists the acetate form, for subcutaneous or intramuscular injectable dosage forms.
5. [Semax](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/Semax): the draft lists the free base, for intranasal use.
6. [TB-500](https://peptidemap.com/encyclopedia/TB-500): the draft lists the acetate form, for subcutaneous or intramuscular injectable dosage forms only.

Other conditions also apply [1] [primary]:

- The product must contain a single listed active ingredient, so blends are outside the draft.
- The drug must be compounded under a valid patient-specific prescription.
- The ingredient must come from a 510-registered, CGMP-compliant original manufacturer and carry a COA that meets FDA-referenced testing criteria.
- Serious adverse events and product-quality issues must be reported to FDA's MedWatch and to the relevant States.
- The label must read "This is a compounded drug product. Not for resale."

For the full conditions, and for how this differs from RUO-labelled products, see our guide [Compounded vs RUO Peptides](/guides/compounded-vs-ruo-peptides). For background, see [Are Research Peptides Legal? FDA & RUO Basics](https://peptidemap.com/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding).

## Timeline

- **22 Apr 2026.** Appendix B says each of the six left FDA's interim Category 2 on this date. None is in Category 1 [1] [primary].
- **23–24 Jul 2026.** FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee met [4] [primary]. Appendix B records that "CDER recommended against listing" each peptide and that the PCAC voted in favour [1] [primary]. NPR reported that FDA's own scientists recommended against unwinding the restrictions [5] [press].
- **14 Oct 2026.** OIRA received the item for review under RIN 0910-ZD94, titled "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" [3] [primary].
- **27 Oct 2026.** OIRA concluded its review "Consistent with Change" [3] [primary].
- **28 Oct 2026.** HHS announced the policy, and the Federal Register notice was filed [2] [primary].
- **30 Oct 2026.** Federal Register publication at 91 FR 68526–68527. Comments are open "until 2 weeks after date of publication" [2] [primary]. Regulations.gov lists the comment period as closing on 13 Nov 2026 at 11:59 pm Eastern Time [7] [primary].

## What it is, and what is still pending

The notice calls the policy a "draft" and says it "does not establish any rights for any person and is not binding on HHS or the public" [2] [primary]. It is not approval: "Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved" [1][2] [primary]. None of the six is on the 503A Bulks List [1] [primary]. The draft has no end date. It applies "until a peptide bulk drug substance is addressed in a final rule," and HHS "may revisit" it if "a safety signal or product quality issue arises" [1] [primary].

Several things are still pending:

- **The comment period,** which Regulations.gov lists as closing on 13 Nov 2026 [7] [primary], and any revision to the draft [2] [primary].
- **Rulemaking.** "FDA's future rulemakings may propose adding some or all of these peptides to the 503A bulks list" [2] [primary]. NCPA reports that pharmacies "are still awaiting final rulemaking" [6] [press].
- **Supply.** NPR quotes a compounder who said "We won't be able to meet demand" [5] [press].

HHS presents the draft as a way to "discourage the continued, widespread use of certain research-use-only peptides supplied by foreign manufacturers that pose a safety risk to US patients" [1] [primary]. That is HHS's framing. The documents make no safety or efficacy finding for any of the six.

## FAQ

### Is this an FDA approval?

No. The issuer is the HHS Office of the Secretary, and the notice says compounded drugs are not FDA-approved [2] [primary].

### Is it final?

No. It is a draft, interim, non-binding policy with a two-week comment window [2] [primary].

### Are blends covered?

No. The draft requires a single listed active ingredient [1] [primary].

### Does it cover RUO-labelled products?

No. Its terms require 503A compounding, a patient-specific prescription and registered CGMP sourcing [1] [primary].
