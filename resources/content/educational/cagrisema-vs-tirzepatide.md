CagriSema and tirzepatide are two once-weekly injectable programs for obesity that reach appetite biology by different routes. CagriSema, from Novo Nordisk, pairs an amylin analogue with a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Tirzepatide, from Eli Lilly, is one molecule that acts on both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. In February 2026 they met head to head in REDEFINE 4, and CagriSema did not meet that trial's primary non-inferiority endpoint.

This article sets out what each product is, what REDEFINE 4 reported, what REDEFINE 1 showed for CagriSema against semaglutide alone, what safety data have been published, and where each stands with the FDA. Every efficacy figure is labelled with its estimand. Numbers from different trials are kept apart, because those trials differ in population, duration, design and comparator.

Peptidemap is an RUO / grey-area peptide comparison publisher, not a clinic, pharmacy or supplier. This article is educational. It is not medical advice, gives no dosing, and does not say which product is better.

## What each is

| | CagriSema | Tirzepatide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developer | Novo Nordisk | Eli Lilly |
| What it is | Fixed-dose combination of two molecules: cagrilintide and semaglutide | A single peptide |
| Targets | Amylin pathway (cagrilintide) plus the GLP-1 receptor (semaglutide) | GIP and GLP-1 receptors |
| How it is given | Once-weekly subcutaneous injection | Once-weekly subcutaneous injection |
| US status (19 October 2026) | Investigational; NDA submitted 18 December 2025 | FDA-approved as Mounjaro (2022) and Zepbound (2023) |

**CagriSema.** Novo Nordisk describes CagriSema as a fixed-dose combination of cagrilintide, a long-acting amylin analogue, and semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist. In Novo's plain wording, the two molecules "induce weight loss by reducing hunger, increasing feelings of fullness and thereby help people eat less." A search of FDA drug-approval records (openFDA Drugs@FDA data, 19 October 2026) found no approval for cagrilintide or CagriSema.

**Tirzepatide.** The FDA label describes tirzepatide as "a GIP receptor and GLP-1 receptor agonist" that "selectively binds to and activates both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors." It is a single modified peptide with a fatty-acid chain that binds albumin and prolongs its half-life. The label calls GLP-1 "a physiological regulator of appetite and caloric intake." The label says nonclinical studies suggest GIP "may further contribute to the regulation of food intake."

Put simply, CagriSema is two molecules in one product, acting on the amylin and GLP-1 systems. Tirzepatide is one molecule acting on the GIP and GLP-1 systems. The only shared target is the GLP-1 receptor.

## How REDEFINE 4 compared them

REDEFINE 4 (ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT06131437](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06131437)) is the head-to-head trial of the two products in obesity.

- Design: phase 3, randomized, parallel-group and open-label. Investigators and participants knew which drug was given.
- Population: 809 randomized adults without diabetes, with obesity and at least one comorbidity, with a mean baseline body weight of 114.2 kg.
- Arms: CagriSema at the full combination dose tested in REDEFINE 1 and 2 (the dose in the US filing), vs tirzepatide at its highest maintenance dose. Both were once weekly, and the registry lists a dose-escalation period at the start of each arm.
- Duration: 84 weeks of treatment.
- Primary endpoint: non-inferiority of CagriSema vs tirzepatide on relative change in body weight from week 0 to week 84. Superiority was a secondary endpoint.

Novo Nordisk announced company-reported headline results on 23 February 2026. They have not been peer-reviewed.

| REDEFINE 4, week 84 (company-reported) | CagriSema | Tirzepatide (highest maintenance dose) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean weight reduction, efficacy estimand | 23.0% | 25.5% |
| Mean weight reduction, treatment-regimen estimand | 20.2% | 23.6% |
| Primary endpoint (non-inferiority of CagriSema) | Not achieved | Comparator |

Novo defines the two estimands in footnotes. The efficacy estimand estimates the effect "if all people adhered to treatment," based on the trial protocol and regardless of dose modification. The treatment-regimen estimand estimates the "treatment effect regardless of treatment adherence." Healio's report on the same results says CagriSema did not achieve non-inferiority under either estimand.

What has not been released: the non-inferiority margin, confidence intervals, the share of participants still on treatment, and secondary endpoints such as the 25% and 30% weight-reduction thresholds listed on the registry. As of 19 October 2026, the ClinicalTrials.gov record shows the trial as completed (primary completion 8 December 2025) with no results posted, and a PubMed search found no peer-reviewed REDEFINE 4 paper.

REDEFINE 4 is the only obesity trial here that randomized people to CagriSema or tirzepatide. Its figures should not be lined up against tirzepatide's or CagriSema's placebo-controlled trials, which used different populations, durations, blinding and comparators.

## CagriSema vs semaglutide: REDEFINE 1

REDEFINE 1 (Garvey et al., *N Engl J Med* 2025; [NCT05567796](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05567796)) did not include tirzepatide. It matters here because it tested CagriSema against each of its own components, which shows what adding cagrilintide to semaglutide did within one trial.

- Design: phase 3a, 68 weeks, multicenter, double-blind, placebo- and active-controlled.
- Population: 3,417 adults without diabetes, with a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with at least one obesity-related complication. Mean baseline weight was 106.9 kg, and 67.6% of participants were women.
- Arms (randomized 21:3:3:7): CagriSema (2,108), semaglutide alone (302), cagrilintide alone (302) and placebo (705). Each active arm used the same component doses, and all groups received lifestyle intervention.
- Primary endpoints: percent change in body weight and the share reaching at least 5% weight reduction, CagriSema vs placebo, at week 68.

The paper reports two estimands. The treatment-policy estimand counts everyone regardless of whether they stopped treatment or used rescue therapy, which is the same kind of question as REDEFINE 4's treatment-regimen estimand. The trial-product estimand estimates the effect if the drugs were taken as intended, which corresponds to REDEFINE 4's efficacy estimand.

| REDEFINE 1, week 68 | CagriSema | Semaglutide alone | Cagrilintide alone | Placebo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mean weight change, treatment-policy estimand | −20.4% | −14.9% | −11.5% | −3.0% |
| Mean weight change, trial-product estimand | −22.7% | −16.1% | −11.8% | −2.3% |

Under the treatment-policy estimand, the estimated difference for CagriSema was −17.3 percentage points vs placebo (95% CI −18.1 to −16.6), −5.5 points vs semaglutide alone (95% CI −6.7 to −4.3) and −8.9 points vs cagrilintide alone (95% CI −10.1 to −7.7). Also under the treatment-policy estimand, 34.7% of the CagriSema group reached a weight reduction of 25% or more, compared with 14.8% with semaglutide alone and 6.5% with cagrilintide alone.

Dosing was flexible. At week 68, 57.4% of the CagriSema group were on the maximum dose, compared with 70.9% of the semaglutide group and 82.5% of the cagrilintide group. Treatment ran 68 weeks in REDEFINE 1 and 84 weeks in REDEFINE 4. The trials also differ in blinding and population, so REDEFINE 1 figures are not interchangeable with REDEFINE 4 figures.

## Safety and tolerability as reported

**REDEFINE 4.** Only a qualitative summary has been published. Novo Nordisk said the most common adverse events with CagriSema were gastrointestinal, that "the vast majority were mild to moderate and diminished over time," and that this was "consistent with the GLP-1 receptor agonist class." No arm-level rates of adverse events, gastrointestinal events or discontinuation have been released for either arm in a primary source. Without them, the two arms cannot be compared on tolerability.

**REDEFINE 1.** The NEJM paper gives arm-level data.

| REDEFINE 1 safety | CagriSema | Semaglutide alone | Cagrilintide alone | Placebo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gastrointestinal adverse events (on treatment) | 79.6% | 73.8% | 54.0% | 39.9% |
| Adverse event leading to permanent discontinuation | 5.9% | 3.6% | 2.6% | 3.5% |
| Gastrointestinal adverse event leading to permanent discontinuation | 3.6% | 1.3% | 1.3% | 0.6% |
| Serious adverse events | 9.8% | 5.0% | 8.9% | 6.1% |
| Gallbladder-related disorders (on treatment) | 4.1% | 3.0% | 2.3% | 1.0% |
| Injection-site reactions (on treatment) | 12.2% | 2.6% | 16.9% | 3.0% |

The authors reported that most gastrointestinal events were mild to moderate. Nausea, diarrhea and vomiting peaked during dose escalation and then decreased, while constipation stayed relatively constant after dose escalation. Novo Nordisk's summary of the paper gives nausea at 55% vs 12.6% with placebo, constipation at 30.7% vs 11.6%, and vomiting at 26.1% vs 4.1%. Two deaths were reported in the CagriSema group. The adjudication committee attributed them to suicide and cancer.

For tirzepatide, the prescribing information for Mounjaro and Zepbound is the reference for side effects and warnings. Rates from tirzepatide's own trials are not set against the CagriSema figures above, because they come from different trials.

## Regulatory status

**CagriSema: not FDA-approved.**

- 18 December 2025: Novo Nordisk announced it had submitted a New Drug Application to the FDA for CagriSema "to reduce excess body weight and maintain weight reduction long term" in adults with obesity, or with overweight and at least one weight-related condition. The submission is based on REDEFINE 1 and REDEFINE 2. Novo's release states: "CagriSema is not approved in the US or EU."
- 23 February 2026: in the REDEFINE 4 announcement, Novo said an FDA decision was "anticipated by late 2026."
- 4 August 2026: Novo's half-year report listed a CagriSema "US decision" as an expected milestone for the fourth quarter of 2026.
- 21 September 2026: Novo said a decision "is expected in Q4 2026."
- No specific FDA action date has been published by Novo Nordisk or the FDA in the sources reviewed. CagriSema did not appear on the FDA's Novel Drug Approvals for 2026 page (content current as of 9 October 2026). Expected timing can change, and an expected decision is not an approval.

**Tirzepatide: FDA-approved under two brand names (Drugs@FDA).**

- Mounjaro (NDA 215866): first approved 13 May 2022 as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve blood-sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes. On 19 December 2025 the indication was expanded to patients aged 10 and older with type 2 diabetes. On 27 August 2026 the FDA approved a new indication to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with type 2 diabetes at high risk of those events, based on SURPASS-CVOT.
- Zepbound (NDA 217806): first approved 8 November 2023 for chronic weight management in adults with obesity, or with overweight and at least one weight-related condition, alongside diet and physical activity. On 20 December 2024 the FDA added an indication to treat moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity.

**The RUO context.** Research-labelled "cagrilintide," or cagrilintide blends sold online, are not CagriSema. They are not the product tested in REDEFINE, and they are not FDA-approved. For how approval, compounding and RUO labelling differ, see [Are Research Peptides Legal? FDA, RUO & Compounding Explained](https://peptidemap.com/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding).

## What the trials don't tell us

- **No head-to-head safety numbers.** REDEFINE 4 has no published arm-level rates for adverse events, gastrointestinal events or discontinuation.
- **No peer-reviewed REDEFINE 4 paper yet.** The non-inferiority margin, confidence intervals and secondary endpoints are not public in the sources reviewed.
- **Open-label design.** Participants and investigators in REDEFINE 4 knew the assigned drug, which can affect behavior, adherence and reporting.
- **One dose pairing.** REDEFINE 4 tested one CagriSema dose against tirzepatide's highest maintenance dose. It does not describe other dose levels. Novo's half-year report says a phase 3 trial of a higher-dose CagriSema combination has started and is expected to read out in the first half of 2028.
- **Other head-to-heads in a different population.** Novo has company-reported two head-to-head trials against tirzepatide in adults with type 2 diabetes. Neither is peer-reviewed. In REIMAGINE 4 (68 weeks, open-label; full CagriSema dose vs tirzepatide's highest maintenance dose), CagriSema met non-inferiority for weight but not for HbA1c. On the efficacy estimand, weight reduction was 15.2% vs 15.8% and HbA1c fell 1.9 vs 2.2 percentage points. In REIMAGINE 5 (60 weeks; a lower CagriSema dose vs a low tirzepatide dose), weight reduction on the efficacy estimand was 12.4% vs 9.1%, and Novo reported HbA1c reductions of 1.71 vs 1.67 percentage points without naming the estimand. Novo said CagriSema met weight superiority and HbA1c non-inferiority in REIMAGINE 5. These trials answer different questions from REDEFINE 4 and should not be merged with it.
- **No cardiovascular outcomes for CagriSema yet.** REDEFINE 3, an event-driven cardiovascular outcomes trial, is listed on ClinicalTrials.gov as active, not recruiting.
- **Cross-trial comparisons are not valid.** REDEFINE 1 lasted 68 weeks, was double-blind and enrolled adults without diabetes. REDEFINE 4 lasted 84 weeks and was open-label. Tirzepatide's placebo-controlled trials used other populations and durations. Only within-trial comparisons are reported here.
- **Not about individual response.** Trial averages do not predict how one person will respond. Choices between approved medicines belong with a licensed clinician.

## FAQ

### Is CagriSema FDA-approved?

No. As of 19 October 2026, CagriSema is investigational and not FDA-approved. Novo Nordisk submitted a New Drug Application for weight management on 18 December 2025, and its 21 September 2026 announcement says an FDA decision is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. That is an expected timeframe, not an outcome.

### What did REDEFINE 4 compare?

REDEFINE 4 was an open-label, 84-week phase 3 trial that randomized 809 adults with obesity and at least one comorbidity to once-weekly CagriSema or once-weekly tirzepatide at its highest maintenance dose. The primary endpoint was non-inferiority of CagriSema on percent weight change at week 84.

### Did CagriSema meet its primary endpoint against tirzepatide?

No. In company-reported headline results, Novo Nordisk said non-inferiority was not achieved. Mean weight reduction was 23.0% with CagriSema vs 25.5% with tirzepatide under the efficacy estimand, and 20.2% vs 23.6% under the treatment-regimen estimand. Those figures are from one head-to-head trial and should not be compared with numbers from other trials.

### Why are there two sets of numbers for each trial?

They answer different questions. In REDEFINE 4, the efficacy estimand estimates the effect if everyone had stayed on treatment, and the treatment-regimen estimand counts everyone regardless of adherence. REDEFINE 1 uses the names trial-product and treatment-policy for two analogous questions. A number without its estimand label is easy to misread.

### How is CagriSema different from tirzepatide?

CagriSema is a fixed-dose combination of two molecules: cagrilintide, a long-acting amylin analogue, and semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Tirzepatide is a single peptide that activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. The two products reach appetite pathways by different routes.

### What were the main side effects reported?

In REDEFINE 4, Novo Nordisk said the most common adverse events with CagriSema were gastrointestinal and mostly mild to moderate, but it has not published arm-level adverse event or discontinuation figures. In REDEFINE 1, gastrointestinal adverse events were reported in 79.6% of the CagriSema group vs 39.9% with placebo, and 5.9% vs 3.5% stopped treatment permanently because of adverse events.

### Is research-labelled cagrilintide the same as CagriSema?

No. Research-labelled cagrilintide vials or blends sold online are not CagriSema, are not the product studied in the REDEFINE trials, and are not FDA-approved. This article is educational and is not medical advice.
