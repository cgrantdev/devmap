Selank and Semax are usually sold, searched and discussed as a pair. Both are synthetic seven-amino-acid peptides developed in Moscow, both end in the same three residues, and both are described as registered medicines in Russia while having no FDA approval. They are still different molecules with different parent hormones, different research records and different standing at the FDA. Selank is built on tuftsin, a small immune peptide. Semax is built on a fragment of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH). This article compares identity, proposed mechanisms, the strength of the evidence and regulatory status, with dates, from primary sources. It is not a dosing guide, a protocol, a ranking or medical advice. Marketplace listings are on the separate [Selank vs Semax compare page](/compare/selank-vs-semax). Compound overviews are on the [Selank](/encyclopedia/selank) and [Semax](/encyclopedia/Semax) encyclopedia entries.

## How to read this page

Each fact is labelled by source: **paper** (peer-reviewed and PubMed-indexed; "abstract" where only the abstract was read), **registry** (ClinicalTrials.gov or PubChem), **FDA document** (FDA bulk-substance lists, the FDA safety-risk page, FDA substance records, or Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) materials), **government document** (other U.S. government records, such as OIRA listings on reginfo.gov), or **news** (news report).

Much of the clinical literature on both peptides is in Russian, and many of the papers were co-written by researchers from the institutes that developed them. Where that applies, the finding is labelled **developer-affiliated**. It is the closest equivalent here to company-reported data. Animal-only and cell-only findings are labelled as such. No doses are given. All sources were checked on 19 October 2026.

## What each compound is

### Selank

Selank is the heptapeptide Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (registry). A 2016 paper by researchers at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (RAS) says it "was designed and produced" at that institute "in cooperation with the V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology" (paper, developer-affiliated). The same paper describes it as a synthetic analogue of tuftsin, "the short Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg fragment of the human immunoglobulin G heavy chain", lengthened by Pro-Gly-Pro "to improve its metabolic stability" (paper). Tuftsin itself was described in 1970 as "a natural phagocytosis stimulating peptide" (paper). FDA's substance registry lists the code name TP-7 among Selank's names, and FDA's safety-risk page uses "Selank acetate (TP-7)" (FDA documents).

### Semax

Semax is the heptapeptide Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro (registry; FDA document). FDA's July 2026 briefing calls it "a synthetic heptapeptide analog of the ACTH 4-10 fragment" that is "devoid of the hormonal corticotropic and melanotropic properties" of ACTH (FDA document). In sequence terms it keeps the first four residues of that fragment, Met-Glu-His-Phe, which is ACTH(4-7), and replaces the last three with Pro-Gly-Pro. FDA's substance registry lists it under the name "ACTH (4-7), PRO-GLY-PRO-" (FDA document). A 2022 review of Russian peptide drugs says Semax was synthesized "by scientists at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the RAS" under academicians I. P. Ashmarin and N. F. Myasoedov (paper).

FDA notes that "Semax is a common name and not a United States Adopted Name", and that it has "encountered multiple salts, and derivatives, including different active moieties, sold commercially under the same common name" (FDA document). The free base and the acetate salt are treated by FDA as two different bulk drug substances.

### What they share: the Pro-Gly-Pro tail

Both peptides end in Pro-Gly-Pro, a "glyproline" motif. For Semax, FDA's briefing says this C-terminal sequence "is thought to increase the heptapeptide stability to hydrolysis catalyzed by peptidases" (FDA document). The Selank paper above gives the same reason for its tail (paper). The shared tail is why the two look alike on a label. The first four residues, which come from two unrelated parent molecules, are where they differ.

## Proposed mechanisms, as reported

Neither peptide has an established molecular target. The proposals below come mostly from rodent and cell work, much of it developer-affiliated.

### Selank

- **Enkephalin-degrading enzymes (in vitro, plus patient blood measurements).** A 2001 study measured shorter enkephalin half-life in the blood of people with generalized anxiety and reported that Selank "dose-dependently inhibited enzymatic hydrolysis of plasma enkephalin" in vitro (paper, abstract, developer-affiliated).
- **GABA-A receptor modulation (cell and membrane work).** A 2018 radioligand study reported that Selank affected GABA binding "as a positive allosteric modulator" in brain membrane preparations (paper, abstract, developer-affiliated). A 2016 rat study reported changes in the expression of genes involved in neurotransmission in the frontal cortex, many of them GABA-related, and proposed "allosteric modulation of the GABAergic system" as one possible mechanism (paper, animal-only, developer-affiliated).
- **Stress behaviour (animal-only).** A 2003 study compared ten tuftsin-family peptides and Selank in rats and mice in a conflict-stress model and reported "positive emotional effects and antistress actions" (paper, abstract, animal-only, developer-affiliated).

A 2021 review by U.S. pharmacists called phenibut and Selank "poorly studied Russian drugs with GABAergic mechanisms that are inexplicably sold to US consumers as dietary supplements" (paper, abstract).

### Semax

- **BDNF and its receptor (animal-only).** Two 2006 rat studies reported that Semax bound specific sites in basal forebrain membranes and raised brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) protein, and that a single application increased BDNF and trkB receptor expression in the hippocampus (papers, abstracts, developer-affiliated).
- **Ischemia models (animal-only).** FDA's briefing summarizes rat studies in which Semax reduced ischemic damage, and notes that dose-response relationships and the exposure needed were not established (FDA document).
- **Pain, serotonin and dopamine (animal-only).** FDA summarizes rodent work linking Semax's analgesic effect partly to serotonergic signalling. It adds that Semax "potentiated amphetamine-induced dopamine release" in mice, which it calls "concerning because increased dopaminergic tone in the striatum is a response typically induced by drugs of abuse", and that no studies of abuse potential were found (FDA document).
- **Clotting (animal-only).** FDA cites rat studies reporting anticoagulant and antithrombotic effects, possibly from the metabolite Pro-Gly-Pro, and says this "raises concern about the risk of bleeding" (FDA document).
- **FDA's overall view.** "Acting through poorly understood mechanisms of action", Semax in rodents showed neuroprotective, anticoagulant, analgesic and antidepressant- and anxiolytic-like effects (FDA document).

FDA found no human pharmacokinetic studies of Semax by any route. In rats, Semax is quickly broken down, with Pro-Gly-Pro as the main metabolite (FDA document).

### The one mechanism measured side by side

A 2001 study tested both peptides in the same assay. Semax and Selank each inhibited enkephalin-degrading enzymes from human serum in vitro, in a dose-dependent way. Their five-residue fragments also did, while their three-, four- and six-residue fragments did not (paper, abstract in English, article in Russian, developer-affiliated). This is a test-tube finding. It shows a shared property in one assay. It does not show that the two act the same way in people.

## Evidence tiers

### How the evidence is graded here

- **Controlled human trial:** a human study with a concurrent comparison group reporting a clinical outcome.
- **Small or uncontrolled human:** pilot, open-label or imaging studies, or trials of a few dozen people.
- **Animal/cell (animal-only):** rodents or cultured cells. These show biological plausibility. They do not show effects in people.

These grades describe the type and size of the evidence. They are not a ranking.

### Selank in humans

- **Comparison with medazepam (2008).** 62 patients with generalized anxiety disorder or neurasthenia, 30 given Selank and 32 given the benzodiazepine medazepam. The authors reported that "the anxiolytic effects of both drugs were similar" (paper, abstract; article in Russian; developer-affiliated).
- **Comparison with phenazepam (2014).** 60 patients with anxiety and somatoform disorders, Selank compared with the benzodiazepine phenazepam. The abstract reports anxiolytic and "mild nootropic" effects for Selank but gives no between-group figures (paper, abstract; article in Russian; developer-affiliated).
- **No placebo comparison found.** Both clinical comparisons used an active drug, not placebo, and neither abstract describes randomization or blinding.

### Semax in humans

- **Acute stroke (1997).** 30 patients given Semax alongside standard intensive care, compared with 80 patients on conventional therapy. The authors reported some influence on the rate of neurological recovery (paper, abstract; article in Russian; developer-affiliated).
- **Stroke rehabilitation (2018).** 110 patients after ischemic stroke, split into early and late rehabilitation groups and then into subgroups with and without Semax. The authors reported higher plasma BDNF and better Barthel index recovery with Semax (paper, abstract; article in Russian).
- **What FDA counted.** Across the eight human references it reviewed, FDA found Semax had been given to 33-47 healthy adults, 69 adults with medical conditions and 451 children, all by the nasal route. Three references were meeting abstracts, and most did not discuss safety. FDA did not consider Russian-language articles on cerebral ischemia without English translations (FDA document).
- **FDA's effectiveness conclusion.** "There is insufficient evidence to support the effectiveness for semax (free base) or semax acetate for cerebral ischemia, migraine, or trigeminal neuralgia" (FDA document). FDA did not evaluate "nootropic" as a separate use, noting it has no ICD-10 code (FDA document).
- **Adverse event reports.** FDA searched its Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) through 3 December 2025 and found one report for Semax-related substances: a consumer reported eye pain and burning, and hospitalization, after using nasal drops bought online (FDA document). FAERS reports are unverified and do not show that a product caused an event.

**Grade summary.** Both peptides rest on small, mostly Russian-language clinical studies, much of it developer-affiliated, plus a larger body of rodent work. Neither has an English-language, placebo-controlled trial that we found, and neither has controlled human evidence for the memory, focus or mood claims common in marketing. Populations, endpoints and eras differ, so cross-study comparison is not meaningful.

## Has anyone compared or combined them?

We searched PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov on 19 October 2026. PubMed returned 136 records for "selank", 233 for "semax" and 14 mentioning both. ClinicalTrials.gov returned no registered study using either peptide as an intervention; the few keyword hits were unrelated studies.

Among the 14 PubMed records:

- **One human study placed both in a single design.** In 52 healthy participants, resting-state functional MRI was recorded before and 5 and 20 minutes after Semax, Selank or placebo. The authors reported differences in connectivity between the right amygdala and right temporal regions and described "both general and specific effects" of each peptide (paper, abstract, developer-affiliated). It measured brain imaging over minutes, not any clinical outcome, and the abstract does not describe randomization or blinding.
- **Animal and cell studies tested them in parallel.** These include the 2001 enzyme assay above, a rat Parkinson's-model study in which neither peptide changed motor activity and Selank reduced anxiety-like behaviour (paper, abstract, animal-only, developer-affiliated), and a mouse stem-cell toxicity screen (paper, abstract, animal/cell, developer-affiliated).
- **No study gave them together.** We found no study of a Selank-plus-Semax combination in humans or animals.

So the claim that the two "work together" or should be combined is not based on a direct test. Combination products exist on the market anyway: FDA's briefing notes that Semax is "marketed as single ingredient formulations and in combination with selank" (FDA document). A 2020 study from Belgium's national public health institute identified Selank and Semax in two seized "suspicious preparations" and urged control laboratories to be ready to identify such peptides (paper, abstract).

## Regulatory status, with dates

### Russia

- **Semax.** FDA's briefing states that "Semax is a registered drug in Russia" available as nasal drops, citing the Russian State Register of Medicines, accessed 4 December 2025 (FDA document).
- **Selank.** A 2022 review of Russian peptide drugs lists Selank, as an anxiolytic, among 14 products it describes as "registered in the Russian Federation", and a 2026 review describes it as "approved in Russia for the treatment of anxiety" (papers). We could not confirm this in the Russian State Register of Medicines (GRLS), which needed an image code on 19 October 2026. FDA has published no Selank evaluation that addresses this.

A Russian registration is a fact about one regulator. It does not make either peptide approved in the United States or the EU.

### United States: approval

FDA says neither Semax form "is a component of an FDA-approved drug" (FDA document). An openFDA query of Drugs@FDA for Selank and Semax on 19 October 2026 returned no applications for either.

### FDA compounding status

Under section 503A, a compounder may use a bulk substance only if it has a USP or NF monograph, is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or is on the 503A bulks list (21 CFR 216.23). FDA says there is no USP or NF monograph for Semax (FDA document). The status of each, as of 19 October 2026:

#### Semax

- **503A Category 2 from 29 September 2023.** FDA's safety-risk page, as archived on 3 October 2024, listed "Semax (heptapeptide)" in 503A Category 2 with that date, citing immunogenicity risk and limited safety information (FDA document).
- **Nominations withdrawn, FDA reviewed it anyway.** Both nominations, from Wells Pharmacy Network and LDT Health Solutions, were withdrawn (docket documents FDA-2015-N-3534-0484 and -0485). FDA "decided to evaluate both" Semax and Semax acetate "on its own initiative" (FDA document).
- **FDA staff proposal (memo dated 11 May 2026).** FDA concluded that "a balancing of the criteria weighs against" either form being placed on the 503A bulks list. It cited poor characterization, lack of evidence of effectiveness, safety concerns including immunogenicity, bleeding risk and unknown abuse potential, and no human safety data for injection (FDA document).
- **PCAC, 24 July 2026.** The committee voted separately on Semax free base and Semax acetate. It recommended adding Semax, one of six peptides recommended at the 23-24 July meeting, each by a narrow majority, according to a BMJ news report (news report). FDA has not posted minutes or a vote summary on the meeting page (content current as of 6 August 2026).
- **Where it sits now.** FDA's safety-risk page (content current as of 22 April 2026) lists Semax (heptapeptide) in its table of substances "nominated but withdrawn", with the same safety language. Semax is not in FDA's 503A category list (updated 14 May 2026) or 503B category list (updated 21 March 2025), and it is not in 21 CFR 216.23. No Federal Register rule adding it was found. A PCAC vote is advisory, so Semax is not on the 503A bulks list.

#### Selank

- **Safety-risk page.** FDA's safety-risk page (content current as of 22 April 2026) lists "Selank acetate (TP-7)" in its "Bulk drug substances nominated but withdrawn" table, which FDA introduces as substances "previously in category 2 of the interim policies" that "were withdrawn by the nominators". FDA gives no Category 2 date for Selank. FDA states that compounded drugs containing it "may pose risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration due to the potential for aggregation and peptide-related impurities" and that "FDA lacks important information regarding any safety issues raised by selank acetate administered to humans" (FDA document).
- **Earlier listing.** In the 3 October 2024 capture, Selank acetate appeared under "Other bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks", not in the dated Category 2 table. That page said a substance might not appear in Category 2 "because, for example, its nomination was withdrawn" (FDA document). We found no FDA record dating Selank's placement in, or removal from, Category 2.
- **Lists and meetings.** Selank is not in the 503A category list (14 May 2026), the 503B category list (21 March 2025) or 21 CFR 216.23. It was not on the July 2026 PCAC agenda, and no FDA briefing document on Selank was found.

#### What comes next

On 14 October 2026 the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs received an FDA item titled "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" (RIN 0910-ZD94, stage: Notice). The listing names no peptides and its text is not public, so whether it covers either compound is unknown. It would not make any peptide an FDA-approved drug.

A research-use-only label is not a compounding pathway or an approval, and a compounded drug is not FDA-approved. The [peptide legality guide](/guides/peptide-legality-fda-ruo-compounding) explains how these categories differ.

### European Union

EMA's medicines table (generated 18 October 2026), which covers medicines evaluated through the EU centralised procedure, contains no entry for Selank, Semax or either sequence. That table does not cover national authorisations in individual member states, which we did not check.

## FAQ

### Are Selank and Semax the same molecule?

No. Both are seven-amino-acid peptides ending in Pro-Gly-Pro, but their first four residues come from different parent molecules. Selank is Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro, built on tuftsin, an immune peptide. Semax is Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro, built on a fragment of ACTH. They have different PubChem records (CID 11765600 and CID 9811102) and different CAS numbers.

### Why are they so often listed together?

Both were developed at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences, both carry the same Pro-Gly-Pro tail, and both are described as registered medicines in Russia. FDA has also noted Semax products marketed in combination with Selank. Shared origin and marketing do not make them interchangeable.

### Is Selank or Semax approved anywhere?

Russia only, as far as we found. FDA's July 2026 briefing says Semax is a registered drug in Russia. Peer-reviewed reviews describe Selank as approved in Russia; we could not open the Russian register to confirm that entry on 19 October 2026. Neither appears in Drugs@FDA or in EMA's centralised medicines table.

### Where does Semax stand for compounding in the US?

Semax is not on the 503A bulks list as of 19 October 2026, and FDA says it has no USP or NF monograph and is not a component of an FDA-approved drug. FDA placed Semax in 503A Category 2 on 29 September 2023, and the nominations were later withdrawn. FDA staff proposed against listing it. On 24 July 2026 the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee recommended adding it by a narrow majority, but the vote is advisory and no final FDA action was found.

### Where does Selank stand with FDA?

FDA lists Selank acetate (TP-7) among substances that may present significant safety risks, citing possible immunogenicity and a lack of human safety information. It is not on the 503A bulks list or in FDA's 503A or 503B category lists, and it was not on the July 2026 advisory committee agenda.

### Has any study compared or combined Selank and Semax?

One small study compared them. In 52 healthy participants, it measured brain connectivity on MRI minutes after Semax, Selank or placebo, with no clinical outcome. Some animal and test-tube studies tested both in parallel. We found no study of the two given together, and no registered trial of either, in PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov searches on 19 October 2026.

### Which one is better?

This page does not rank them. They come from different parent molecules, were studied in different patient groups, mostly in small Russian-language studies, and have no head-to-head clinical trial. Neither has controlled human evidence for the memory, focus or mood claims common in marketing.

### Is a research-use-only vial the same as the Russian medicine?

No. The Russian products are registered medicines made under that country's rules. A research-use-only vial is neither a Russian-registered medicine nor a compounded or FDA-approved drug, and FDA found that none of the websites it searched said whether their Semax was the free base or a salt. None of the studies on this page describe the identity, purity or sterility of a research-labelled product.
