## Paste-ready body

## Field: overviewShort

**LL-37** is the C-terminal antimicrobial peptide derived from human cathelicidin (hCAP18), studied for antimicrobial, immunomodulatory, and wound-biology effects. It is **not FDA-approved** as a drug product. Limited topical clinical trials exist; injectable research-chemical use is a different, thinner evidence lane. Educational only.

## Field: overview

LL-37 is an endogenous host-defense peptide with cationic amphipathic structure that can disrupt microbial membranes and modulate innate immune signaling. Pharmaceutical interest includes topical wound applications. Peptidemap catalogs research materials under this slug; FDA compounding discussions have raised characterization/immunogenicity themes for related peptide bulks (verify live table).

## Field: background

Cathelicidin is cleaved to release LL-37 in human neutrophils and epithelial tissues. Synthetic LL-37 and fragments appear in wound-healing and anti-infective research. Marketplace vials should not be confused with approved antibiotics or biologics.

## Field: humanUseIntro

Human evidence for synthetic LL-37 is strongest in **topical wound** randomized settings (e.g., diabetic foot ulcer cream; venous leg ulcer programs)—not in systemic research-chemical injection culture. Results are indication- and formulation-specific.
