# CagriSema

## Field: overviewShort

**CagriSema** is Novo Nordisk's investigational fixed-dose combination of **cagrilintide**, a long-acting amylin analogue, and **semaglutide**, a GLP-1 receptor agonist. It is being studied for weight management and type 2 diabetes in the phase 3 REDEFINE and REIMAGINE programmes. As of 19 Oct 2026 it is **not approved** in the US or EU; Novo Nordisk submitted a New Drug Application to the FDA on 18 Dec 2025. Educational only. Not medical advice.

## Field: overview

CagriSema pairs two peptides that act on different hormone pathways. Cagrilintide mimics amylin, a pancreatic hormone involved in satiety and post-meal glucose control. Semaglutide mimics GLP-1. In the 68-week phase 3 REDEFINE 1 trial, adults with overweight or obesity assigned to CagriSema lost a mean 20.4% of body weight, compared with 3.0% on placebo (NEJM 2025). In REDEFINE 2, which enrolled people with type 2 diabetes, the figures were 13.7% and 3.4%. In REDEFINE 4, an 84-week open-label comparison with tirzepatide, CagriSema did not meet its primary endpoint of non-inferiority on weight loss (Novo Nordisk, 23 Feb 2026). Novo Nordisk submitted a New Drug Application (NDA) to the FDA on 18 Dec 2025, based on REDEFINE 1 and 2. No EU marketing application was found as of 19 Oct 2026. **It is not an approved medicine.** Peptidemap is a research-use comparison publisher, not a clinic, pharmacy, or supplier, and makes no supply, availability, vendor, or price claims. Any product sold online under this name, including vials labelled "research use only", is not Novo Nordisk trial supply.

## Field: background

Amylin is a 37-amino-acid hormone secreted with insulin by pancreatic beta cells (Pharmacol Rev 2015). Pramlintide, the first amylin analogue, has a short half-life (J Med Chem 2021). Novo Nordisk developed cagrilintide as a stable, lipidated, long-acting amylin analogue. It is a separate molecule with its own INN, registered by WHO in Proposed INN List 123 (2020). In a phase 1b trial, giving cagrilintide together with semaglutide was well tolerated, and the authors called for larger and longer trials (Lancet 2021). A phase 2 trial in type 2 diabetes followed (Lancet 2023), and then the phase 3 REDEFINE (weight management) and REIMAGINE (type 2 diabetes) programmes. "CagriSema" is Novo Nordisk's name for the combination. It is not an INN. The FDA Global Substance Registration System (GSRS) lists the two components separately and has no record for the combination.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Novo Nordisk describes cagrilintide's effect as additive to semaglutide (company statement, 23 Feb 2026). CagriSema combines activation of two receptor systems: cagrilintide acts on amylin receptors, and semaglutide acts on the GLP-1 receptor. Both pathways reduce food intake. No synergistic mechanism has been shown in people.

## Field: mechanismSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Cagrilintide (amylin pathway)
**items:**
- item: Amylin receptors — description: Amylin acts through receptors formed when a core receptor protein, the calcitonin receptor, is co-expressed with receptor activity-modifying proteins (RAMPs). This gives several receptor subtypes (Pharmacol Rev 2015).
- item: Brain sites — description: Amylin acts mainly in the circumventricular organs of the brain, which include the area postrema (Pharmacol Rev 2015).
- item: Long action — description: Cagrilintide is lipidated for long duration of action. Its human half-life was 159–195 hours in a phase 1b trial (J Med Chem 2021; Lancet 2021).

### subsection 2 — title: Semaglutide (GLP-1 pathway)
**items:**
- item: GLP-1 receptor agonist — description: Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist that is approved on its own in several countries. Its approvals do not extend to CagriSema.
- item: Half-life in the combination study — description: Semaglutide's half-life was 145–165 hours when given with cagrilintide in the phase 1b trial (Lancet 2021).

### subsection 3 — title: Combination
**items:**
- item: What the trials compared — description: REDEFINE 1 included cagrilintide-only and semaglutide-only groups, and REIMAGINE 2 compared CagriSema with each component. These designs test whether the combination adds to each component alone (NEJM 2025; Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2026).
- item: Novo Nordisk's description — description: Novo Nordisk describes CagriSema as targeting "complementary obesity-related pathways". That is the developer's framing, not an independent finding.

## Field: humanUseSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Early trials (2021–2023)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: **Phase 1b (peer-reviewed; Lancet 2021, PMID 33894838):** a randomised, placebo-controlled, multiple-ascending-dose trial of cagrilintide given with semaglutide. Most adverse events were mild to moderate. The human half-life was 159–195 hours for cagrilintide and 145–165 hours for semaglutide.
- type: content — value: **Phase 2 in type 2 diabetes (peer-reviewed; Lancet 2023, PMID 37364590):** 92 participants were randomised to CagriSema, semaglutide, or cagrilintide for 32 weeks. HbA1c fell more with CagriSema than with cagrilintide but not significantly more than with semaglutide. Body weight fell 15.6% with CagriSema, 5.1% with semaglutide, and 8.1% with cagrilintide.

### subsection 2 — title: REDEFINE programme (weight management)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: **REDEFINE 1 (peer-reviewed; NEJM 2025, PMID 40544433; NCT05567796):** 68-week phase 3 trial in 3,417 adults with overweight or obesity and without diabetes. Mean change in body weight: −20.4% with CagriSema vs −3.0% with placebo (estimated difference −17.3 percentage points; 95% CI −18.1 to −16.6; treatment-policy estimand). Gastrointestinal adverse events: 79.6% vs 39.9%, mainly transient and mild to moderate.
- type: content — value: **REDEFINE 2 (peer-reviewed; NEJM 2025, PMID 40544432; NCT05394519):** 68-week phase 3 trial in 1,206 adults with overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes. Mean change in body weight: −13.7% vs −3.4% with placebo (estimated difference −10.4 percentage points; 95% CI −11.2 to −9.5).
- type: content — value: **REDEFINE 4 vs tirzepatide (company announcement No 13/2026, 23 Feb 2026; NCT06131437):** 84-week open-label phase 3 trial in 809 adults with obesity. Novo Nordisk reported: "The trial did not achieve its primary endpoint of demonstrating non-inferiority on weight loss for CagriSema compared to tirzepatide after 84 weeks." Mean weight loss was 23.0% with CagriSema vs 25.5% with tirzepatide if all participants adhered to treatment, and 20.2% vs 23.6% with the treatment-regimen estimand. Not yet peer-reviewed.
- type: content — value: **REDEFINE 5, East Asia (peer-reviewed; Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2026, PMID 42009015; NCT05813925):** 68-week trial in 331 adults in Japan and Taiwan, against semaglutide alone. Mean change in body weight: −18.4% vs −11.9% (estimated difference −6.5 percentage points; 95% CI −8.4 to −4.6).
- type: content — value: **REDEFINE 9 (company report, 4 Aug 2026):** Novo Nordisk reported that this 68-week trial of lower-dose combinations "achieved a superior weight loss compared to placebo". Detailed results have not been published.
- type: content — value: **Ongoing:** REDEFINE 3, a cardiovascular outcomes trial with 7,076 participants randomised (design paper: Am Heart J 2026, PMID 42838477; NCT05669755), is listed as active, not recruiting; Novo Nordisk expects a readout in H2 2027. REDEFINE 11 is expected to report in H1 2027. A higher-dose CagriSema phase 3 trial started in 2026.

### subsection 3 — title: REIMAGINE programme (type 2 diabetes)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: **REIMAGINE 2 (peer-reviewed; Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2026, PMID 42251859; NCT06065540):** 2,713 people with type 2 diabetes were randomised. The trial compared CagriSema with semaglutide, cagrilintide, and placebo. The HbA1c change at 68 weeks was −1.91 percentage points with CagriSema vs −1.75 with semaglutide (efficacy estimand), an estimated difference of −0.16 (95% CI −0.27 to −0.05).
- type: content — value: **REIMAGINE 1 and 3 (peer-reviewed; Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2026, PMID 42251860; Lancet 2026, PMID 42251856):** phase 3 trials in people with type 2 diabetes managed with diet and exercise (REIMAGINE 1) and on basal insulin (REIMAGINE 3).
- type: content — value: **REIMAGINE 4 vs tirzepatide (company report, 4 Aug 2026):** Novo Nordisk reported that CagriSema "demonstrated non-inferiority versus tirzepatide … for weight reduction, but not for HbA1c reduction".
- type: item — value: Trial results are not approvals. Long-term cardiovascular outcomes are not yet reported.

## Field: conclusion

CagriSema is an investigational combination of an amylin analogue and a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Two peer-reviewed phase 3 trials, REDEFINE 1 and 2, reported weight loss well beyond placebo. A company-reported head-to-head trial did not show non-inferiority to tirzepatide. Novo Nordisk submitted an NDA on 18 Dec 2025 and expects an FDA decision by late 2026; CagriSema is not approved (as of 19 Oct 2026). ## FAQs

**What is CagriSema?** CagriSema is Novo Nordisk's investigational fixed-dose combination of cagrilintide, a long-acting amylin analogue, and semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist. It is being studied for weight management and type 2 diabetes.

**Is CagriSema approved?** No. As of 19 Oct 2026 CagriSema is not approved in the US or EU. Novo Nordisk submitted a New Drug Application to the FDA on 18 Dec 2025 and says a decision is anticipated by late 2026. Optum Rx lists an expected decision date of 18 December 2026, which FDA and Novo Nordisk have not published.

**How much weight did people lose in REDEFINE 1?** In REDEFINE 1, a 68-week phase 3 trial in 3,417 adults with overweight or obesity, mean body weight changed by −20.4% with CagriSema and −3.0% with placebo (NEJM 2025).

**Did CagriSema match tirzepatide?** No. Novo Nordisk reported on 23 Feb 2026 that REDEFINE 4 did not achieve its primary endpoint of demonstrating non-inferiority on weight loss for CagriSema compared to tirzepatide after 84 weeks.

**Does CagriSema have a CAS number?** No CAS number or UNII for the combination was found. FDA's substance registry lists cagrilintide (CAS 1415456-99-3) and semaglutide (CAS 910463-68-2) as separate substances.

**Are research vials sold as CagriSema the trial drug?** No. Any product sold online under this name, including vials labelled "research use only", is not Novo Nordisk trial supply. Its identity, purity, content, and sterility are unverified, and Peptidemap makes no supply, vendor, or price claims.
