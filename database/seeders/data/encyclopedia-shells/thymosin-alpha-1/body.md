## Paste-ready body

## Field: overviewShort

**Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1)**, synthetic form **thymalfasin**, is a 28-amino-acid N-terminally acetylated immunomodulatory peptide derived from prothymosin α. It is used in multiple countries outside a broad U.S. NDA approval pathway; **FDA has not approved a thymosin alpha-1 drug product** for general U.S. marketing. It is **not the same molecule as thymosin β4 / TB-500**. Educational only—not medical advice.

## Field: overview

Tα1 has a long clinical research history in viral hepatitis, oncology-adjuvant immunology, vaccine response, and critical illness settings, with large narrative reviews summarizing thousands of human exposures across heterogeneous trials. U.S. readers must separate (1) international thymalfasin products (e.g., historical Zadaxin branding in some markets), (2) orphan-drug designations (designation ≠ approval), and (3) research-chemical / compounding commerce subject to FDA bulk-substance and immunogenicity concerns.

## Field: background

Originally characterized from thymic peptide research, Tα1 became a synthetic pharmaceutical peptide (thymalfasin) with approvals or registrations in various jurisdictions for hepatitis and immune-enhancement indications. It is frequently confused with **thymosin β4 / TB-500**—a different molecule with different WADA and FDA narratives.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Tα1 is described as an immune modulator acting in part through innate immune receptor pathways (including TLR-related signaling narratives) and effects on T-cell maturation/cytokine balance—mechanism reviews should be read as research summaries, not dosing guides.
