### overviewShort

BPC-157 / TB-500 is a **marketplace blend** (often nicknamed a “Wolverine” stack) that co-packages two chemically unrelated research peptides: the synthetic pentadecapeptide BPC-157 and a product sold as TB-500 (usually a thymosin β4 fragment, not full-length thymosin β4). The combination is widely sold; **published human studies of the pair together are lacking**. Educational only—not medical advice.

### overview

On Peptidemap and across research-peptide vendors, **BPC-157 / TB-500** names a co-formulated or co-listed product category rather than a single approved drug. BPC-157 is a 15-amino-acid synthetic peptide from the gastric “body protection compound” research line. TB-500 is a grey-market name most often used for a short synthetic fragment of thymosin beta-4 (commonly the actin-binding motif LKKTETQ), which is **not** the same molecule as the full 43-amino-acid endogenous protein studied in some ophthalmic programs.

Vendors market the blend for overlapping tissue-repair narratives. That marketing outruns the evidence: each component’s literature is largely preclinical, and **no peer-reviewed human interventional program was identified that prospectively tests co-administration of BPC-157 and TB-500 for musculoskeletal efficacy or combination safety**. Mechanistic “complementarity” is a hypothesis, not a validated clinical claim.

### background

Online recovery communities popularized pairing BPC-157 with TB-500 (and pre-mixed vials) under informal stack names. Peptidemap’s catalog treats the pair as its own encyclopedia/compare category with substantial vendor presence, while the educational body for this slug has historically been empty—hence this draft.

Important naming traps:

- **TB-500 ≠ full-length thymosin β4.** Borrowing human eye-drop trial credibility for injectable fragment products is a common category error.
- **Blend ratios and salt forms vary by vendor.** Always read the specific lot label/COA; encyclopedia identity here is “two-component marketplace class,” not a pharmacopeial standard.
- **RUO labeling** does not create FDA approval or compounding permission.

### preclinicalDisclaimer

Almost all musculoskeletal outcome literature for these molecules is **animal or cell-based**. Positive rodent findings do not guarantee human benefit. Combination (blend) preclinical packages are also not a substitute for human combination trials.

### preclinicalIntro

Component-level preclinical work is relatively extensive for BPC-157 (tendon, muscle, ligament, GI models in rodents) and substantial for thymosin β4 biology broadly. Direct musculoskeletal interventional data for the **TB-500 fragment**, and for **BPC-157+TB-500 together**, are far thinner than marketplace narratives imply.

### humanUseIntro

Human evidence for **injectable musculoskeletal use** is limited for each component and essentially absent for the **blend as a product**. Small uncontrolled BPC-157 clinic reports and unrelated topical full-length Tβ4 programs must not be conflated with proof that a dual vial “works.”

### keyPoints

- BPC-157 / TB-500 is a **vendor blend category**, not an FDA-approved drug or a single CAS substance.
- Components are chemically unrelated; TB-500 usually means a **thymosin β4 fragment**, not full-length Tβ4.
- Evidence for each component is mainly **preclinical**; human injectable MSK evidence is limited/absent.
- **No published human blend study** establishing co-administration safety or efficacy was identified.
- **PCAC recommendations ≠ compounding permission**; Category 2 removal ≠ Bulks List listing.
- WADA prohibits BPC-157 (S0) and thymosin-β4 derivatives including TB-500 (S2.3) at all times—sport rules, separate from FDA approval.

### conclusion

BPC-157 / TB-500 earns encyclopedia space because it is a high-intent marketplace pairing with large vendor presence—not because the combination has been clinically validated. Readers should evaluate each peptide’s literature separately, refuse to equate animal data with human outcomes, and treat stacking claims as untested until combination trials exist. Regulatory and anti-doping constraints further separate “sold online” from “approved” or “compoundable.” This page is educational and is not medical advice.

### regulatoryImportantNote

“Removed from Category 2” or a favorable **PCAC vote** does not place a substance on the final **503A Bulks List** and does not authorize compounding. Verify current FDA compounding pages before any pharmacy-pathway claim.

### regulatorySubsections

### subsection 1 — title: FDA approval and compounding literacy
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Neither BPC-157 nor TB-500 is an FDA-approved drug for injury recovery. FDA safety-risk materials discuss BPC-157 and thymosin beta-4 fragment (LKKTETQ / TB-500) in compounding bulk-substance contexts (immunogenicity/impurity/characterization concerns; limited human data for proposed routes / no identified human exposure data for the fragment in FDA’s TB-500 summary).
- type: content — value: July 2026 PCAC reporting recommended 503A Bulks List inclusion for several peptides including BPC-157 and TB-500; recommendations are advisory. Educational trackers as of late summer 2026 stated these had not been added by final rulemaking.

### subsection 2 — title: Sport / workplace (not general legality)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: WADA 2026 Prohibited List names BPC-157 under S0 and thymosin-β4 and derivatives e.g. TB-500 under S2.3 (prohibited at all times).
- type: content — value: DoD OPSS describes BPC-157 as an unapproved drug and prohibited dietary-supplement ingredient for service members.
