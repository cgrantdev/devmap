### overviewShort

**Melanotan II (MT-II)** is a synthetic cyclic analog of α-MSH that acts as a **nonselective melanocortin receptor agonist**. It has been investigated historically for tanning and sexual-arousal pharmacology. It is **not FDA-approved**, and regulators have warned consumers about unapproved “tanning injections.” Educational only—not medical advice.

### overview

MT-II stimulates melanogenesis via MC1R pathways but also engages other melanocortin receptors (including pathways linked to sexual function and autonomic effects), which explains both research interest and adverse-effect breadth. A related peptide, **afamelanotide (Melanotan I analog lineage)**, has a distinct approved-product story in photomedicine in some jurisdictions; **MT-II itself remains an unapproved research chemical** in the U.S. marketplace context.

### background

Developed in academic melanocortin programs in the late 20th century, MT-II entered grey-market tanning commerce despite lacking approval. FDA warning letters and consumer notices date back many years. Peptidemap lists vendor research products under this slug; encyclopedia content must foreground safety case reports and regulatory status—not how-to use guidance.

### humanUseIntro

Small experimental human studies historically reported tanning and erectile effects alongside **nausea, flushing, stretching/yawning**, and other acute effects. Contemporary safety literature emphasizes **case reports of serious harm** associated with unregulated use. FDA safety-risk compounding materials cite published adverse events including melanoma concerns, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome, sympathomimetic toxidrome, and priapism.

### regulatoryImportantNote

Not FDA-approved. Unapproved “tanning injections” have drawn FDA consumer warnings and enforcement. Compounding significant-safety-risk materials cite serious published adverse-event case reports—see references. FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meeting page (read 3 October 2026) lists melanotan II among bulk drug substances scheduled for discussion before the end of February 2027, for possible inclusion on the 503A Bulks List. A scheduled discussion, a PCAC recommendation, or a Category 2 list change is **not** FDA drug approval and **not** final 503A Bulks List permission to compound.
