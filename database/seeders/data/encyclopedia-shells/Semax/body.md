## Field: overviewShort

**Semax** is a synthetic heptapeptide analog of ACTH(4-7) with a Pro-Gly-Pro C-terminal extension (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro). It is registered for certain neurologic indications in Russia; it is **not FDA-approved** in the United States. Educational only—not medical advice.

## Field: overview

Developed within Russian regulatory-peptide programs, Semax is studied and used clinically in that jurisdiction for cerebrovascular and neuroprotective contexts (including ischemic stroke rehabilitation research). U.S. research-chemical sales and “nootropic” marketing outrun the availability of large, independent Western RCTs. Peptidemap lists vendor research products under this slug.

## Field: background

Semax derives from ACTH fragment structure–activity work aimed at retaining neurotropic effects while reducing classical ACTH endocrine activity. Related Russian peptides include Selank (different parent motif). Naming in commerce is usually “Semax”; salt forms and concentrations on nasal/research products vary by vendor.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Leading research themes include neurotrophin expression (BDNF/NGF pathways), transcriptome shifts in ischemic brain models, and neuroprotective signaling—not a simple single-receptor consumer story.

### subsection 2 — title: U.S. compounding policy (as of 29 October 2026)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Semax is not an FDA-approved drug and is not on the 503A Bulks List (21 CFR 216.23(a)). Semax was in FDA’s interim Category 2 from 29 September 2023 until 22 April 2026. FDA’s categories list (updated 14 May 2026) does not place it in Category 1, 2 or 3.
- type: content — value: Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (24 July 2026). FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) considered Semax (free base) and Semax acetate for the 503A Bulks List on 24 July 2026. In its briefing document, FDA proposed that neither form be included. FDA has not posted minutes or vote results on the meeting page, and this page gives no vote counts. The HHS policy’s Appendix B states that “CDER recommended against listing either form, and the PCAC voted in favor of both forms.” Advisory committee recommendations are non-binding.
- type: content — value: Draft interim policy published for comment (announced 28 October 2026). The HHS Office of the Secretary filed a Federal Register notice of availability (FR Doc. 2026-22192; Docket No. HHS-OASH-2026-0496; RIN 0910-ZD94) for a draft “Enforcement Discretion Policy Regarding 503A Compounding Using Certain Peptide Bulk Drug Substances”. The notice is scheduled for publication on 30 October 2026, and it states that written comments can be submitted “until 2 weeks after date of publication in the Federal Register”. OIRA concluded its review on 27 October 2026. Semax is one of six peptides in the policy’s Appendix A. The policy states that, until a peptide “is addressed in a final rule as being included or not included on the 503A bulks list,” HHS “does not intend to take action against a State licensed pharmacy, Federal facility, or licensed physician” compounding with it “when all of the following circumstances are present”.
- type: item — value: Scope: the identity listed in Appendix B is semax free base (H-Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro-OH), for intranasal use only. Semax acetate is not the listed identity, and no injectable route is listed. The compounded product may contain only a single active ingredient. This describes the policy’s scope. It is not usage guidance.
- type: item — value: Conditions (summary): the bulk substance must come from a facility registered under section 510 with a valid certificate of analysis (COA) whose test results meet the testing standard in Appendix B; the original manufacturer must be FDA-registered and CGMP-compliant, with its location on the certificate; compounders must report products compounded, serious adverse events and product quality issues to FDA and to the States, and serious adverse events and product quality issues to FDA’s MedWatch program; the label must state “This is a compounded drug product. Not for resale.”; the only co-packaged device may be an FDA 510(k)-cleared syringe; and section 503A’s insanitary-conditions and patient-specific prescription requirements still apply.
- type: item — value: Not an approval: the notice states that “Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, which means FDA does not review their safety, effectiveness, or quality before they are marketed.” The policy makes no safety or efficacy finding for Semax.
- type: item — value: Not a Bulks List listing and not Category 1: Semax is not on the 503A Bulks List (21 CFR 216.23(a)) and is not in any FDA interim category. The notice says FDA’s future rulemakings “may propose adding some or all of these peptides” to the list; no such proposed rule has been published.
- type: item — value: Interim and non-binding: there is no calendar end date. The policy applies until a final rule addresses each peptide, HHS “may revisit” it “if, among other things, a safety signal or product quality issue arises”, and the notice says it “is not binding on HHS or the public.”
- type: item — value: Research-use-only products: HHS describes the policy as meant to “discourage the continued, widespread use of certain research-use-only peptides supplied by foreign manufacturers that pose a safety risk to US patients.” Nothing in the policy treats research-use-only products as covered.
