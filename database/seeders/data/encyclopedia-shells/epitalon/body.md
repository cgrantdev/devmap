### overviewShort

**Epitalon (Epithalon)** is the synthetic tetrapeptide **Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG)**, developed in pineal bioregulator research associated with the Khavinson group. It is studied primarily for **telomerase/telomere** effects in cell systems. It is **not FDA-approved**. Independent human longevity RCTs are lacking. Educational only.

### overview

Epitalon is marketed in research-peptide catalogs as a “longevity” peptide. The strongest reproducible scientific signals to date are **in vitro**: induction of telomerase-related activity and telomere length changes in human cell lines, including a 2025 independent cell-line study. Extrapolating dish results to human lifespan claims is not justified by current controlled clinical evidence.

### background

Synthetic AEDG was designed in relation to **epithalamin**, a pineal extract studied in Russian gerontology programs. Naming variants (Epitalon/Epithalon/Epithalone) appear in literature and commerce. Much older clinical/gerontology reporting traces to a concentrated research network; readers should weigh independence and study design carefully.

### mechanismOfActionIntro

Leading hypothesis: effects on telomerase expression/activity and telomere maintenance pathways in somatic cells—with cell-type nuances (including ALT pathway observations in some cancer lines in recent work).

### regulatoryImportantNote

Regulatory literacy (as of 29 October 2026): Epitalon is not FDA-approved and is not on the 503A Bulks List. Removal from FDA Category 2 is not Category 1, and a favorable Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee vote is advisory only. The draft interim enforcement-discretion policy that the HHS Office of the Secretary published for comment in October 2026 is not an approval or a Bulks List listing, is not binding, and makes no safety or efficacy finding. See the FDA / compounding section above. This page does not claim WADA naming of Epitalon; athletes should still clear any peptide with their ADO.

### regulatorySubsections

### subsection 1 — title: FDA / compounding (as of 29 October 2026)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Not FDA-approved, and not on the 503A Bulks List (21 CFR 216.23(a)). Epitalon here means the tetrapeptide Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG). Epitalon was in FDA’s interim Category 2 from 29 September 2023 until 22 April 2026. FDA’s categories list (updated 14 May 2026) does not place it in Category 1, 2 or 3.
- type: content — value: Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (24 July 2026). FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) considered Epitalon (free base) and Epitalon acetate for the 503A Bulks List on 24 July 2026. In its briefing document, FDA proposed that neither form be included. FDA has not posted minutes or vote results on the meeting page, and this page gives no vote counts. The HHS policy’s Appendix B states that “CDER recommended against listing either form, and the PCAC voted in favor of inclusion.” Advisory committee recommendations are non-binding.
- type: content — value: Draft interim policy published for comment (announced 28 October 2026). The HHS Office of the Secretary filed a Federal Register notice of availability (FR Doc. 2026-22192; Docket No. HHS-OASH-2026-0496; RIN 0910-ZD94) for a draft “Enforcement Discretion Policy Regarding 503A Compounding Using Certain Peptide Bulk Drug Substances”. The notice is scheduled for publication on 30 October 2026, and it states that written comments can be submitted “until 2 weeks after date of publication in the Federal Register”. OIRA concluded its review on 27 October 2026. Epitalon is one of six peptides in the policy’s Appendix A. The policy states that, until a peptide “is addressed in a final rule as being included or not included on the 503A bulks list,” HHS “does not intend to take action against a State licensed pharmacy, Federal facility, or licensed physician” compounding with it “when all of the following circumstances are present”.
- type: item — value: Scope: the identity listed in Appendix B is epitalon acetate (H-Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly-OH·CH3COOH), for subcutaneous or intramuscular injectable dosage forms “dispensed in intermittent courses”. The compounded product may contain only a single active ingredient. This describes the policy’s scope. It is not usage guidance.
- type: item — value: Conditions (summary): the bulk substance must come from a facility registered under section 510 with a valid certificate of analysis (COA) whose test results meet the testing standard in Appendix B; the original manufacturer must be FDA-registered and CGMP-compliant, with its location on the certificate; compounders must report products compounded, serious adverse events and product quality issues to FDA and to the States, and serious adverse events and product quality issues to FDA’s MedWatch program; the label must state “This is a compounded drug product. Not for resale.”; the only co-packaged device may be an FDA 510(k)-cleared syringe; and section 503A’s insanitary-conditions and patient-specific prescription requirements still apply.
- type: item — value: Not an approval: the notice states that “Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, which means FDA does not review their safety, effectiveness, or quality before they are marketed.” The policy makes no safety or efficacy finding for Epitalon.
- type: item — value: Not a Bulks List listing and not Category 1: Epitalon is not on the 503A Bulks List (21 CFR 216.23(a)) and is not in any FDA interim category. The notice says FDA’s future rulemakings “may propose adding some or all of these peptides” to the list; no such proposed rule has been published.
- type: item — value: Interim and non-binding: there is no calendar end date. The policy applies until a final rule addresses each peptide, HHS “may revisit” it “if, among other things, a safety signal or product quality issue arises”, and the notice says it “is not binding on HHS or the public.”
- type: item — value: Research-use-only products: HHS describes the policy as meant to “discourage the continued, widespread use of certain research-use-only peptides supplied by foreign manufacturers that pose a safety risk to US patients.” Nothing in the policy treats research-use-only products as covered.
