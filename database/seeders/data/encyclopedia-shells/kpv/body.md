## Field: overviewShort

**KPV** is the tripeptide **lysine–proline–valine**, corresponding to α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone residues 11–13. It is studied mainly for **anti-inflammatory** activity in cell and animal models, with particular interest in intestinal inflammation and PepT1-mediated uptake. It is **not FDA-approved** as a drug. Educational only.

## Field: overview

Unlike many long research peptides, KPV is only three amino acids, which makes synthesis and transport biology distinctive. Research interest grew because fragments of α-MSH can retain anti-inflammatory effects while showing pharmacology that differs from full-length melanocortin receptor agonists used for pigmentation. Marketplace vials are sold as research materials; human clinical development remains limited relative to preclinical narratives.

## Field: background

α-MSH is cleaved from POMC and acts through melanocortin receptors (notably MC1R for pigmentation). Work since the late 1980s–2000s dissected which portions of α-MSH carry anti-inflammatory activity. KPV emerged as a minimal C-terminal motif with anti-inflammatory readouts in multiple experimental systems. It also appears as the fourth component in marketplace **KLOW** blends.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Proposed mechanisms are preclinical. KPV’s anti-inflammatory profile is often described as **not simply classical melanocortin agonism**.

## Field: mechanismSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Inflammatory signaling
**intro:** Cell studies report reduced NF-κB / MAPK pathway activation and lower pro-inflammatory cytokine output under KPV exposure in stimulated epithelial and immune cell models.
**items:**
- item: NF-κB pathway damping — description: Reporter and biochemical assays show inhibited inflammatory transcription programs in vitro
- item: Cytokine reduction — description: Lower secretion of selected pro-inflammatory mediators in stimulated cultures

### subsection 2 — title: Transport biology (PepT1)
**intro:** The intestinal di/tripeptide transporter PepT1 can carry KPV into epithelial cells.
**items:**
- item: PepT1-mediated uptake — description: Competition/uptake experiments link KPV entry to PepT1 in intestinal epithelial models
- item: IBD expression context — description: PepT1 induction in inflamed colon is part of the translational rationale in mouse colitis papers—not proof of human therapy

### subsection 3 — title: Melanocortin receptor relationship
**intro:** Comparative pharmacology papers argue KPV’s anti-inflammatory effects differ from core MSH peptides that engage MC3/MC4-type signaling.
**items:**
- item: Limited pigmentation pathway agonism narrative — description: Positioned as anti-inflammatory with reduced pigmentary signaling relative to full α-MSH—still preclinical framing
- item: Distinct from MT-II class — description: Not the same pharmacology as nonselective melanotan research chemicals

## Field: preclinicalDisclaimer

Mouse colitis and cell assays generate hypotheses. They do **not** establish safety or efficacy of research-chemical KPV in humans.

## Field: preclinicalIntro

Key experimental themes include crystal-induced peritonitis comparisons, IL-1–driven inflammation models, and DSS/TNBS colitis with oral KPV in drinking water in mice.

## Field: preclinicalSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Intestinal inflammation models
**findings:**
- title: DSS/TNBS colitis — content: Dalmasso et al. reported that KPV reduced inflammatory signaling and improved colitis metrics in mice, with PepT1-dependent uptake contributing to epithelial delivery.
- title: Stability caveat — content: Later transport/stability work notes enzymatic instability concerns for oral peptide delivery—relevant to translating rodent oral findings.

### subsection 2 — title: Broader inflammation models
**findings:**
- title: Peritonitis / leukocyte migration — content: Getting et al. (2003) compared KPV with core melanocortin peptides; KPV reduced PMN accumulation with pharmacology inconsistent with simple MC3/4 agonism.

## Field: humanUseIntro

Registered, robust human efficacy programs for KPV as a pharmacy drug were not identified. FDA compounding materials have listed KPV among substances lacking identified human exposure data in the agency’s summary narrative (verify live FDA page).

## FAQs

**Is KPV a tanning peptide?** No—research focus is anti-inflammatory fragment biology, not MT-II-style tanning.

**Is it approved for IBD?** No.

**Why is it in KLOW?** Commercial formulation choice adding an anti-inflammatory tripeptide to a GLOW-like trio—not clinical proof.
