## Paste-ready body


## Field: overviewShort

**KPV** is the tripeptide **lysine–proline–valine**, corresponding to α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone residues 11–13. It is studied mainly for **anti-inflammatory** activity in cell and animal models, with particular interest in intestinal inflammation and PepT1-mediated uptake. It is **not FDA-approved** as a drug. Educational only.

## Field: overview

Unlike many long research peptides, KPV is only three amino acids, which makes synthesis and transport biology distinctive. Research interest grew because fragments of α-MSH can retain anti-inflammatory effects while showing pharmacology that differs from full-length melanocortin receptor agonists used for pigmentation. Marketplace vials are sold as research materials; human clinical development remains limited relative to preclinical narratives.

## Field: background

α-MSH is cleaved from POMC and acts through melanocortin receptors (notably MC1R for pigmentation). Work since the late 1980s–2000s dissected which portions of α-MSH carry anti-inflammatory activity. KPV emerged as a minimal C-terminal motif with anti-inflammatory readouts in multiple experimental systems. It also appears as the fourth component in marketplace **KLOW** blends.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Proposed mechanisms are preclinical. KPV’s anti-inflammatory profile is often described as **not simply classical melanocortin agonism**.

## Field: mechanismSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Inflammatory signaling
**intro:** Cell studies report reduced NF-κB / MAPK pathway activation and lower pro-inflammatory cytokine output under KPV exposure in stimulated epithelial and immune cell models.
**items:**
- item: NF-κB pathway damping — description: Reporter and biochemical assays show inhibited inflammatory transcription programs in vitro
- item: Cytokine reduction — description: Lower secretion of selected pro-inflammatory mediators in stimulated cultures

### subsection 2 — title: Transport biology (PepT1)
**intro:** The intestinal di/tripeptide transporter PepT1 can carry KPV into epithelial cells.
**items:**
- item: PepT1-mediated uptake — description: Competition/uptake experiments link KPV entry to PepT1 in intestinal epithelial models
- item: IBD expression context — description: PepT1 induction in inflamed colon is part of the translational rationale in mouse colitis papers—not proof of human therapy

### subsection 3 — title: Melanocortin receptor relationship
**intro:** Comparative pharmacology papers argue KPV’s anti-inflammatory effects differ from core MSH peptides that engage MC3/MC4-type signaling.
**items:**
- item: Limited pigmentation pathway agonism narrative — description: Positioned as anti-inflammatory with reduced pigmentary signaling relative to full α-MSH—still preclinical framing
- item: Distinct from MT-II class — description: Not the same pharmacology as nonselective melanotan research chemicals

## Field: preclinicalDisclaimer

Mouse colitis and cell assays generate hypotheses. They do **not** establish safety or efficacy of research-chemical KPV in humans.

## Field: preclinicalIntro

Key experimental themes include crystal-induced peritonitis comparisons, IL-1–driven inflammation models, and DSS/TNBS colitis with oral KPV in drinking water in mice.

## Field: preclinicalSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Intestinal inflammation models
**findings:**
- title: DSS/TNBS colitis — content: Dalmasso et al. reported that KPV reduced inflammatory signaling and improved colitis metrics in mice, with PepT1-dependent uptake contributing to epithelial delivery.
- title: Stability caveat — content: Later transport/stability work notes enzymatic instability concerns for oral peptide delivery—relevant to translating rodent oral findings.

### subsection 2 — title: Broader inflammation models
**findings:**
- title: Peritonitis / leukocyte migration — content: Getting et al. (2003) compared KPV with core melanocortin peptides; KPV reduced PMN accumulation with pharmacology inconsistent with simple MC3/4 agonism.

## Field: humanUseIntro

Registered, robust human efficacy programs for KPV as a pharmacy drug were not identified. FDA compounding materials have listed KPV among substances lacking identified human exposure data in the agency’s summary narrative (verify live FDA page).

## Field: regulatoryImportantNote

Regulatory literacy (as of 29 October 2026): KPV is not FDA-approved and is not on the 503A Bulks List. Removal from FDA Category 2 is not Category 1, and a favorable Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee vote is advisory only. The draft interim enforcement-discretion policy that the HHS Office of the Secretary published for comment in October 2026 is not an approval or a Bulks List listing, is not binding, and makes no safety or efficacy finding. See the FDA / compounding section above.

## Field: regulatorySubsections

### subsection 1 — title: FDA / compounding (as of 29 October 2026)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Not an FDA-approved drug, and not on the 503A Bulks List (21 CFR 216.23(a)). KPV here means the tripeptide Lys-Pro-Val (CAS 67727-97-3). KPV was in FDA’s interim Category 2 from September 2023 until 22 April 2026. FDA’s categories list (updated 14 May 2026) does not place it in Category 1, 2 or 3.
- type: content — value: Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (23 July 2026). FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) considered KPV (free base) and KPV acetate for the 503A Bulks List on 23 July 2026. In its briefing document, FDA proposed that neither form be included. FDA has not posted minutes or vote results on the meeting page, and this page gives no vote counts. The HHS policy’s Appendix B states that “CDER recommended against listing either form, and the PCAC voted in favor of inclusion.” Advisory committee recommendations are non-binding.
- type: content — value: Draft interim policy published for comment (announced 28 October 2026). The HHS Office of the Secretary filed a Federal Register notice of availability (FR Doc. 2026-22192; Docket No. HHS-OASH-2026-0496; RIN 0910-ZD94) for a draft “Enforcement Discretion Policy Regarding 503A Compounding Using Certain Peptide Bulk Drug Substances”. The notice is scheduled for publication on 30 October 2026, and it states that written comments can be submitted “until 2 weeks after date of publication in the Federal Register”. OIRA concluded its review on 27 October 2026. KPV is one of six peptides in the policy’s Appendix A. The policy states that, until a peptide “is addressed in a final rule as being included or not included on the 503A bulks list,” HHS “does not intend to take action against a State licensed pharmacy, Federal facility, or licensed physician” compounding with it “when all of the following circumstances are present”.
- type: item — value: Scope: the identity listed in Appendix B is KPV acetate only, for “topical and oral dosage forms”. No injectable route is listed. For oral forms the compounder is expected to keep documentation of adequate oral bioavailability in humans. The compounded product may contain only a single active ingredient, so blends such as KLOW are outside the policy. This describes the policy’s scope. It is not usage guidance.
- type: item — value: Conditions (summary): the bulk substance must come from a facility registered under section 510 with a valid certificate of analysis (COA) whose test results meet the testing standard in Appendix B; the original manufacturer must be FDA-registered and CGMP-compliant, with its location on the certificate; compounders must report products compounded, serious adverse events and product quality issues to FDA and to the States, and serious adverse events and product quality issues to FDA’s MedWatch program; the label must state “This is a compounded drug product. Not for resale.”; the only co-packaged device may be an FDA 510(k)-cleared syringe; and section 503A’s insanitary-conditions and patient-specific prescription requirements still apply.
- type: item — value: Not an approval: the notice states that “Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, which means FDA does not review their safety, effectiveness, or quality before they are marketed.” The policy makes no safety or efficacy finding for KPV.
- type: item — value: Not a Bulks List listing and not Category 1: KPV is not on the 503A Bulks List (21 CFR 216.23(a)) and is not in any FDA interim category. The notice says FDA’s future rulemakings “may propose adding some or all of these peptides” to the list; no such proposed rule has been published.
- type: item — value: Interim and non-binding: there is no calendar end date. The policy applies until a final rule addresses each peptide, HHS “may revisit” it “if, among other things, a safety signal or product quality issue arises”, and the notice says it “is not binding on HHS or the public.”
- type: item — value: Research-use-only products: HHS describes the policy as meant to “discourage the continued, widespread use of certain research-use-only peptides supplied by foreign manufacturers that pose a safety risk to US patients.” Nothing in the policy treats research-use-only products as covered.

## FAQs

**Is KPV a tanning peptide?** No—research focus is anti-inflammatory fragment biology, not MT-II-style tanning.

**Is it approved for IBD?** No.

**Why is it in KLOW?** Commercial formulation choice adding an anti-inflammatory tripeptide to a GLOW-like trio—not clinical proof.
