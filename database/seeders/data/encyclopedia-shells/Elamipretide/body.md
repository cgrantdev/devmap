## Field: overviewShort

**Elamipretide** (also known as **SS-31**, MTP-131) is a mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide researched for cardiolipin/inner-membrane biology. In September 2025 the FDA granted **accelerated approval** to **Forzinity (elamipretide)** injection as the first treatment for **Barth syndrome** in patients weighing at least 30 kg. Research-marketplace vials are **not** the approved Forzinity product. Educational only—not medical advice.

## Field: overview

Elamipretide is a cell-permeable aromatic-cationic tetrapeptide designed to associate with the inner mitochondrial membrane. Preclinical and clinical programs (Stealth BioTherapeutics and collaborators) explored mitochondrial myopathies and cardiometabolic settings; the first U.S. marketing authorization is indication-specific for Barth syndrome under accelerated approval, with confirmatory trial obligations. Peptidemap catalogs research-chemical listings under this encyclopedia slug—those commercial RUO materials must not be equated with FDA-approved Forzinity.

## Field: background

SS-31 emerged from Szeto–Schiller peptide chemistry aimed at mitochondrial targeting. Synonyms in literature include elamipretide, SS-31, MTP-131, and historical Bendavia branding in older trials. Barth syndrome (TAZ-related cardiolipin remodeling disorder) became the lead approved rare-disease path after multi-year clinical development, including the TAZPOWER program and long-term extension analyses.

## Field: humanUseSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Barth syndrome program behind the Forzinity approval
**entries:**
- type: content — value: FDA’s Drug Trials Snapshot names the key trial as SPIBA-201: a small randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study in 12 patients with genetically confirmed Barth syndrome, followed by a single-arm, open-label extension.
- type: content — value: FDA reports that Forzinity was not superior to placebo on the randomized period’s primary endpoints: 6-minute walk distance and a Barth syndrome fatigue score. Knee-extensor strength was a secondary endpoint. FDA reports that strength increases were not observed during the randomized period but were observed during the open-label extension. That is why the approval is accelerated and tied to a confirmatory trial.
- type: item — value: FDA reports that the most common side effects in clinical trials were mild-to-moderate injection-site reactions. Serious reactions, including allergic reactions that in some cases needed emergency care, have also been reported. See the FDA prescribing information for the approved product. This page does not restate label dosing or administration.

### subsection 2 — title: Naming: SS-31 = elamipretide
**entries:**
- type: content — value: “SS-31” is a research code from the Szeto–Schiller (SS) peptide series. It refers to the same molecule as the nonproprietary name elamipretide. Other aliases in the literature include MTP-131 and Bendavia. Peer-reviewed papers routinely use “elamipretide (SS-31).”
- type: item — value: Seeing “SS-31” on a research-chemical listing does not mean the item is a different or newer compound. It also does not mean the item is the approved Forzinity product.

## Field: drugStatus

**FDA accelerated approval (19 September 2025):** Forzinity (elamipretide) injection, by Stealth Biotherapeutics Inc., is the first FDA-approved treatment for **Barth syndrome**, in patients weighing **at least 30 kg**. FDA describes Forzinity as a **mitochondrial cardiolipin binder, indicated to improve muscle strength** in adults and children with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg. The approval is accelerated, not traditional. Continued approval depends on a confirmatory trial. Research-use-only (RUO) listings sold as “elamipretide” or “SS-31” are **not** Forzinity and are not FDA-approved.

## Field: regulatoryImportantNote

On 19 September 2025, FDA granted Forzinity (elamipretide) injection **accelerated approval**, not traditional approval, for Barth syndrome in patients weighing at least 30 kg. Accelerated approval relies on a measure FDA considers *reasonably likely to predict* patient benefit that does not directly measure that benefit. For Forzinity, that measure is the strength of the knee-extensor muscle (the muscle that straightens the leg at the knee). As a condition of approval, FDA requires the manufacturer to run a post-approval randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to confirm that the strength changes translate into patient benefit. Continued approval may depend on that confirmatory trial.

This approval covers **one product, one indication, and one weight threshold**. It does **not** make research-chemical listings of “elamipretide” or “SS-31” approved, compounded, or quality-equivalent to the approved drug. Those listings are not made under the Forzinity application, and an FDA approval says nothing about their identity, purity, sterility, or lawful sale for human use. Peptidemap compares publisher and vendor listings only. It is not a clinic, pharmacy, or supplier, and it does not sell or dispense Forzinity.

## FAQs

**Is elamipretide FDA-approved?** Forzinity (elamipretide) has accelerated approval for Barth syndrome in patients ≥30 kg. That does not approve research-chemical vials or wellness uses.

**Is accelerated approval “full” approval?** It allows earlier access based on an intermediate endpoint reasonably likely to predict benefit; confirmatory trials are required.

**Are Peptidemap vendor vials Forzinity?** No. Catalog research products are not the approved NDA product.

**Is SS-31 the same thing as elamipretide?** Yes. SS-31 is a research code from the Szeto–Schiller peptide series for the molecule whose nonproprietary name is elamipretide. It is an alias, not a second molecule. MTP-131 and Bendavia are other names used in the literature.

**Does the Forzinity approval make research “elamipretide” or “SS-31” listings approved?** No. FDA’s accelerated approval covers Forzinity (elamipretide) injection for Barth syndrome in patients weighing at least 30 kg. It does not make research-chemical listings approved, lawful to compound, or equal in quality to the approved product.

**Does SS-31 need to come before MOTS-c?** The literature does not establish that. There is no validated stacking or sequencing protocol for SS-31 (elamipretide) and MOTS-c. MOTS-c is a research peptide that is not FDA-approved. The only FDA approval involving elamipretide is Forzinity for Barth syndrome in patients weighing at least 30 kg. Peptidemap is a publisher comparison resource, not medical guidance, and it does not give dosing, sequencing, or combination advice.

**Why is Forzinity’s approval called “accelerated”?** FDA based it on knee-extensor muscle strength, an intermediate measure considered reasonably likely to predict benefit, rather than a directly measured clinical outcome. FDA requires a confirmatory trial, and continued approval may depend on it.
