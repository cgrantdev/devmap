## Paste-ready body

## Field: overviewShort

**Dihexa** (also styled PNB-0408 / N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6)-aminohexanoic amide) is a metabolically stabilized **angiotensin IV (AngIV) analog** designed for blood–brain-barrier permeability in preclinical cognition and synaptogenesis research. It is **not FDA-approved**. **No published human efficacy trials** were identified. Educational only—not medical advice.

## Field: overview

Dihexa emerged from Washington State University / Harding–Wright medicinal-chemistry programs aiming to convert short-lived AngIV fragments into orally competent, CNS-accessible candidates. Marketplace “nootropic” framing far outruns the evidence: the public record is dominated by rodent models, and some highly cited early mechanism papers carry journal notices of concern or retractions. Peptidemap lists research-chemical products under this slug—those materials are not approved drugs.

## Field: background

AngIV (VYIHPF) and related analogs showed procognitive signals in older pharmacology work but suffered metabolic instability and poor barrier penetration. Structure–activity work truncated the pharmacophore and added N-/C-terminal lipophilic modifications, yielding dihexa. Related clinical-stage chemistry in the broader program (e.g., fosgonimeton / Athira) is a **different development molecule**—do not equate dihexa research-chemical vials with that clinical path.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Proposed mechanisms in the literature include HGF/c-Met pathway modulation and PI3K/AKT signaling in neurodegeneration models. Treat mechanism claims as preclinical hypotheses, especially where primary papers have integrity notices.
