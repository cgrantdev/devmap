### overviewShort

**GLOW** is a **marketplace blend name**, not a single molecule or CAS-registered substance. On Peptidemap and across multiple vendors, GLOW listings typically denote a co-lyophilized trio of **GHK-Cu**, **BPC-157**, and **TB-500** (often labeled around a 50 mg / 10 mg / 10 mg pattern totaling ~70 mg—**vendor-defined, not a pharmacopeial standard**). There is **no published human trial of the GLOW combination** as a finished product. Educational only.

### overview

Live Peptidemap product examples explicitly name the trio—e.g., “GLOW – GHK-Cu/BPC-157/TB-500 BLEND” and slugs encoding `ghk-cu-50mg` / `bpc-157-10mg` / `tb-500-10mg`. Other SKUs only say “GLOW (70mg)” or “GLOW (80mg)” without on-card stoichiometry, so **composition must be confirmed per lot/COA**.

GLOW marketing borrows separate literatures: copper-tripeptide skin/matrix biology (GHK-Cu), gastric pentadecapeptide preclinical repair themes (BPC-157), and TB-500 (Ac-LKKTETQ, the 7-residue thymosin β4 fragment, residues 17–23 — not full-length thymosin β4). **Component papers do not validate the three-way blend.**

### background

“GLOW” is a commercial nickname that spread across research-peptide catalogs (often overlapping “skin + recovery” storytelling). It is distinct from **KLOW**, which adds **KPV** as a fourth component on Peptidemap’s blend taxonomy. Because the name is unregulated, researchers should treat GLOW as a **label class** and verify each vendor’s bill of materials.

**intro:** Synthetic 15-aa peptide; preclinical angiogenesis/cytoprotection themes.
**items:**
- item: Pleiotropic preclinical repair signaling — description: VEGF/NO/anti-inflammatory themes in animal/cell literature

### subsection 3 — title: TB-500
**intro:** Usually a thymosin β4 fragment (not full-length Tβ4).
**items:**
- item: Actin / migration hypotheses — description: Derived from parent Tβ4 actin biology
- item: Identity caveat — description: Fragment ≠ ophthalmic full-length Tβ4 programs

### regulatoryImportantNote

**Regulatory literacy:** Removal from FDA Category 2 (or a favorable Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee vote) is **not** placement on the final 503A Bulks List and does **not** by itself authorize compounding—verify current FDA compounding pages. Components (especially BPC-157 and TB-500 fragment) retain separate FDA compounding and WADA issues—verify live lists.
