# CBL-514

## Field: overviewShort

**CBL-514** is an investigational, **non-peptide**, small-molecule lipolytic injection developed by **Caliway Biopharmaceuticals**. It is being studied as a local subcutaneous injection for abdominal subcutaneous fat. It has no FDA or EU marketing approval in the regulator records checked on 23 November 2026, and its developer says it is not approved in any country. This page is an educational overview — not medical advice and not a use instruction.

## Field: overview

CBL-514 sometimes appears in discussions of research peptides, which can make it look like a peptide. It is not. Peer-reviewed trial reports describe it as a **small-molecule** injectable lipolysis agent. The public protocol for Caliway’s pharmacokinetic study **CBL-0203** (ClinicalTrials.gov **NCT05234736**, protocol version 1.0 dated 18 October 2021) names the active pharmaceutical ingredients as **curcumin** and **trans-resveratrol**. Those are small-molecule polyphenols, not amino-acid chains.

The same public protocol redacts the quantitative formula (concentration and the amount of each component). A development code is not a complete public specification of a drug substance. Findings from dietary curcumin or resveratrol, or from a research vial sold under the same name, must not be treated as results for the investigational trial product.

## Field: background

Caliway Biopharmaceuticals Co., Ltd. (New Taipei City, Taiwan) is the named developer and the lead sponsor on the ClinicalTrials.gov records reviewed for this page. The published programme so far has been aimed at **local reduction of abdominal subcutaneous fat**, measured as fat volume or thickness and as clinician- and patient-reported visual scales — not as change in body weight.

In a company-reported statement dated 30 October 2026, Caliway said CBL-514 remains an investigational candidate, has not received marketing approval in any country or jurisdiction, and is not commercially available. That is the company’s statement, not a finding by this publisher. Orphan-drug or Fast Track designations, where announced by the company, are not marketing approvals.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Published abstracts describe CBL-514 as an injectable lipolysis agent studied for **adipocyte apoptosis** (programmed death of fat cells) in the treated subcutaneous area. That is the authors’ and sponsor’s stated rationale. This page does not treat a pathway story as a proven clinical mechanism, and it does not give use instructions.

## Field: humanUseSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: What the trials measure
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Across the papers and registry records cited here, primary or key endpoints are local abdominal subcutaneous fat volume or thickness (ultrasound or MRI) and change on clinician- or patient-reported abdominal fat rating scales (CR-AFRS and PR-AFRS). They are not whole-body weight-loss percentages.
- type: content — value: The two pivotal Phase 3 records, SUPREME-01 (NCT07140939) and SUPREME-02 (NCT07526974), use a primary endpoint of the percentage of participants with at least a 2-grade improvement on both PR-AFRS and CR-AFRS, with MRI-measured percent change in abdominal fat as a secondary endpoint. The time frame given in both records is from baseline to 4 weeks after final treatment.

### subsection 2 — title: Peer-reviewed trial reports
**entries:**
- type: content — value: **Phase IIa (peer-reviewed; developer-affiliated; Goodman et al., Aesthet Surg J 2022, PMID 35709509; NCT04698642, named in the paper’s methods).** An open-label, random-allocation study of CBL-514 injected into abdominal subcutaneous fat, with ultrasound follow-up. The abstract reported statistically significant reductions from baseline in abdominal fat volume and thickness in the middle and highest of the three groups studied, and a 24.96% reduction in abdominal fat volume from baseline in the highest group. The study was not placebo-controlled. The full text states Caliway funded the study; two coauthors are Caliway employees. The abstract’s “safe / promising treatment” wording is the authors’ conclusion and is not adopted as a publisher claim here.
- type: content — value: **Phase II (peer-reviewed; developer-affiliated; Gold et al., Aesthet Surg J 2025, PMID 40037659; NCT04897412, named in the paper).** A single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial. Seventy-six participants were randomized 2:1 (75 were treated, which matches the registry’s actual enrollment of 75). The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants with a subcutaneous fat volume loss of at least 150 mL from baseline, measured by ultrasound, compared with placebo. In the intention-to-treat population, at 8 weeks after the final treatment, 69.6% of CBL-514-treated participants had lost at least 150 mL, versus none in the placebo group (P < .001); 60.9% also reached a 200 mL threshold. The authors reported that the most common treatment-emergent adverse events were injection-site reactions of mild-to-moderate severity. Developer-affiliated: the full text states Caliway funded the study, and three coauthors are Caliway employees.
- type: content — value: **Phase 2b (peer-reviewed; Lorenc et al., Aesthet Surg J 2026, PMID 42159040; NCT05736107, CBL-0204).** The abstract describes a randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2b trial in 108 adults with moderate or severe abdominal subcutaneous fat. The ClinicalTrials.gov record lists actual enrollment of 107 and masking as double (participant and outcomes assessor); that masking description differs from the abstract’s “single-blind” wording and is noted rather than reconciled here. In the full analysis set, 76.7% (23/30) of CBL-514-treated participants had at least a 1-grade improvement on CR-AFRS versus 18.9% (7/37) with placebo (P<0.0001); PR-AFRS results were 76.7% (23/30) versus 19.4% (7/36). MRI showed a least-squares mean abdominal fat volume change of −152.9 mL ± 17.75 (least-squares mean percent change −20.3%), both reported as greater than placebo (P<0.0001). Developer-affiliated: four coauthors are listed with Caliway. The abstract reported mainly mild, transient injection-site reactions.

### subsection 3 — title: Company-reported CBL-0205 (not peer-reviewed)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: **CBL-0205 (Caliway release, 9 February 2025; NCT06005441).** Company-reported, not peer-reviewed. Caliway announced results from a second Phase 2b study at 15 sites in the United States and Canada, enrolling 173 participants with moderate or severe abdominal fat on the Abdominal Fat Rating Scale. The company reported that the study met its primary and listed critical secondary efficacy endpoints.
- type: content — value: **Company-reported MRI and scale figures (4 weeks after the final treatment).** Caliway reported that 54.2% of participants in the CBL-514 group achieved at least a 20% reduction in fat volume, versus 0% with placebo; a mean subcutaneous fat volume change of 166.6 mL reduction versus a 15.7 mL increase with placebo; a mean 22.4% fat-volume reduction versus a 1.8% increase with placebo; and at least a 1-grade CR-AFRS improvement in 75.7% versus 27.5% with placebo, and a 1-grade PR-AFRS improvement in 73.0% versus 20.0%. The release also reported comparisons with two additional arms named CBL-A1 and CBL-A2. The public registry and the release do not define the composition of those arms, so those comparator figures are not restated here.
- type: content — value: **Company-reported safety language.** Caliway reported no drug-related serious adverse events and said most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild-to-moderate injection-site reactions. That is a sponsor summary, not a peer-reviewed safety analysis.

## FAQs

**What is CBL-514?** CBL-514 is an investigational, non-peptide small-molecule lipolytic injection developed by Caliway Biopharmaceuticals. It is being studied as a local subcutaneous injection for abdominal subcutaneous fat, not as an approved drug and not as a body-weight medicine.

**Is CBL-514 a peptide?** No. Peer-reviewed trial reports describe it as a small-molecule injectable. The public CBL-0203 protocol (NCT05234736) names the active ingredients as curcumin and trans-resveratrol, which are small-molecule polyphenols, not peptides.

**Is CBL-514 approved?** No marketing approval was found in FDA (Drugs@FDA) or EU (EMA) records checked on 23 November 2026. An EU orphan designation for Dercum disease is not a marketing approval. In a company-reported statement dated 30 October 2026, Caliway said CBL-514 has not received marketing approval in any country or jurisdiction. Two Phase 3 trials, SUPREME-01 and SUPREME-02, were listed as recruiting on 23 November 2026.

**Do the CBL-514 trials measure body weight?** No. The cited papers and Phase 3 registry records measure local abdominal fat volume, thickness, or clinician- and patient-reported visual fat-rating scales, not whole-body weight-loss percentages.

**Can a research vial be confirmed as the same product used in the trials?** No. The public CBL-0203 protocol redacts the quantitative formula, so a laboratory cannot confirm from public information that a vial matches the investigational trial product. A certificate of analysis can show what was detected in a tested vial; it cannot, by itself, establish that the vial equals CBL-514.
