## Paste-ready body

## Field: overviewShort

**Orexin-A** (also **hypocretin-1**) is a **33-amino-acid hypothalamic neuropeptide** that activates **orexin-1 (OX1R)** and **orexin-2 (OX2R)** GPCRs. It is central to wake/arousal and feeding-regulation biology. Synthetic research peptide is **not** an FDA-approved narcolepsy or insomnia medicine (approved orexin drugs are typically **small-molecule antagonists**, e.g., suvorexant class—different modality). Educational only—not medical advice.

## Field: overview

Orexin-A and orexin-B are cleaved from prepro-orexin. Orexin-A contains an N-terminal pyroglutamate, C-terminal amidation, and two disulfide bonds (Cys6–Cys12, Cys7–Cys14). Loss of orexin neurons underlies narcolepsy type 1 pathophysiology—motivating replacement and agonist strategies that remain largely investigational relative to marketed dual orexin receptor antagonists (DORAs) for insomnia.

## Field: background

Discovered independently as orexins (Sakurai et al., 1998) and hypocretins (de Lecea et al.). Decades of neuroscience link the system to sleep–wake stability, reward, and autonomic themes. Peptide replacement faces delivery/BBB challenges; most approved clinical orexin pharmacology to date is antagonism, not agonist peptide vials.
