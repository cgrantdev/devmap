# Pemvidutide

## Field: overviewShort

**Pemvidutide** (development code **ALT-801**) is an investigational peptide **GLP-1 / glucagon dual receptor agonist** developed by Altimmune. It is in a Phase 3 trial (PERFORMA, NCT07795164) for **MASH** (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) and was earlier studied in obesity. It is **not approved** by the FDA or any other regulator found in the sources checked. Educational only. Not medical advice.

## Field: overview

Pemvidutide activates two receptors: the glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor and the glucagon receptor. Altimmune describes the activity as "balanced 1:1". The sponsor's rationale is that glucagon-receptor signalling acts directly on the liver, while GLP-1-receptor signalling drives appetite and weight effects. Development started with Phase 1 studies in people with overweight, obesity, or fatty liver disease. It then moved to a 48-week obesity trial (MOMENTUM) and a biopsy-based Phase 2b MASH trial (IMPACT). In August 2026 Altimmune said it had begun enrolling PERFORMA, its registrational Phase 3 MASH trial. The company also runs Phase 2 programs in alcohol use disorder (RECLAIM) and alcohol-associated liver disease (RESTORE). The FDA has granted Fast Track designation for MASH and for alcohol use disorder, and Breakthrough Therapy designation for MASH. These designations speed up development and review steps. **They are not approvals.** Peptidemap is a research-use comparison publisher, not a clinic, pharmacy, or supplier. This page makes no supply, availability, vendor, or price claims about pemvidutide.

## Field: background

Pemvidutide was developed by Altimmune, Inc. (Gaithersburg, Maryland). The FDA Global Substance Registration System (GSRS) record lists the INN/USAN name **pemvidutide**, UNII **A35F525WBG**, CAS Registry Number **2538014-94-5**, and the synonyms MD-1373 and SP-1373. The INN chemical name describes a 29-residue peptide amide. It contains 2-methylalanine (Aib) at position 2, a side-chain lactam bridge between positions 16 and 20, and a lysine side chain carrying a glucuronide-linked C18 fatty diacid. Altimmune calls that lipidated motif its **EuPort** domain and says it extends serum half-life to allow once-weekly administration in trials. A naming caution: **"ALT-801" has also been used for an unrelated Altor BioScience fusion-protein immunotherapy** in older oncology trials (for example NCT01326871). Those records are a different molecule and not pemvidutide. Disease naming also changed during development: earlier records say NAFLD/NASH, and current usage is MASLD/MASH.

## Field: mechanismOfActionIntro

Pemvidutide is a single peptide that agonizes two class B G-protein-coupled receptors. It belongs to the same broad "glucagon co-agonist" family as survodutide and mazdutide (GLP-1/glucagon). It differs from GLP-1-only drugs such as semaglutide, from GIP/GLP-1 agonists such as tirzepatide, and from GIP/GLP-1/glucagon triple agonists such as retatrutide.

## Field: mechanismSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Dual receptor pharmacology (as described by the sponsor and trial authors)
**items:**
- item: GLP-1 receptor — description: Appetite and satiety signalling familiar from the GLP-1 receptor agonist class. The sponsor links it to weight loss and, in its alcohol use disorder program, to craving and reward pathways.
- item: Glucagon receptor — description: Acts on the liver. Trial authors describe stimulation of hepatic fatty-acid oxidation and inhibition of lipogenesis (J Hepatol 2025; JHEP Rep 2025). This is the main rationale for a liver-fat and MASH focus.
- item: "Balanced 1:1" activity — description: This is Altimmune's own description of relative receptor activity. Receptor-potency ratios differ between glucagon co-agonists, and sponsors describe them in non-standardized ways.
- item: Weekly-interval design — description: The sponsor attributes its once-weekly trial schedule to the lipidated EuPort domain. That is a design feature, not a use instruction.

### subsection 2 — title: Liver effect versus weight effect
**items:**
- item: Open question — description: A common reader question is whether liver-fat reduction is independent of weight loss. Trial authors propose a direct hepatic glucagon-receptor contribution. The Phase 1b MASLD study reported large relative liver-fat reductions at 12 weeks alongside modest weight change. Published trials were not designed to fully separate the two effects.

## Field: preclinicalIntro

The main peer-reviewed preclinical report is a sponsor-affiliated study in a biopsy-confirmed diet-induced obese mouse model of NASH (Nestor et al., *Sci Rep* 2022).

## Field: preclinicalSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: DIO-NASH mouse model (Sci Rep 2022; PMID 35461369)
**findings:**
- title: Design — content: Mice with biopsy-confirmed steatosis and fibrosis were given ALT-801, semaglutide, elafibranor, or vehicle for 12 weeks while remaining on a NASH-inducing diet.
- title: Reported findings — content: The authors report approximately 25% body-weight reduction with ALT-801, reductions in plasma aminotransferases and liver lipids, and lower markers of inflammation (galectin-3) and fibrosis (collagen type 1 alpha 1) compared with vehicle.
- title: Interpretation limits — content: This is a single sponsor-affiliated mouse study. Mouse NASH models do not predict human histology outcomes reliably, and the comparisons with semaglutide and elafibranor were made in mice, not people.

## Field: humanUseSubsections

### subsection 1 — title: Phase 1 program (2020–2023)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: First-in-human single- and multiple-ascending-dose study in healthy volunteers with overweight or obesity: NCT04561245 (start 10 Nov 2020; completed 29 Oct 2021; 100 enrolled, per ClinicalTrials.gov). Other Phase 1 registrations include a drug-interaction study (NCT04972396) and a study in adults with overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes (NCT05134662; completed 9 Mar 2023, per ClinicalTrials.gov).
- type: content — value: **Phase 1b MASLD (peer-reviewed):** NCT05006885. Harrison et al., *J Hepatol* 2025 (PMID 39002641). 94 randomized participants with liver fat content of at least 10% received one of three pemvidutide dose levels or placebo for 12 weeks. Relative liver-fat reductions were 46.6%, 68.5%, and 57.1% in the three dose arms (lowest to highest), compared with 4.4% for placebo (p<0.001 for each arm versus placebo). Maximum mean weight change was −4.3%.
- type: content — value: **12-week extension (peer-reviewed):** NCT05292911. Browne et al., *JHEP Rep* 2025 (PMID 41113119). 64 participants continued on their assigned arm for 24 weeks in total. Relative liver-fat reductions were 56.3%, 75.2%, and 76.4% (lowest to highest dose arm), compared with 14.0% for placebo.

### subsection 2 — title: MOMENTUM, Phase 2 obesity trial (topline only)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: NCT05295875. 48 weeks, 391 adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related comorbidity, without diabetes (ClinicalTrials.gov; Altimmune topline release, 30 Nov 2023).
- type: content — value: **Topline, not peer-reviewed in sources located:** mean body-weight change at week 48 was −10.3%, −11.2%, and −15.6% in the three dose arms (lowest to highest), compared with −2.2% for placebo. These are MMRM least-squares means as reported by the company. The release does not name an estimand. Adverse events leading to discontinuation were 6.2% for placebo and 5.1%, 19.2%, and 19.6% across the three dose arms. Nausea and vomiting were the most common adverse events.
- type: item — value: No peer-reviewed MOMENTUM primary paper was found in PubMed or Europe PMC on 8 Oct 2026. Treat these figures as sponsor topline.

### subsection 3 — title: IMPACT, Phase 2b MASH trial (peer-reviewed at 24 weeks; topline at 48 weeks)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: NCT05989711. 212 adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH and fibrosis stage F2–F3 were randomized to one of two pemvidutide dose levels or placebo, given without dose titration, across 83 sites in the USA and Australia. The dual primary endpoints were assessed at 24 weeks in the intention-to-treat population.
- type: content — value: **24 weeks (peer-reviewed; Noureddin et al., *Lancet* 2025, PMID 41237796):** MASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis occurred in 58% (lower-dose arm, 24/41) and 52% (higher-dose arm, 45/85) of participants, compared with 20% for placebo (18/86) (p<0.0001 for each). Fibrosis improvement of at least one stage without worsening of MASH occurred in 33% and 36%, compared with 28% for placebo. That difference was **not statistically significant** (p=0.59 and p=0.27). The authors state the trial met one primary endpoint but not the fibrosis endpoint at 24 weeks.
- type: content — value: **48 weeks (company topline, 19 Dec 2025; not peer-reviewed in sources located):** the company reported statistically significant reductions versus placebo in non-invasive fibrosis markers. Mean change in Enhanced Liver Fibrosis (ELF) score was −0.49 and −0.58 versus +0.16 for placebo. Mean change in liver stiffness measurement (LSM) was −3.04 and −3.97 versus −0.03 for placebo. Mean weight loss was 4.5% and 7.5% versus 0.2% for placebo. The release does not name an estimand. Non-invasive tests are not liver biopsy histology.

### subsection 4 — title: PERFORMA, Phase 3 MASH trial (enrolling; no results)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: NCT07795164 (sponsor protocol ALT-801-301). Phase 3, multinational, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in non-cirrhotic (F2–F3) MASH. ClinicalTrials.gov lists an actual start date of 30 Jul 2026, status recruiting, estimated enrollment of 1,800, estimated primary completion December 2028, and estimated study completion December 2032 (record first posted 31 Aug 2026; last updated 23 Sep 2026).
- type: content — value: Altimmune announced on 3 Aug 2026 that it had begun enrolling. The company describes two cohorts. Cohort 1 (about 990 biopsy-confirmed participants) is designed to support an accelerated-approval pathway based on 52-week histology endpoints. Cohort 2 (about 800 participants identified by non-invasive tests) adds to the safety dataset. Final regulatory evaluation is to be based on liver-related clinical outcomes at about 60 months. The company anticipates the 52-week readout in 2029. The trial will use the FDA-qualified AIM-MASH AI Assist pathology tool.
- type: content — value: Registered primary outcomes include time to first clinical outcome event (progression to cirrhosis, liver-related events, transplantation, or death from any cause, through about month 60). They also include two 52-week histology endpoints: MASH resolution without fibrosis worsening, and fibrosis improvement of at least one stage without MASH worsening.
- type: item — value: An anticipated readout or an accelerated-approval design is **not** a prediction of approval. This page does not forecast regulatory outcomes.

### subsection 5 — title: Other indications (Phase 2)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: **RECLAIM** (alcohol use disorder with obesity or overweight; NCT06987513): in a topline release on 28 Jul 2026, the company reported that the trial met its primary endpoint, a reduction in heavy drinking days versus placebo. Not peer-reviewed in sources located.
- type: content — value: **RESTORE** (alcohol-associated liver disease; NCT07009860): ClinicalTrials.gov lists it as active, not recruiting. No results located.

- type: content — value: Across the published and topline reports, gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation) were the most common. Most were reported as mild to moderate. Long-term safety and outcome data do not exist yet. Establishing them is part of the purpose of PERFORMA.

## Field: conclusion

Pemvidutide is a late-stage investigational glucagon co-agonist focused on liver disease. It has peer-reviewed Phase 1b and Phase 2b evidence, more data available only as topline releases, and a Phase 3 trial that has just started. Its record so far shows a MASH-resolution signal and liver-fat reductions, a fibrosis endpoint that was not met at 24 weeks, and no long-term outcome data. It is not approved for any use. ## FAQs

**Is pemvidutide approved?** No. As of 8 Oct 2026 pemvidutide is investigational: in Phase 3 (PERFORMA) for MASH and in Phase 2 for alcohol use disorder and alcohol-associated liver disease. FDA Fast Track and Breakthrough Therapy designations are not approvals.

**What is the PERFORMA trial?** PERFORMA (NCT07795164) is Altimmune's registrational Phase 3, placebo-controlled trial in non-cirrhotic F2–F3 MASH. Altimmune announced enrollment had begun on 3 Aug 2026, and ClinicalTrials.gov lists a start date of 30 Jul 2026. It combines 52-week liver-biopsy endpoints with clinical outcomes at about 60 months. The company anticipates the 52-week readout in 2029.

**How does pemvidutide differ from survodutide, mazdutide, and retatrutide?** Pemvidutide, survodutide, and mazdutide are all GLP-1/glucagon dual agonists, but they are different molecules from different sponsors with different development and regulatory status. Mazdutide is approved in China; pemvidutide and survodutide are investigational. Retatrutide is a GIP/GLP-1/glucagon triple agonist and is also investigational. Their trials enrolled different populations with different durations and endpoints, so cross-trial numbers do not show that one is better than another.

**Is pemvidutide the same as the "ALT-801" in older cancer trials?** No. Altor BioScience used the code ALT-801 for an unrelated fusion-protein immunotherapy in oncology trials. Pemvidutide's ALT-801 is Altimmune's GLP-1/glucagon peptide.

**Are the liver effects just a result of weight loss?** This is not settled. Trial authors propose a direct liver effect through the glucagon receptor and report large liver-fat reductions alongside modest weight change in early studies. Published trials were not designed to fully separate the two effects.
