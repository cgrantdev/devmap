### overviewShort

CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin is a widely sold **growth-hormone (GH) axis research blend** pairing a GHRH-pathway analog marketed as CJC-1295 with the selective ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist ipamorelin. Complementary receptor pharmacology is the usual rationale. **No published human randomized trial of this specific marketed combination** was identified. Educational only—not medical advice.

### overview

Peptidemap lists **CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin** as a multi-vendor blend category. Product titles often specify “No DAC” (Modified GRF 1-29–type constructs) versus long-acting **CJC-1295 with DAC** (Drug Affinity Complex)—these are **not interchangeable** identities. Ipamorelin is a selective GH secretagogue designed for GH release with relatively less cortisol/prolactin co-secretion than older GHRPs in characterization studies.

Marketplace stacking assumes synergy because GHRH and ghrelin receptors both drive pituitary GH release. Synergy has been studied for **related** GHRH+GHRP pairings in humans historically, but that is not the same as an outcome RCT of modern CJC-1295+ipamorelin vials.

### background

The blend rose with anti-aging and recovery marketing of “GH optimization.” Live Peptidemap product names frequently include ratios such as 5 mg/5 mg and “No DAC,” underscoring naming variance. FDA compounding reviews and PCAC history for related secretagogues are active regulatory context; neither peptide is an FDA-approved obesity/GH-deficiency therapy analogous to branded GH or approved GHRH analogs like tesamorelin (different molecule/indication).

### humanUseIntro

Human data exist for **components** (notably CJC-1295 with DAC PK/PD; ipamorelin PK and a Phase 2 postoperative ileus efficacy trial that did not meet its primary endpoint). That is **not** blend validation.

### subsection 1 — title: CJC-1295 (component)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Teichman et al. (2006) reported prolonged GH and IGF-I stimulation after CJC-1295 in healthy adults (randomized ascending-exposure pharmacology). This is PK/PD evidence, not a body-composition outcome approval package.

### subsection 2 — title: Ipamorelin (component)
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Early human pharmacology characterized GH responses. Beck et al. (2014; PMID 25331030) ran a multicenter Phase 2 proof-of-concept trial of IV ipamorelin for postoperative ileus after bowel resection; the primary endpoint (time to first tolerated solid meal) did not reach statistical significance versus placebo, and development for that indication did not yield an approved therapy.
- type: item — value: FDA compounding safety-risk materials have discussed ipamorelin acetate regarding immunogenicity/impurity characterization and limited route-specific human safety information.

### subsection 3 — title: Combination
**entries:**
- type: content — value: Targeted evidence reviews report no published human RCT of CJC-1295 administered together with ipamorelin for therapeutic endpoints. Mechanistic synergy arguments are not clinical proof.

### keyPoints

- Marketplace blend of a GHRH-pathway analog (“CJC-1295,” DAC or no-DAC) plus ipamorelin.
- Dual-receptor rationale is pharmacologically coherent; **pair-specific human outcome RCTs were not identified**.
- Component human data ≠ blend approval or proven body-composition benefit.
- Labels must be read carefully—DAC vs no-DAC changes the molecule’s time course.
- Not an FDA-approved drug product; compounding status is pathway-specific and can change—verify FDA primaries.
- WADA prohibits relevant GH releasing factors / secretagogues under S2 (athletes).

### conclusion

CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin is one of the most visible GH-axis stacks in research-peptide commerce. Encyclopedia honesty requires separating (1) solid class pharmacology, (2) limited component human PK/efficacy history, and (3) the absence of pair-specific outcome RCTs. Educational only.

### regulatoryImportantNote

**Regulatory literacy:** Removal from FDA Category 2 (or a favorable Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee vote) is **not** placement on the final 503A Bulks List and does **not** by itself authorize compounding—verify current FDA compounding pages. FDA significant-safety-risk materials discuss CJC-1295 and ipamorelin acetate in compounding contexts (characterization/immunogenicity concerns; limited clinical safety information)—confirm the live FDA table.
